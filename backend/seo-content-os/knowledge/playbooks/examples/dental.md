# Dental Vertical: The Example Library (YMYL)

Real, live-verified teardowns for every one of the six page types, dental edition. Dentistry is **Your-Money-or-Your-Life**. Google's own guidance: "Of these aspects, trust is most important" (Creating Helpful Content). So the bar here is stricter than plumbing or HVAC, not looser. A dental page is judged on medical E-E-A-T: a named, credentialed, licensed responsible human (DDS or DMD), real clinical experience shown not claimed, honest outcomes with no overstatement, and proof handled inside HIPAA and FTC law.

Governing law: `knowledge/doctrine/seo-system-doctrine.md`, especially **Law 8** (optimize the reward function, not proxies: no detector-evasion, humanize with real facts). Read alongside `knowledge/doctrine/google-compliance-spine.md` and the six page-type playbooks.

**Verification legend, applied to every example below:**
- **[LIVE]** fetched and read this session (2026-07); quotes are from the live page.
- **[SEARCH]** confirmed via search-result snippet only; page itself not fetched. Treat the specific quote as directional.
- **[PATTERN]** a real, documented failure pattern described generically rather than pinned to one named victim (per CLAUDE.md: never fabricate a named victim network). Each is anchored to a cited source that documents the pattern or the enforcement action.

The dental reality that reorders everything: the four cross-cutting YMYL gates in Section 7 (medical authorship, HIPAA review handling, HIPAA before/after consent, FTC substantiation) sit on top of every page type. A dental page can pass the doorway test and still get the practice fined. Both risks are live at once.

## 1. City / Location page

The page that ranks and converts for "dentist in [city]" and anchors the practice as the local entity.

### GOOD

**G1 - Clock Tower Dental, New Hyde Park NY** [LIVE] `clocktower-dental.com/new-hyde-park-ny-dentist/`
Why it wins: names real local anchors that collapse the moment you swap the city (Memorial Park, Umberto's Pizzeria, Hillside Public Library, Northwell Health LIJ Medical Center, Eisenhower Park, Lakeville Road), lists four named credentialed providers with distinct roles (Michael F. Kelley DDS, general/cosmetic; Ramin Rayhan DDS, cosmetic/restorative; Richard Nejat DDS, periodontist; Jacqueline Simons DDS, orthodontist), a real staffed NAP (167 A Lakeville Rd, 516-352-1000), surrounding communities served (Garden City, Elmont, Floral Park, Hempstead), and named-attributed reviews (Anthony B., David B.). Passes the strip-the-city test.
Gate/law: passes the Minimum Unique Local Substance bar and the medical-authorship gate (four named DDS). Location-page playbook, uniqueness gate.

**G2 - Complete Dental Care of Fishers IN** [LIVE] `cdcfishers.com/dentist-in-fishers/`
Why it wins: deep, externally verifiable local substance a competitor cannot regenerate by swapping a token. Names Conner Prairie Living History Museum, Mudsock Fields, Flat Fork Creek Park, White River Greenway, Heritage Park, Hamilton Southeastern schools; carries genuine city history ("in 1802 William Conner established a trading post... Salathial Fisher platted Fisher's Switch in 1872"); the responsible dentist is named and credentialed (Dr. Afaq Kheiri, Indiana University School of Dentistry) with a real tenure ("over 30 years serving the community; Dr. Kheiri joined in 2017"). The named dental school and the public city-history facts are externally verifiable.
Gate/law: passes strip-the-city + external-verifiability + medical authorship. This is the "gumbo clay soil" move translated to dental: substitute the physical mechanism with named local institutions, schools, and a verifiable dental-school credential.

### BAD

**B1 - The templated DSO location network** [PATTERN] documented at groupdentistrynow.com/dso-group-blog/google-spam/ and ricketyroo.com/blog/location-page-spam/
A dental group with dozens or hundreds of location pages where "every city page says the same three sentences with only the city name swapped out" (Group Dentistry Now's exact framing). Why it fails: fails the strip-the-city test simultaneously across the whole cohort; SpamBrain reads the near-duplicate set as scaled content abuse. Large national dental brands run location directories at this scale (fetch-blocked to this tool, so labeled PATTERN); the structure is a doorway hazard for a single-office copy that inherits none of the brand's link authority.
Gate/law: FAILS doorway + scaled-content-abuse (developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies). Uniqueness gate.

**B2 - "We proudly serve [City] and surrounding areas"** [PATTERN] the canonical thin city page, named as a spam tell by ricketyroo.com/blog/location-page-spam/
A "dentist in [City]" page with zero named streets, landmarks, schools, or water/fluoride facts, no named dentist, and a stock hero. Why it fails: the entire body reads correctly for any town in the country; nothing external is verifiable; on a YMYL page the missing named-and-licensed dentist also fails the trust gate.
Gate/law: FAILS strip-the-city + external-verifiability + medical-authorship.

## 2. Service page (single service, brand-wide)

Ranks for "dental implants," "invisalign," "root canal" across the whole practice, then routes to the service-in-city children.

### GOOD

**G1 - Eggleston Dental Care, Invisalign cost guide** [LIVE] `egglestondentalcare.com/cost-of-invisalign-2024-comprehensive-guide/`
Why it wins: real numbers, not adjectives ("the average cost of Invisalign treatment is around $5,000... not covered by most insurance plans"), honest financing framing ("most providers offer 0% financing... many orthodontists offer in-house financing plans with low down payments"), and a named responsible owner-dentist (Dr. Jim Eggleston, DDS) with an experience signal ("Dr. Eggleston and his staff participate in continual education each year"). Real NAP (2053 Geer Rd, Turlock CA 95382).
Gate/law: passes no-fabricated-facts + medical authorship. YMYL caveat below (B-side).

**G2 - Sloan's Lake Dental, implant financing** [LIVE] `sloanslakedental.com/affording-implants-your-guide-to-dental-implant-financing-in-denver-co/`
Why it wins: honest, specific price bands a reader can verify in their head (single implant "$2,000-$4,000"; implant + crown "$3,000-$6,000"; full-arch "$20,000-$50,000 per arch"; bone grafting "$300-$3,000"); financing partners named (CareCredit, LendingClub, in-house plans); insurance truth stated plainly ("most dental insurance caps implant coverage or excludes implants entirely"); named dentist with a relevant credential claim (Dr. Sage Pollack, DMD, "advanced implant credentials," iTero scanning, in-house lab). Dated (Nov 22, 2025).
Gate/law: passes no-fabricated-facts + the Reg Z financing-honesty note (names financing without dangling a triggering-term teaser that lacks disclosure).

### BAD

**B1 - The un-reviewed YMYL clinical service page** [PATTERN] and note this is the standing weakness in BOTH good examples above
Both Eggleston and Sloan's Lake carry clinical cost-and-treatment content with **no article author byline and no "Medically reviewed by Dr. X, DDS" line** (verified live: Sloan's Lake is "attributed to the organization, no individual author byline"; Eggleston shows "no individual article author"). Why it is a miss on YMYL: Google's medical-content guidance wants clinical claims tied to a named, credentialed reviewer. The contrast case done right: **Dental Made Easy** publishes clinical blog content "medically reviewed by Dr. Rafael Boruchov, DDS" with a review date [SEARCH] (dentalmadeeasy.com/blog). A service page that discusses implant surgery risk or Invisalign outcomes without a named DDS reviewer is a YMYL trust gap, not a doorway problem.
Gate/law: FAILS (partially) the medical-authorship / "reviewed by" gate. The fix is one line, not a rewrite.

**B2 - Overstated clinical outcomes** [PATTERN] FTC substantiation + state dental-board advertising rules
A service page promising "painless root canals," "permanent results guaranteed," "100% success rate on implants," or "whitening that lasts forever." Why it fails: YMYL medical claims must be substantiated and must not overstate outcomes; most state dental boards prohibit guarantees of outcome and unverifiable superlatives in dental advertising; the FTC requires substantiation for objective claims. Implant osseointegration failure and endodontic complication rates are real; a guarantee is both a compliance violation and a trust destroyer.
Gate/law: FAILS FTC substantiation + no-overstated-outcomes (YMYL). Google-compliance-spine.

## 3. Service-in-city page (the money page: one service, one city)

"Emergency dentist Lenexa," "dental implants Denver," "invisalign Scottsdale." Highest intent, highest doorway risk.

### GOOD

**G1 - Rosehill Dentistry, emergency dentist Lenexa KS** [LIVE] `rosehilldentalkc.com/services/emergency-dentist-lenexa-ks/`
Why it wins for URGENT intent: real staffed NAP with a matching local area code (8615 Rosehill Rd Suite 204, Lenexa KS 66215; 913-888-2474), an operationally honest same-day promise scoped to real hours ("same-day emergency dental appointments... Monday through Thursday 8AM-5PM," Friday "by appointment," plus an explicit after-hours fallback "we will get you in as soon as the next business day"), deep service detail (abscesses, extractions, trauma), a named provider (Dr. Rickabaugh; caption "Dr. Austin doing an emergency exam"), and a genuine community signal ("HABLAMOS ESPANOL"). The honest, scoped availability is the point: it does not promise a 24/7 line the office cannot answer.
Gate/law: passes the operational-truth gate on the response promise. Watch-item below.

**G2 - Sloan's Lake Dental, implant financing IN Denver** [LIVE] `sloanslakedental.com/affording-implants-your-guide-to-dental-implant-financing-in-denver-co/`
Why it wins as a CONSIDERED combo page: it fuses the service (implant financing) with the city (Denver) using a real local mechanism ("references local demand, clinic overhead, and regional cost of living affecting Denver pricing") rather than a swapped token, plus verifiable price bands and a named DMD. The Denver-cost-of-living causal tie is the considered/YMYL analogue of the "gumbo clay soil" local mechanism.
Gate/law: passes Minimum Unique Local Substance (named city mechanism + verifiable prices + named dentist).

### BAD

**B1 - The AI-spun combo network** [PATTERN] the exact scaled-content-abuse example in the live policy (developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
160 "invisalign in [city]" or "dental implants in [city]" pages generated in one pass, each convincingly specific but none tied to a booked local case, none externally verifiable, none with a named local dentist who actually treats that town. Why it fails: it is "using generative AI tools to generate many pages without adding value" verbatim; it fails the external-verifiability gate even when it passes a naive strip-the-city read.
Gate/law: FAILS scaled-content-abuse + external-verifiability. Service-in-city playbook Section 5.

**B2 - The "24/7 emergency dentist" that hits voicemail** [PATTERN] operational gate, emergency-dental SEO sources (revitadigital.com, blog.frizerly.com)
An emergency service-in-city page promising "24-hour" or "always available" when after-hours calls go to voicemail. Why it fails: the promise converts the click and burns the panicked lead; on a YMYL health-urgency page a broken availability claim is both a conversion leak and a trust violation. Contrast Rosehill (G1), which scopes the promise to true hours and states the honest fallback.
Gate/law: FAILS the operational-truth gate on the response promise.

**G2 - Grayson Heritage Family Dentistry** [LIVE] entity verified via `graysonheritagedental.com/meet-your-dental-team/` and `.../areas-we-serve/`
Why it wins as a local-entity homepage: it anchors to a deeply credentialed, locally rooted responsible dentist (Dr. Erin Pickwick, DMD; full credentials in Section 5) and to genuine local coverage (named communities with drive times). The homepage's job is entity clarity plus the primary conversion, and the surrounding pages prove the entity is real, licensed, and local.

### BAD

**B1 - Superlative-stacked homepage with no substantiation** [LIVE, used as the anti-pattern] `celebritydentist.com` (Dr. Anthony Mobasser)
Real named dentist with real memberships (CDA, ADA, Penn Dental affiliation) and real experience, but the homepage stacks unsubstantiated superlatives on a YMYL medical page: "World Renowned Cosmetic Dentist," "best veneer dentist," "best Cosmetic Dentist in Los Angeles," and "all of the 1000's of clients he has treated have gotten more than they ever imagined." Why it fails: objective/comparative superlatives ("best," "#1") on a medical service require FTC substantiation and are restricted by many state dental-board advertising rules; the subjective-outcome claim ("more than they ever imagined") is an unmeasured results claim. The credentials are fine; the superlatives are the liability.
Gate/law: FAILS FTC substantiation + dental-board advertising. Note: this is a strong practice with a fixable copy problem, not a spam site.

**B2 - The stock-photo, no-named-dentist homepage** [PATTERN] fails the YMYL trust gate at the front door
"Your trusted family dentist" over a Getty smiling-model hero, no named DDS/DMD, no license, no real NAP, and self-marked Review/AggregateRating JSON-LD to mint star snippets (which renders nothing since 2019 and signals manipulation). Why it fails: on a health homepage the missing named, licensed human is the trust gate; the self-serving review schema is wasted-to-harmful markup.
Gate/law: FAILS medical authorship + review-schema rule (do not self-mark your own reviews).

## 5. About / team page (the E-E-A-T and trust surface)

The single most important page in a dental site for YMYL trust. This is where the credential, the license, the school, and the experience live.

### GOOD

**G1 - Dr. Erin Pickwick, Grayson Heritage** [LIVE] `graysonheritagedental.com/meet-your-dental-team/`
Textbook dental YMYL E-E-A-T. Credential and school ("Doctor of Medicine in Dentistry, DMD, Medical College of Georgia School of Dentistry, 2010"), postgraduate training ("year-long GPR residency, University of Alabama and VA Medical Center, Birmingham"), quantified experience ("16+ years practicing, treated thousands of patients"), named memberships (American Dental Association, Academy of General Dentistry, Georgia Dental Association, Hinman Dental Society), a leadership marker ("former president of the Georgia Academy of General Dentistry"), first-hand local experience ("daughter of Dr. Kathy Huber, grew up assisting in her mother's office," resides in Lawrenceville), and community proof ("volunteers with Georgia Missions of Mercy"). Every credential is externally verifiable against the school, the boards, and the state license.
Gate/law: passes the medical-authorship / E-E-A-T gate at the highest bar. This is the model dental about page.

**G2 - Nashville Aesthetic Dentistry team** [LIVE] `drdenniswells.com`
Why it wins: leads with a verifiable accreditation ("AACD Accredited since 1994"), names every provider with a real degree (DDS/DMD), and shows genuine expertise markers (developed a named proprietary veneer technique; teaches other dentists; treats dentists as patients). Experience is shown, not asserted.
Gate/law: passes medical authorship + real-experience proof.

### BAD

**B1 - The credential-free "team" page** [PATTERN] fails the YMYL trust gate
Smiling stock "team" photos, first names only, no dental school, no DDS/DMD, no license number, no memberships, generic "years of combined experience." Why it fails: on a YMYL page the credential and the named responsible human ARE the trust signal; without them there is nothing for a rater or an answer engine to verify.
Gate/law: FAILS medical authorship + external-verifiability.

**B2 - Misrepresented specialty status** [PATTERN] state dental-board + FTC advertising violation
An about page calling a general dentist a "specialist in cosmetic dentistry" or "endodontic specialist" when the dentist holds no ADA-recognized specialty license. Cosmetic dentistry is **not** an ADA-recognized specialty; many state boards prohibit a general dentist from advertising as a "specialist" in a non-recognized area, and misrepresenting specialty status is a documented board-discipline and FTC-deception trigger. Why it fails: the claim is verifiable against the state board and is false; YMYL trust collapses and the practice risks discipline.
Gate/law: FAILS FTC/board advertising + no-overstated-credentials. This is verifiable in the wrong direction, which is worse than vague.

## 7. The dental-YMYL cross-cutting gates (these sit on top of every page above)

These four gates are dental-specific and non-negotiable. A page can pass every doorway and conversion test in the playbook and still fail here, and failing here can cost the practice real money in fines, not just rankings.

### 7.1 Medical authorship / "reviewed by" (E-E-A-T)

Every page making a clinical claim ties to a named, credentialed, licensed dentist (DDS or DMD), and clinical/blog content carries a "Medically reviewed by Dr. X, DDS" line with a date. Done right: **Dental Made Easy** clinical content "medically reviewed by Dr. Rafael Boruchov, DDS" [SEARCH]. Done at the highest bar: **Dr. Erin Pickwick's** fully verifiable bio (Section 5, G1). The common miss: cost/treatment pages with no named reviewer (Section 2, B1). Trust is the dominant YMYL E-E-A-T aspect; this gate is the price of admission.

### 7.2 HIPAA-safe review handling (the sharpest dental-specific liability)

Responding to a patient's online review by naming or confirming the patient, their treatment, or their visit is a HIPAA disclosure of protected health information. Real enforcement, all cited:
- **Elite Dental Associates - $10,000 OCR settlement** for disclosing PHI in a Google review response [SEARCH] (cda.org, dentistryiq.com).
- **New Vision Dental - $23,000 settlement** for PHI in review responses [SEARCH].
- **Manasa Health Center - $30,000 settlement** for disclosing four patients' diagnosis/treatment info in Google review responses [SEARCH].
- **North Carolina dentist - $50,000 civil penalty** for naming the patient, the treatment plan, and specific visits in a review reply [SEARCH] (bassberry.com, docseducation.com).
Rule for the system: on-page testimonials and any modeled review reply must never confirm a person was a patient or reference their treatment. Display first-party testimonials collected with written permission; do not reproduce identifiable clinical detail. This gate governs the review blocks on the homepage, service-in-city, and about pages.

### 7.3 HIPAA before/after photo consent

Before/after smile photos are PHI. Per dentplicity.com/blog/dental-before-after-photos-marketing [LIVE]: using them without written authorization "can result in fines ranging from $10,000 to over $100,000 per incident." A valid release must state the specific images, the purpose (website/social/print), whether the patient is identified, a duration, the right to revoke, that authorization is voluntary and does not affect treatment, plus a signature and date; it must **not** be bundled into general new-patient paperwork; verbal consent is insufficient; and revocation means prompt removal from all channels. Safer default: close-up intraoral photos (teeth and tissue only) do not reveal identity and still show the clinical result. Done right on-page: Dr. Dennis Wells' gallery disclaimer "our ACTUAL patients" (Section 4, G1) signals real, consented cases. This gate governs every before/after and smile-gallery block.

### 7.4 FTC substantiation + dental-board advertising

- **Fake or incentivized reviews:** promoting a review a real patient did not write, misrepresenting experience, or paying/compensating for reviews violates the FTC rule; the maximum civil penalty is **$51,744 per violation** [SEARCH] (cda.org). "Leave us a 5-star review and we'll enter you in a drawing" is a named violation.
- **Superlatives and outcome claims:** "best dentist in [city]," "#1," "world-renowned," "painless," "guaranteed" require substantiation and are restricted by state dental-board advertising rules. See Section 4, B1 (celebritydentist.com superlatives) and Section 2, B2 (guaranteed outcomes).
- **Financing:** published financing terms with a stated repayment period ("0% APR for 12 months") are triggering terms under Truth-in-Lending / Reg Z and may require full disclosure. Sloan's Lake (Section 2, G2) names financing partners without dangling an unqualified teaser rate.
