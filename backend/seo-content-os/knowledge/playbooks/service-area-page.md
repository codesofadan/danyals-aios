# The Service-Area Page: The Definitive Build Playbook

Leaf-artifact build spec for SEO-CONTENT-OS. A writer handed this file plus one real service-area business (name, real services, the real towns and counties it actually books jobs in, real reviews, real photos, real license numbers) can produce a single service-area page that communicates coverage, ranks, gets cited by answer engines, and stays on the safe side of Google's doorway line, with zero further guidance.

This is the page type that most often gets a local business penalized, because it sits one bad decision away from a doorway-page network. The whole playbook is built around keeping that one decision correct.

Read `knowledge/doctrine/seo-system-doctrine.md` (Law 8) and `knowledge/doctrine/google-compliance-spine.md` first. This page type is where Law 8 and the no-doorway hard rule are tested hardest.

Companion playbook: `local-service-location-page-playbook.md` (harvested from MARKETING-OS) covers the single city/service-in-city page. This file covers the coverage page that sits above those and decides when they should exist at all. Read both when a client is a service-area business.

Every quantitative figure below is directional and carries a source. There is no live A/B test behind this document. Re-open the named primary before quoting any number to a client. Local SEO facts (ranking-factor positions, policy dates, GBP mechanics) perish fast; re-verify quarterly.

## 1. Purpose, the one job, and the decision that governs everything

### The one job

A service-area page communicates and ranks for the geographic footprint of a business that travels to its customers. Its one job: make a stranger who searches "do you cover my town" get an unambiguous yes, backed by proof the business really works there, and give an answer engine a clean, extractable statement of the coverage it can cite. If a visitor cannot finish the job on the page (confirm coverage, see local proof, call or book), the page has failed regardless of how it ranks.

Service-area pages target businesses that go to the customer and often have no public storefront: plumbers, HVAC techs, electricians, roofers, landscapers, mobile mechanics, pest control, cleaning crews, towing. BrightLocal states the distinction cleanly: service-area pages "revolve around how a traveling business supports a region, not how a customer visits a store" ([brightlocal.com/learn/service-area-pages](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/service-area-pages/), verify). If customers come to you, you want a location page. If you go to them, you want a service-area page.

### The three-way distinction the writer must hold

This is the heart of the playbook. There are three different artifacts, and confusing them is the #1 way a local site walks into a penalty:

1. **The service-area (coverage) page.** ONE page. It states the whole region the business covers (map plus a real list of counties/cities), proves the business operates across that region, and converts. It is a hub. It does not try to rank for every individual "[service] [city]" query on its own body.

2. **The individual location / city page.** One page per city, each one carrying genuinely unique, first-hand local substance (named neighborhoods, local failure modes, city-tagged reviews, real completed jobs). It ranks for "[service] [city]". Its full build spec is `local-service-location-page-playbook.md`. It is a spoke.

3. **The doorway-page network.** Many near-duplicate city pages where the city name is the only variable, spun from a template to blanket a keyword list. This is a Google spam-policy violation. It is not a page type you build. It is the failure state this playbook exists to prevent.

The service-area page is the hub. Individual city pages are spokes. A doorway network is what you get when you generate spokes you cannot fill with real local substance.

### The decision rule (memorize this)

The question every service-area build must answer: for each area the business covers, does it get an entry on the ONE coverage page, or does it earn its OWN individual city page?

**The rule:**

> Give a city its own individual page ONLY when BOTH are true: (a) the business books real, recurring jobs in that city, and (b) you can truthfully write the local-proof block for it (Section 4.3) - named neighborhoods or landmarks the crew actually works in, a local condition or failure mode specific to that city, at least one real city-tagged job or review. For every other area the business covers, it lives as a listed entry on the ONE service-area page: named in the coverage list, honestly served, but not spun into its own thin page.

Two tests, both must pass, before a city gets its own page:

- **The booked-jobs test:** does the business actually do recurring paid work there, or is this a zip code someone found in a keyword tool? Tie page creation to real service areas the business books jobs in, never to a keyword list. If the crew has been to a town twice in two years, it is a coverage-list entry, not a page.
- **The local-proof test:** can you write Section 4.3 truthfully for this city right now, from real facts (crew notes, completed-job records, real reviews, real local conditions)? If you cannot, you do not have a page. You have a template with a city swapped in, which is the exact thing Google penalizes.

If a city fails either test, it does not get a page. It gets a line on the coverage list. There is no shame in a business serving 60 towns and having only 8 individual city pages. That is the correct, safe shape. The alternative - 60 thin pages - is the doorway network that gets the whole domain demoted.

John Mueller has stated that a set of roughly 1,300 location-based landing pages "would likely count as doorway pages" ([searchengineland.com/guide/service-area-pages](https://searchengineland.com/guide/service-area-pages), verify). Practitioner consensus for a genuine service-area business is to concentrate on 15-20 core service areas rather than papering the region ([searchengineland.com](https://searchengineland.com/guide/service-area-pages), verify). The coverage list can be long and honest; the set of individual pages must be short and real.

### Why this page exists at all

If a business only builds individual city pages, the coverage question ("do you serve MY town, which does not have a page?") goes unanswered, and there is no hub to link the city pages into a browseable hierarchy - Google's own named safe pattern. The service-area page is that hub. It answers the coverage question for every town, links to the individual pages where they legitimately exist, and gives the answer engines one authoritative coverage statement to cite. It is the parent node in the hub-and-spoke structure that keeps individual city pages from being orphaned.

## 2. Target intent and query patterns

The service-area page serves a specific, high-value moment: the visitor has found the business and now needs to confirm it will physically come to them before they call. This is late-funnel, high-intent traffic. It is cheap to convert and expensive to lose.

**Primary query patterns (what the page must answer):**

- Coverage-confirmation: "do you serve [city]", "does [business] cover [zip]", "[service] in my area", "who covers [town/county]".
- Regional service-near-me: "[service] near me" fired from anywhere across the region (the pack and the organic result both point here).
- County / metro-level: "[service] in [county]", "[metro-area] [service]", "[service] [region] area".
- The mismatch query: "[service] [small town]" where the town is real coverage but does not merit its own page - the coverage page is what should rank and answer.

**Intent split:**

- **URGENT verticals** (burst pipe, no-heat, lockout, roof leak): the visitor is confirming coverage in seconds before dialing. Phone leads, response-time promise is above the fold, the coverage answer must be instant and unmissable. Ruler Analytics 2026 reports professional/home services convert heavily by phone (directional, verify at [ruleranalytics.com](https://www.ruleranalytics.com)); for these the tappable number is the primary CTA.
- **CONSIDERED verticals** (remodel, install, recurring maintenance, landscaping): the visitor is checking coverage while comparing two or three providers. Form or scheduler can lead, coverage proof and trust signals carry more weight, financing and portfolio matter.

**The AI-answer intent (Law 13).** Answer engines (AI Overviews, ChatGPT search, Perplexity, Claude) increasingly field "does [business] serve [place]" as a direct question. The page must lead with a self-contained, liftable coverage sentence they can quote verbatim: "[Business] provides [service] across [named region], including [3-5 named areas], based out of [base city] with [response-time] response." That one sentence is the single highest-leverage line on the page for GEO.

**What this page does NOT target:** it does not try to rank a single generic body for 60 separate "[service] [city]" queries. That is the job of the individual city pages (the spokes). The coverage page ranks for the regional, county, and coverage-confirmation queries, and it inherits and passes authority to the spokes.

## 3. The 2026 reality for service-area businesses (SABs)

This section is the load-bearing context, and it is where Google's guidance and measured reality openly contradict each other. The writer must hold both.

### GBP service-area settings and the hidden address

A service-area business on Google Business Profile can (and per Google's guidelines, should) hide its street address and instead declare the geographic areas it serves. Google renders the service area as a visual, non-ranking overlay: the areas you list in GBP tell users where you go, but Sterling Sky's testing indicates the listed service areas do **not** themselves rank you for those areas ([sterlingsky.ca/does-the-service-area-in-google-my-business-impact-ranking](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/does-the-service-area-in-google-my-business-impact-ranking/), verify). Proximity to the searcher, primary category, and reviews do the ranking work; the GBP service-area field is a display, not a lever.

### The hidden-address paradox (the single biggest SAB tension in 2026)

Google instructs SABs to hide their address. Sterling Sky's testing found a **negative correlation** between hiding the address and local-pack ranking: rankings dropped when the address was removed and recovered when it was added back ([sterlingsky.ca/hiding-address-google-business-ranking](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/hiding-address-google-business-ranking/), verify). A visible address is reported as roughly the 7th most influential local ranking factor in recent Local Search Ranking Factors data ([localfalcon.com](https://www.localfalcon.com/blog/when-should-you-hide-your-address-on-google-business-profile), verify). So Google tells SABs to do the thing that measurably lowers their pack visibility.

What this means for the writer:

- This is a GBP-configuration decision, not a website-copy decision, and it belongs to the client and their local-SEO strategist, not to this page. Surface it; do not decide it. The GBP audit spec (`seo-local-gbp-audit-2026.md`, item A3/B7) treats intentional SAB hiding as sometimes correct, sometimes costly - a genuine judgment call, not a rule.
- Correlation plus one client A/B is not a universal law. Do not tell a client "unhide your address and you will rank." Do tell them the tension exists and it is a strategy conversation.
- **The website is a separate surface from GBP.** Even when the address is hidden on GBP, the site can and should carry a clear, honest service-area statement. Separate what GBP *displays* from what the *website* shows.

### Never fabricate or rent an address

The one hard line: never invent, rent, or borrow a street address (virtual mailbox, UPS box, coworking desk, a relative's house in the target city) to look more local. It is a GBP suspension trigger and a doorway signal at the same time. The 2026 suspension wave hit trades harder than most, and fabricated/ineligible addresses drive it (`seo-local-gbp-audit-2026.md`, item A3). If a client asks you to make them "look local" in a city they have no presence in, that request is the doorway impulse itself - refuse it and cite this section.

### The proximity handicap and how the page compensates

A SAB competes at a structural disadvantage in the local pack: it has no fixed address near most of the searchers in its region, and proximity is among the top 2-3 pack signals. No page fixes proximity. What the page and the surrounding local-SEO program *can* move: primary category correctness, review volume/velocity/response, entity consistency (NAP and `sameAs`), and a real GBP-linked local page for the areas that matter. Do not promise top-3 pack rank in a town where the client has no proximity - that is a proximity problem no copy can solve. Set that expectation in the client conversation, not on the page.

### The 2026 quality-over-volume shift

Google's 2024 spam updates and the core-plus-spam updates that followed gave the scaled-content-abuse policy real enforcement teeth, and over 80% of doorway-pattern pages lost rankings after the March 2024 core update (directional, [gsqi.com/marketing-blog/google-december-2024-spam-update-case-studies](https://www.gsqi.com/marketing-blog/google-december-2024-spam-update-case-studies/), verify). The market has moved to fewer, stronger, more differentiated pages. Programmatic templating that swaps a city token is the exact pattern the enforcement targets. In 2026, a service-area business wins with a strong coverage page plus a small set of genuinely local city pages, not a page per zip.

## 4. Section-by-section architecture

Each section carries: must-contain, named framework, local-SEO requirement, schema requirement, real-example precedent, anti-pattern, and a binary PASS test. Build the shared core once; branch on URGENT vs CONSIDERED where flagged.

### 4.1 Coverage hero

**Must contain:** an H1 that names the service and the region ("[Service] Across [Region/Metro]" or "Areas We Serve in [Region]"), a one-line self-contained coverage sentence that names the base city plus 3-5 real areas and a response commitment, a tappable `tel:` number, and the primary CTA (call for URGENT, form/scheduler for CONSIDERED). An authentic hero image of the real crew or a branded truck, never stock.

**Framework:** Message match plus the extractable-answer lead. The first sentence is written to be lifted verbatim by an AI Overview and to answer the coverage question before the visitor scrolls. Formula: `[Business] provides [service] across [named region], including [named areas], from [base city], with [response commitment].`

**Local-SEO requirement:** the H1 carries the region/metro term the page targets (county or metro, not a single city - the single cities belong to the spokes). The lead sentence names real places, never "and surrounding areas" alone.

**Schema requirement:** the coverage claim in the hero must exactly match the `areaServed` values in the JSON-LD (Section 5). Visible text and structured data state the same coverage.

**Real-example precedent:** Len The Plumber leads with "Areas We Serve in the Mid-Atlantic Region" and immediately names "Baltimore, MD, Washington, D.C., Northern Virginia, New Jersey, Pennsylvania & the Delaware Valley" ([lentheplumber.com/service-areas](https://lentheplumber.com/service-areas/), verify) - region-first H1, named coverage in the first breath.

**Anti-pattern:** a brand-voice hero naming neither service nor region ("Comfort, Delivered"). A hero that claims a region with zero named places. A single-city H1 on what is supposed to be the regional coverage hub (that collapses the hub into a spoke and cannibalizes the city page).

**PASS test:** the H1 names service and region; the first sentence names the base city and 3+ real areas and would answer "do you serve my area" if read aloud; phone and CTA are visible on a 375px viewport without scrolling.

### 4.2 The real areas served (map plus list) - the coverage core

**Must contain:** a visual map of the actual territory (embedded Google map of the base and coverage overlay, or a clean region graphic), AND a real, structured, text list of the areas served. The list must be crawlable HTML text, organized in a browseable hierarchy (by county or metro, then cities), with a "do we serve your area" zip/town lookup where feasible. Where an individual city page legitimately exists (passed the Section 1 decision rule), that city in the list links to its page. Where it does not, the city is plain text - named, honestly served, not linked to a thin page.

**Framework:** the browseable hierarchy (Google's own named safe structure - the opposite of "substantially similar pages closer to search results than a clearly defined, browseable hierarchy"). County/metro headings, cities nested beneath, "and more" only after real names.

**Local-SEO requirement:** this list is the hub of the hub-and-spoke structure. It is how individual city pages avoid being orphaned and how a new city page inherits crawl and authority. Internal links flow: coverage page links down to each real city page; each city page links back up to the coverage hub and across to relevant service pages. Never leave a city page reachable only through a JavaScript store-locator that renders no crawlable `<a href>` - that orphans it. The list must render as real anchors in HTML.

**Schema requirement:** every area named in this list should be represented in `areaServed` (as `City` / `AdministrativeArea` / `Place`, or a `GeoShape` radius where the coverage is a drive-radius rather than a named set). The list and the schema agree.

**Real-example precedent:** Len The Plumber nests cities under counties (Anne Arundel County: "Annapolis, Arnold, Brooklyn, Crofton, Edgewater, Glen Burnie...") with a "Schedule in [Location]" link per area resolving to real pages like `/baltimore-area/anne-arundel-county-md/` ([lentheplumber.com/service-areas](https://lentheplumber.com/service-areas/), verify). Bayonet Plumbing pairs a Google map with a zip-search prompt - "Do we serve your neighborhood?" - and an alphabetical list organized under 8 real hub locations, linking to dedicated pages like `/service-areas/tampa-fl` ([bayonet-inc.com/service-areas](https://www.bayonet-inc.com/service-areas), verify).

**Anti-pattern:** a bare wall of 300 city names each linking to its own near-duplicate page (the doorway shape). A map with no text list (uncrawlable, unciteable). A list where every entry links to a page that fails the local-proof test. Listing towns the business does not actually serve to catch more queries (fabricated coverage - a trust violation and, if the business then declines those jobs, a conversion leak and a review risk).

**PASS test:** the map is present AND a crawlable text list of real areas is present; the list is organized in a browseable county/metro hierarchy; every linked city resolves to a page that passes the local-proof test; no listed area is one the business does not truly serve.

### 4.3 Why-local proof, per region, without spinning up thin pages

This is the section that separates a safe coverage page from a doorway hub, and it is the one most builds skip. The coverage page must prove the business really operates across its region - without minting a thin page for every town to do it.

**Must contain (hard minimum):** on the ONE coverage page, 3-5 pieces of region-specific proof that a competitor could not copy by find-and-replace. Real completed-job references tied to named towns ("re-piped a 1970s ranch in Round Rock, cleared a mainline in Pflugerville last month"), city-tagged reviews with real names that resolve to a real Google profile, a named local condition that recurs across the region (the clay-soil slab leaks, the hard-water scale, the freeze-thaw bursts, the coastal salt corrosion), photos of the real crew and trucks on real local jobs, and named crew members who cover specific zones.

**Framework:** first-hand experience at regional altitude (the "E" in E-E-A-T, added Dec 2022 to reward has-actually-done-this proof). The 2026 moat is not specificity alone - an LLM can now fabricate convincing specifics across 40 towns in one pass - it is **external verifiability**: a review that resolves to a real profile, a dated geotagged job photo, a permit record a skeptic can look up. Every proof point should resolve to something checkable.

**Local-SEO requirement:** this block is how the coverage page earns the right to exist as content rather than as a lookup table. It is also the source you mine to decide which cities graduate to their own page: if you have enough real, verifiable, city-specific proof for a town that you could write a full local-proof block for it, that town is a candidate for its own page (Section 1 rule). If the only proof you have for a town is that it is in the coverage radius, it stays a list entry.

**Schema requirement:** none specific to this block, but named crew referenced here should appear as `Person` nodes where they are shown, and real reviews are displayed as on-page HTML (never self-serving `Review`/`AggregateRating` markup on your own nodes - since 2019 Google does not render self-controlled review stars; it is wasted markup).

**Real-example precedent:** the individual Roto-Rooter city pages are the gold standard for a graduated spoke (Pflugerville names Falcon Pointe, Highland Park, Blackhawk, clay soils, root intrusion, the SH-130 corridor - `local-service-location-page-playbook.md`, Section 8, verify live). On the coverage page itself, the equivalent is a condensed regional version: the recurring local conditions across the metro plus a handful of named-town job references, not a full page per town.

**Anti-pattern:** "We proudly serve [Region] and all surrounding areas" with zero named towns, conditions, jobs, or crew - the presumed-doorway tell. Generic "why choose us" boilerplate that reads identically for any region in the country. Fabricated local specifics (an LLM inventing neighborhoods or conditions the business never mentioned) - this is the fastest path to a trust failure and violates the no-fabricated-facts rule; every local fact comes from the SME interview or cited research, never invented.

**PASS test:** the coverage page carries 3+ verifiable, region-specific proof points (named towns in real job references, real city-tagged reviews, a named recurring local condition, real crew/truck photos); strip the region name from the proof block and it stops making sense (it fails to generalize, which is the goal); no local fact on the page is unsourced or invented.

### 4.4 Response-time and coverage promise

**Must contain:** an explicit, precise commitment the operation can actually keep - a response-time or arrival window ("same-day across the metro," "on-site within 90 minutes in the core service area"), a coverage guarantee ("if we list your town, we come to it - no trip surcharge inside the primary area"), or a workmanship guarantee. State the honest edge cases: if outer-ring towns carry a trip fee or a longer window, say so.

**Framework:** risk reversal matched to the SAB's specific scar tissue - the buyer's fear here is "will they actually come out to MY town, or am I too far?" The promise kills that specific objection.

**Local-SEO requirement:** none. **Operational gate (mandatory):** audit the real answer-rate and real drive times before publishing any response promise. A "same-day, anywhere we serve" promise that dispatch cannot keep converts the click and burns the lead, and for a SAB the drive-time reality varies across the coverage map - promise per zone honestly.

**Schema requirement:** none. Do not encode a response time as a structured claim you cannot keep.

**Real-example precedent:** Len The Plumber fronts "prompt, same day service" across its named coverage region ([lentheplumber.com](https://lentheplumber.com/service-areas/), verify); Bayonet fronts "24/7 Emergency Services" ([bayonet-inc.com/service-areas](https://www.bayonet-inc.com/service-areas), verify). Both tie the promise to the coverage claim, which is exactly the SAB move.

**Anti-pattern:** "Fast, reliable service you can count on" - commits to nothing, converts nothing. A blanket "24/7 same-day anywhere" the operation cannot honor at the edge of the map. A response promise with no zone honesty when drive times genuinely differ.

**PASS test:** an explicit, precise coverage-or-response promise is present; it is honest about zone differences; the operation has confirmed it can keep it.

### 4.5 Service overview

**Must contain:** a scannable list of the services offered across the region, the primary/most-searched service first and visually emphasized, each item benefit-led. Where individual service pages exist, each links out to its dedicated page.

**Framework:** scannability confirms "yes, we do your specific thing" without forcing reading. This block also feeds internal linking: coverage page links to service pages, service pages link down to the cities served.

**Local-SEO requirement:** a dedicated page per core service is a rising ranking factor (Whitespark: "dedicated page for each service" rose sharply; internal links to the GBP landing page entered the factor set - `local-service-location-page-playbook.md`, Section 2.3, verify). The coverage page is a hub that links these together, not a place to dump every service-times-city permutation (that is the thin, spammy pattern).

**Schema requirement:** each service can be represented as a `Service` node with `provider` pointing to the business `@id` and its own `areaServed` (Section 5). One `Service` node per genuine service, not one per service-city permutation.

**Real-example precedent:** Len The Plumber lists general plumbing, drain/sewer, water heaters, wells, AC, heating, and indoor air quality on the coverage hub, each linking deeper ([lentheplumber.com](https://lentheplumber.com/service-areas/), verify).

**Anti-pattern:** a 20-item equal-weight mega-list (paralysis, diluted focus). Cramming every service-plus-city combination onto the coverage page to catch long-tail queries (thin, spammy, exactly the scaled-content pattern).

**PASS test:** services are scannable, the primary service leads, and each genuine service links to its own page where one exists.

### 4.6 Local trust signals

**Must contain:** at least 3 distinct, verifiable trust signals - Google star rating with review count and source, license number(s) as literal lookup-able strings, insured/bonded status, years in business, relevant certifications. For a SAB with a hidden GBP address, these trust signals carry extra weight because the reassurance a storefront address gives is absent.

**Framework:** trust-before-benefit. Local buyers are trust-gated before benefit-gated, and inviting a tradesperson to your home from a business with no visible storefront raises the trust bar higher. The license number is the strongest single signal because it is a public regulatory record the visitor (and an answer engine) can verify.

**Local-SEO requirement:** license numbers are public records, safe to print, and a hard E-E-A-T "who is responsible" signal. Print the number and the carrier/insured status. Never print the insurance policy number or coverage limits (impersonation and claims-fraud vector).

**Schema requirement:** encode license/credential context where a schema property fits (e.g. the business `@type`, `areaServed`, and `sameAs` to the verified GBP). Do NOT self-mark `aggregateRating`/`review` about your own business.

**Real-example precedent:** Len The Plumber stacks "4.8 average Google rating," "120 skilled technicians," "60,000 families served every year," "30 years of service," and lists Master Plumber licenses per state in the footer ([lentheplumber.com](https://lentheplumber.com/service-areas/), verify). Bayonet prints its license numbers ("CAC058062 & CFC042998") directly on the page ([bayonet-inc.com/service-areas](https://www.bayonet-inc.com/service-areas), verify) - the correct move for a SAB with no storefront to anchor trust.

**Anti-pattern:** "Fully licensed and insured" as a floating badge with no number. A "5.0" with no review count (reads manufactured). Twelve logos diluting the two that matter (Google rating and state license).

**PASS test:** 3+ distinct, verifiable trust signals present; license number printed as a literal string; no policy numbers exposed; no self-serving review schema.

### 4.7 FAQs (coverage questions)

**Must contain (hard minimum):** 5 FAQs written as natural question headers, at least 3 of them coverage-specific: "Do you serve [outer town]?", "Is there a trip charge outside [core area]?", "How fast can you reach [region edge]?", "Do you cover [county]?", "What areas do you NOT serve?". Each answer is a self-contained, liftable 1-2 sentences.

**Framework:** objection kill plus passage-level AI citability. For a SAB, the top hesitation is a coverage/logistics question, so the FAQ is where the highest-intent doubts get resolved. Each answer written to be extracted whole by an answer engine (Law 13).

**Local-SEO requirement:** answer the real coverage logistics honestly, including the negative space ("we do not currently serve [X]") - honesty about non-coverage is a trust signal and prevents wasted calls. Do not pad keywords ("What is the best plumber in [town]? We are the best plumber in [town]" is a named spam tell).

**Schema requirement:** ship `FAQPage` JSON-LD, but expect no rich-result stars (Google restricted FAQ rich results to authoritative gov/health domains in Aug 2023). The markup aids parsing and AI extraction; it is not SERP decoration.

**Real-example precedent:** the graduated Roto-Rooter city pages carry 13+ location-specific FAQs each (`local-service-location-page-playbook.md`, Section 8); on the coverage hub the equivalent is coverage-logistics FAQs rather than per-city ones.

**Anti-pattern:** generic FAQs that answer nothing local ("What are your hours?" with no coverage content). Keyword-padded question/answer pairs that say nothing to a human. Claiming coverage in a FAQ that contradicts the coverage list.

**PASS test:** 5+ FAQs, 3+ coverage-specific, each answer self-contained and honest (including any non-coverage), `FAQPage` JSON-LD present.

### 4.8 Closing CTA and NAP

**Must contain:** a final CTA repeating call and form/scheduler, the canonical NAP (name, phone, and - per the client's GBP strategy - either the real address or an honest service-area statement), hours, and a repeat of the coverage confirmation ("serving [region]; call to confirm your town").

**Framework:** last-chance conversion for the scroller who read the whole page, plus the NAP consistency that anchors the entity.

**Local-SEO requirement:** NAP on the page must match the GBP and the schema exactly (same phone digits, same name root, same formatting tolerance). **SAB-specific:** the website can show a service-area statement even when the GBP address is hidden - separate the two surfaces. Never show a fabricated or rented address. If a tracking number is used for paid channels, keep the true GBP-matched number as the static canonical NAP in HTML and JSON-LD; swap tracking numbers client-side only, gated to exclude crawlers, so Googlebot renders the static number.

**Schema requirement:** the NAP in the closing block matches the `LocalBusiness` node exactly (Section 5). `hasMap` points to a real Google Maps URL, not a bare Place ID.

**Real-example precedent:** Len The Plumber closes with its canonical phone ((410) 694-7747) and per-state licensing in the footer; Bayonet with its 800 number and license numbers, plus the "8 locations" hub structure ([lentheplumber.com](https://lentheplumber.com/service-areas/), [bayonet-inc.com](https://www.bayonet-inc.com/service-areas), verify).

**Anti-pattern:** a tracking number in the visible NAP and schema that never matches GBP. A fabricated street address to look local. A NAP that disagrees with the GBP.

**PASS test:** canonical NAP present and GBP-matched; SAB coverage stated honestly (no fabricated address); phone matches schema; `hasMap` is a real Maps URL.

### The default coverage-page skeleton

A defensible starting order, A/B per vertical (not a proven law):

1. Coverage hero (region-first H1 + extractable coverage sentence + primary CTA + click-to-call)
2. Trust signals (rating + count, license numbers, insured, years)
3. Response/coverage promise (honest by zone)
4. Areas served: map + crawlable browseable list (linked spokes where real)
5. Why-local regional proof (named towns, conditions, jobs, crew - the un-templated core)
6. Service overview (primary first, linked to service pages)
7. Coverage FAQs (5+, `FAQPage` JSON-LD)
8. Closing CTA + canonical NAP + coverage restatement
9. Sticky mobile CTA bar (whole scroll)

For URGENT verticals, the response promise and phone move up and lead. For CONSIDERED, trust signals, portfolio, and scheduler move up.

## 5. The doorway line and the compliant coverage-schema pattern

This is the section that keeps the business out of the penalty zone. Read it as the governing constraint on every decision above.

### Google's actual words

Verified verbatim from Google's Search spam policies ([developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)):

> "Doorway abuse is when sites or pages are created to rank for specific, similar search queries."

Named patterns include "having multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" and creating "substantially similar pages that are closer to search results than a clearly defined, browseable hierarchy."

> "Scaled content abuse is when many pages are generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users."

Named patterns include "using generative AI tools or other similar tools to generate many pages without adding value for users."

Read those together and the rule is exact: a network of near-identical city pages, spun to catch "[service] [city]" queries, that funnel the visitor to one central conversion point, is a doorway network AND scaled content abuse. Since the March 2024 core-plus-spam updates, this is enforced with real teeth.

### The line, stated operationally

The doorway test is behavioral and about usefulness, not raw page count (RicketyRoo, [ricketyroo.com/blog/location-page-spam](https://ricketyroo.com/blog/location-page-spam/), verify):

- A site with 50 thin, city-swapped pages can be penalized.
- A site with a strong coverage page plus 12 genuinely local, useful city pages is untouched.
- The trigger is: can the user finish the job here (local proof, local logistics, a real conversion path), or are they forwarded to a generic central form from a page that exists only to rank?

**When ONE coverage page with a list is correct:** whenever you cannot write a genuinely unique, verifiable local-proof block per town. Which is most of the time. The default for a service-area business is one strong coverage page (map + honest browseable list) plus individual city pages ONLY for the handful of cities that pass both the booked-jobs test and the local-proof test (Section 1). If in doubt, it is a list entry, not a page.

**When individual city pages are justified:** only when each page carries a genuine majority of location-specific substance a competitor could not reproduce by find-and-replace - named neighborhoods, a named local condition, real city-tagged reviews, a real completed job, real local photos - AND the business books real recurring work there. A practitioner heuristic is roughly half brand/service boilerplate, half unique local substance; treat the ~50% as a working floor, never a safe harbor (there is no Google-published percentage). The real test: could a competitor regenerate this page by swapping the city token? If yes, it fails. If no, it is a legitimate spoke.

### The RicketyRoo safe/risky/spammy axis (use as the gate)

| Trait | Safe | Risky | Spammy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Content | Localized, original, relevant | Mostly duplicated, minor changes | City names swapped, boilerplate everywhere |
| Local proof | Testimonials, photos, examples | Vague area mentions | No visible connection |
| Navigation | Clearly linked (hub-and-spoke) | Buried/inconsistent | Orphaned, no clear path |
| Conversion | Clear CTAs, trust signals | Generic forms | No real CTA, ranking-only |
| Transparency | Clear office vs service area | Implies presence without proof | Fakes an address / implies location deceptively |
| Engagement | Indexed, real dwell | Inconsistent indexing | Crawled-not-indexed, zero visibility |

Source: [ricketyroo.com/blog/location-page-spam](https://ricketyroo.com/blog/location-page-spam/), verify. Every page in a service-area build must sit fully in the "Safe" column. Any single "Spammy" cell = do not publish.

### The compliant way to express coverage: `areaServed` schema

The schema layer is how you tell Google and the answer engines your true coverage WITHOUT minting a page per area. This is the structured, penalty-free alternative to a doorway network. Express the whole coverage set once, in JSON-LD on the coverage page, using `areaServed`.

`areaServed` on a `LocalBusiness` (or the specific subtype) or a `Service` node accepts `AdministrativeArea`, `City`/`Place`, `GeoShape`, or plain `Text`, and an array covers multiple regions. Prefer `areaServed` over the superseded `serviceArea`; it has wider type coverage and cleanly decouples coverage from the physical address, which is exactly the SAB need ([schema.org/areaServed](https://schema.org/areaServed); [karpi.studio/schema-glossary-terms/area-served](https://www.karpi.studio/schema-glossary-terms/area-served), verify).

Two patterns:

- **Named-set coverage** (you serve a defined list of towns/counties): `areaServed` as an array of `City` and `AdministrativeArea` objects, each optionally carrying a Wikidata `@id` for entity clarity.
- **Radius coverage** (you serve a drive-radius from a base): `areaServed` as a `GeoShape` with a `circle` value in the format `"latitude longitude radiusInMeters"` (schema.org `circle` uses meters by default - verify units before shipping) ([schema.org/GeoShape](https://schema.org/GeoShape), verify). This honestly encodes "we go this far from here."

Full JSON-LD in Section 10. The point of this section: the schema is how you claim coverage of 60 towns compliantly. You do NOT need 60 pages to rank for or be cited for that coverage. You need one coverage page, honest content, a browseable list, a handful of real city pages, and complete `areaServed` markup.

## 6. Best-in-class teardowns (real pages, verify live before quoting)

**WINNER - Len The Plumber Heating & Air** ([lentheplumber.com/service-areas](https://lentheplumber.com/service-areas/), opened; verify live). Why it works as a coverage hub: region-first H1 ("Areas We Serve in the Mid-Atlantic Region") with named coverage in the first line; a visual regional map plus a browseable county-then-city hierarchy (Anne Arundel County: Annapolis, Arnold, Crofton, Glen Burnie, "and more"); each area links to a real nested page (`/baltimore-area/anne-arundel-county-md/`), so the hub-and-spoke structure is intact and no city page is orphaned; strong SAB trust stack (4.8 Google rating, 120 technicians, 60,000 families/year, 30 years, per-state Master Plumber licenses in the footer); "prompt, same day service" as the coverage/response promise. It is the correct shape: one coverage hub feeding a real, browseable set of area pages, with the trust signals a storefront-less business needs. Authority caveat: Len The Plumber is a large regional brand with link and entity strength a single-truck operator does not have; a smaller operator copies the structure but must earn the ranking benefit through reviews, GBP, and links.

**WINNER (hybrid with a zip-lookup) - Bayonet Plumbing, Heating & Air** ([bayonet-inc.com/service-areas](https://www.bayonet-inc.com/service-areas), opened; verify live). Why it works: a single consolidated coverage page anchored by a Google map AND a zip-search tool prompting "Do we serve your neighborhood?" - the exact coverage-confirmation intent this page type serves; the list is organized under 8 real hub locations (Tampa, Sarasota, Ocala, North Port, Lakeland, Jacksonville, Ft. Myers, Clermont), each linking to a dedicated page (`/service-areas/tampa-fl`); license numbers printed on-page (CAC058062 & CFC042998), 24/7 messaging, financing. The zip-lookup is a best-practice pattern most builds omit: it turns the coverage question into an interactive, instant yes/no. Watch-the-line note: with 200+ towns listed under 8 hubs, Bayonet must ensure the individual hub pages carry real local substance and the 200+ non-hub towns stay as list entries, not spun into thin pages - a live crawl of the linked pages is the check.

**GRADUATED-SPOKE STANDARD - Roto-Rooter city pages** (e.g. Pflugerville, Baltimore; documented in `local-service-location-page-playbook.md`, Section 8; verify live). These are not coverage pages - they are the individual city pages a coverage hub should link to when a city passes the decision rule. They show what "graduated" looks like: roughly 70% genuinely local content, named neighborhoods, a named local mechanism (clay soils, root intrusion, freeze-thaw pipe bursts), a named local licensed manager, city-tagged reviews, 13+ location-specific FAQs. Use these as the bar a city page must clear before it earns its own URL. If you cannot get a page near this bar, the city stays on the coverage list.

## 7. Worst teardowns (the doorway pattern, and why it is penalized)

An honesty note first: I will not brand a specific named business as a penalized doorway site without direct evidence that it was penalized - that would be fabrication, and potentially defamatory. What follows is the verified pattern class and the documented enforcement, plus the publicly-analyzed near-miss.

**The pattern class: "we serve [hundreds of cities]" thin doorway networks.** The shape is consistent and well-documented: a business (or an agency on its behalf) generates a page per city across a whole state or country - "[Service] in [City]" - where the body is identical except the city token, no page carries a real local-proof block, and every page funnels to one central contact form. This matches Google's doorway-abuse pattern verbatim ("multiple pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page") and the scaled-content-abuse pattern ("many pages generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings"). Why it gets penalized: after the March 2024 core-plus-spam update, documented case studies show doorway-pattern sites losing rankings at scale (over 80% of doorway pages affected in some analyses; sharp organic traffic drops within 30 days - directional, [gsqi.com/marketing-blog/google-december-2024-spam-update-case-studies](https://www.gsqi.com/marketing-blog/google-december-2024-spam-update-case-studies/), verify). The concrete smoke signal a builder can watch: "Crawled - currently not indexed" and "Discovered - currently not indexed" spreading across a city-page cohort in Search Console is Google treating the set as scaled content.

**The John Mueller data point.** Asked about a set of roughly 1,300 location landing pages, Mueller indicated they "would likely count as doorway pages" ([searchengineland.com/guide/service-area-pages](https://searchengineland.com/guide/service-area-pages), verify). The number itself is not the trigger - usefulness is - but at that scale it is nearly impossible for every page to carry real local substance, which is why volume correlates with doorway risk.

**The publicly-analyzed near-miss (compliant but generic).** Bill Joplin's heating repair page for Plano, TX (documented in `local-service-location-page-playbook.md`, Section 8) is a legitimate 1978 business, not a doorway, but it is the failure mode this playbook prevents on the content side: the H1 immediately broadens to seven other towns, diluting the target, and the body ("Common Heating System Issues," "Why Choose Us") reads identically for any city - a template with the city swapped in. It is "risky" on the RicketyRoo axis: real business, generic content, no named local proof. It will lose to a competitor with named neighborhoods and a response promise. The lesson for the coverage page: even a real business fails when the content generalizes; the fix is real local substance, not more pages.

**Why the doorway network always loses, stated plainly:** it optimizes a proxy (page count against a keyword list) instead of the reward function (useful, verifiable local value). That is Law 8 in reverse. The pages carry no verifiable first-hand experience, so they earn no trust; they duplicate, so they trip scaled-content detection; they funnel to one form, so they trip doorway detection.

## 8. Google-compliance notes

Every service-area page ships against these. Any fail = do not publish.

- **Doorway abuse:** no network of near-duplicate city pages funneling to one page. One coverage hub + only genuinely-unique city pages. Structure the coverage list as a clearly-defined browseable hierarchy, not a page-farm ([developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)).
- **Scaled content abuse:** do not generate many pages by templating a city token. Tie page creation to real booked service areas, not a keyword list. If you cannot write the local-proof block truthfully, do not make the page.
- **Accurate representation of coverage:** never list a city the business does not actually serve. Fabricated coverage is a trust violation, a conversion leak (calls you decline), and a review risk (the customer you turned away). Coverage claimed in text, in `areaServed`, and on GBP must be the true coverage.
- **No fabricated or rented address:** never invent, rent, or borrow a street address to look local. GBP suspension trigger and doorway signal.
- **Address visibility is a strategy decision, not a copy decision:** the hidden-address paradox (Section 3) belongs to the client and their GBP strategist. Surface the tension; do not decide it in copy or promise a ranking outcome from unhiding.
- **NAP and schema integrity:** visible NAP = schema NAP = GBP, exactly. `hasMap` is a Maps URL. No self-serving `review`/`aggregateRating` markup.
- **No fabricated local facts:** every named town, condition, job, review, license, and year comes from the client profile, the SME interview, or cited research. Never invented. This is the single fastest path to a trust penalty and it is a hard rule (`CLAUDE.md`).
- **Honest promises only:** audit answer-rate and drive times before publishing a response/coverage promise. Verify financing terms against Reg Z and consent language against TCPA before publishing those.
- **Cite or do not claim:** any external stat on the page carries its real source; never fabricate a URL.

## 9. Voice and humanization notes

Both voice layers are always on (`knowledge/voice/humanization-layer.md`). For the service-area page specifically:

- **Substance first, craft second.** A coverage page reads human when it names real towns, real conditions, real crew, and a real coverage promise - not when it is run through any rhythm pass. If a section reads generic, the fix is a missing local fact, not a swapped word. There is NO detector-evasion, NO humanizer chain, NO "passes AI detection" step, ever (Law 8, Hard Line 5). If a client asks for one, refuse and cite the doctrine.
- **The coverage sentence is spoken, not marketed.** The hero's extractable coverage line should sound like the owner answering the phone: "We cover the whole west side of the metro - Round Rock, Pflugerville, Cedar Park, and out to Georgetown." Plain, specific, confident. Not "proudly serving the greater metropolitan area and its surrounding communities."
- **Honesty is the voice.** The strongest SAB trust move is telling the truth about the edges: "We don't cover south of the river yet." "Towns past the 40-minute line carry a trip fee." That candor reads unmistakably human and builds more trust than any superlative. It also happens to be compliant.
- **Kill the coverage-page clichés.** Blocklist tells that infest this page type specifically: "and surrounding areas" (used alone, with no named places), "proudly serving," "your trusted local [trade]," "no job too big or too small," "servicing all of [region]." Each is a genericity flag. Replace with named places and specific facts.
- **Vary the list, not just the prose.** A coverage list is inherently repetitive. Break it with real county headings, a zip-lookup, a one-line local note per hub ("Tampa crew runs out of the Hillsborough shop"), so the page does not read as a machine dump of place names.
- **Per-client layer.** Run the draft against the client's `one_line_direction` and `tone_by_context`. Ask: would this owner say this coverage promise out loud to a customer who asked "do you come to my town?" Rewrite what fails.

## 10. Meta formulas and JSON-LD

### Meta title formulas

- `Service Areas | [Service] Across [Region/Metro] | [Business]`
- `Areas We Serve - [Service] in [Metro] & Surrounding Counties | [Business]`
- `[Service] Coverage: [County], [County] & [Metro] | [Business]`

Keep under ~60 characters of visible width (Google truncates by pixel width, expect rewrites). Lead with the coverage/region term the page targets, not a single city.

### Meta description formula

`[Business] provides [service] across [named region], including [3-4 named areas], from [base city]. [Response/coverage promise]. Call [phone] or check if we cover your town.`

Written to double as the extractable coverage answer and to name real places, so it earns the click and can be lifted by an answer engine.

### JSON-LD (LocalBusiness with areaServed + Service + BreadcrumbList)

Validate every template with Google's Rich Results Test before shipping. Mirror every value to the visible page and the GBP exactly. Do not self-mark `review`/`aggregateRating`. Do not invent geo precision. Verify `GeoShape.circle` units (meters by default) before use.

## 11. Finished-page checklist (includes the doorway decision test)

Binary. Any single FAIL = do not publish.

**The doorway decision test (run FIRST, before writing):**

- [ ] Is every individual city page justified by BOTH the booked-jobs test AND the local-proof test? (any city page that fails either = FAIL - demote it to a coverage-list entry)
- [ ] Could a competitor regenerate any city page by swapping the city token? (if YES for any page = FAIL)
- [ ] Is the coverage expressed as ONE hub page + a browseable list + only genuinely-unique spokes, NOT a page-per-town network? (page-farm = FAIL)
- [ ] Does every page sit fully in the RicketyRoo "Safe" column? (any "Spammy" cell = FAIL)

**Coverage and content:**

- [ ] H1 names service + region; first sentence names base city + 3+ real areas and answers "do you serve my area"
- [ ] Map present AND a crawlable, browseable text list of real areas present
- [ ] 3+ verifiable, region-specific proof points (named towns in real jobs, real city-tagged reviews, named local condition, real crew/truck photos)
- [ ] Strip-the-region test: remove the region name and the proof block stops making sense (it must fail to generalize)
- [ ] Explicit, honest response/coverage promise (zone differences stated; operation can keep it)
- [ ] Services scannable, primary first, linked to service pages where they exist
- [ ] 3+ verifiable trust signals; license number as a literal string; no policy numbers; no self-serving review schema
- [ ] 5+ FAQs, 3+ coverage-specific, each self-contained, including honest non-coverage
- [ ] No listed area is one the business does not truly serve
- [ ] No fabricated local facts anywhere; every local specific sourced to SME or cited research

**Technical and compliance:**

- [ ] Canonical NAP present, GBP-matched, no fabricated/rented address
- [ ] SAB coverage stated honestly on the site even if the GBP address is hidden (two separate surfaces)
- [ ] Hidden-address decision flagged to the client as a strategy call, not decided in copy; no ranking outcome promised from unhiding
- [ ] Every city page reachable via a crawlable HTML link from the coverage hub (no JS-only store-locator orphaning)
- [ ] JSON-LD: LocalBusiness (correct subtype) + `areaServed` (matching the visible coverage) + Service + BreadcrumbList; `hasMap` is a real Maps URL; validated in Rich Results Test
- [ ] `areaServed` in schema exactly matches the coverage claimed in text and on GBP
- [ ] `FAQPage` JSON-LD present; no self-serving `review`/`aggregateRating`
- [ ] Meta title leads with region/coverage term; meta description names real places and doubles as the coverage answer
- [ ] Both voice layers run; coverage-page clichés ("and surrounding areas" alone, "proudly serving") removed; no em dash
- [ ] Answer-rate / drive-time audited before publishing any response promise; financing (Reg Z) and consent (TCPA) verified if present
