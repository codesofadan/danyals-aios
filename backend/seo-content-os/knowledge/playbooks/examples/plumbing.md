# Plumbing Example Library: Real Pages, Torn Down (live-verified 2026-07)

The reference corpus for every plumbing page this system writes. Six page types. For each: at least two real GOOD examples (live URL, what it does right, why it ranks or converts, which gate or law it exemplifies) and at least two BAD or penalty-risk examples (live URL where verified, otherwise a clearly-labeled real pattern, what fails, which gate catches it).

Governing law: `knowledge/doctrine/seo-system-doctrine.md`, especially **Law 8** (humanize with real facts and specificity, never detector-evasion). Every winner below reads human because it is made of verifiable local physics and a named licensed human, not because it was laundered. Read alongside the service-in-city playbook (`service-city-page.md`); this file is its plumbing-specific evidence base.

Every page here was fetched live in the window shown. Where a fetch was bot-blocked or 404, the entry says so and is labeled a pattern, not an invented page. No business, URL, license number, or figure below is fabricated; each was read off the live page. Re-verify perishable specifics (review counts, coupons, license validity) at build time.

## The plumbing-specific reality that governs all six page types

Before the examples, the four facts that make plumbing different from a generic local vertical. Every teardown below turns on one of them.

1. **Emergency vs planned intent splits the whole site.** Burst pipe, sewer backup, no hot water, gas leak, slab leak flooding a room: URGENT. The buyer is standing in water. Phone-first, click-to-call in the hero, response-time promise load-bearing. Repipe, tankless upgrade, water softener, planned water-heater replacement, bathroom rough-in: CONSIDERED. Form or financing-first, brand comparison, before/after, warranty. The same service (water heater) has both an emergency face ("no hot water tonight") and a considered face ("upgrade to tankless"). Decide which query the page serves before writing a line.

2. **The license number is plumbing's un-fakeable superpower.** Every US master plumber carries a state-issued, publicly-lookup-able number: Texas Master Plumber `M-#####`, Texas Responsible Master Plumber `RMP-#####`, California `CSLB #######`, Minnesota `PM######` / `PC######` plus per-city licenses. It is the single strongest E-E-A-T "who is responsible" signal AND an externally-verifiable local proof that defeats the strip-the-city test by itself. Winners print it. Losers write "fully licensed and insured" with no number. This is the sharpest tell in the entire vertical.

3. **The local condition is physical and measurable.** Hard water in grains per gallon (Sacramento 16-18 gpg, Austin "notoriously hard"), slab-on-grade foundations that turn a pinhole into a slab leak (TX, AZ), expansive "gumbo" clay that shears underground pipe, freeze-burst in the north (Minneapolis uninsulated pipe), lead service lines and cast iron in pre-war housing stock, tree-root intrusion, drought/conservation code, earthquake pipe damage, hurricane-surge sewer backup. These are causally tied to the service and independently confirmable (water authority, USGS, city code). They are what makes a plumbing page pass the strip-the-city test.

4. **Pricing is custom-quote, not fixed SKU.** Real shape: diagnostic/trip fee, "$55 off" coupon, flat quote before work, financing via Synchrony, occasional productized banner ("$2,299 garage water-heater install"). Honest operators critique the "$69 / $99 drain special" bait. A custom-quote service is not honestly price-snippet markup-able; do not mint `priceSpecification` rich results for it.

### GOOD

**G1. Roto-Rooter Sacramento** - `rotorooter.com/sacramento/` (live, fetched 2026-07)
- What it does right: H1 "Trusted Sacramento Plumber for Drains, Water Heaters & Water Cleanup." Names the franchise operator (Clyde Ashley) and prints the license (`CSLB 966412`). - Why it ranks/converts: the grains-per-gallon figure and the county list are externally verifiable and collapse the moment you swap the city. 4.9 / 17 reviews, a $79-off coupon, and a "No Hassle Guarantee" close the loop.
- Gate/law it exemplifies: Minimum Unique Local Substance bar (3+ anchors, 1+ causal condition, verifiable proof) + the strip-the-city test. Law 8 (specificity, not spun text).
- Caveat: 17 reviews is thin next to a metro leader; a single-location operator inherits none of Roto-Rooter's national link authority. The structure is necessary, not sufficient.

**G2. Roto-Rooter Minneapolis** - `rotorooter.com/minneapolis/` (live, fetched 2026-07)

### BAD / penalty-risk

**B1. Reliant Plumbing, Round Rock** - `reliantplumbing.com/areas-we-serve/round-rock/` (live, fetched 2026-07)
- What fails: H1 is a bare "Round Rock Plumbers." Zero named neighborhoods, streets, or landmarks. No Round Rock condition (no hard-water number, no soil, no foundation type). The three genuinely local sentences that exist are good ("Max Hicks ... grew up here and as a little tyke would drive his go kart up and down 79 back when it was a one lane street"; "installing rough ins for builders like Lamar and KB Homes") but they are drowned by boilerplate service blocks and three unattributed testimonials (Art, Robert, L Cam) with no city context. Strip "Round Rock" and the body reads as the template it is.
- Why it is penalty-risk: it fails the strip-the-city test on balance. The owner-texture keeps it off "penalty-certain," but it sits one core update from "Crawled - currently not indexed." It is the exact "real local seed, template body" failure mode.
- Gate that catches it: doorway / strip-the-city test; MULS bar (fails anchors + condition).

**B2. Pattern - the "[City] Plumbers + two generic paragraphs + phone" city page** (clearly-labeled real pattern; documented by plumbingwebmasters.com/plumber-city-page-seo/ and blaksheepcreative and verified in the wild on the Reliant network above)
- What fails: headline "[Service/Plumber] in [City Name]," two paragraphs that apply to any town, a services list, unattributed praise, and a phone number. The body is word-for-word identical across every city child except the city token.
- Why: this is Google's named doorway example ("substantially similar pages ... closer to search results than a clearly defined, browseable hierarchy") and the scaled-content-abuse "many pages ... without adding value" case. A cohort of these reads to SpamBrain as one manufactured network.
- Gate: strip-the-city test + the doorway smoke detector (index-coverage watch across the city cohort).

## 2. Service page (single service, brand-wide)

Job: rank + convert for the service place-agnostically ("drain cleaning," "water heater repair"), and act as the hub that links down to service-in-city children.

### GOOD

**G1. Roto-Rooter drain-cleaning hub** - `rotorooter.com/plumbing/drain-cleaning/` (live, fetched 2026-07)
- What it does right: H1 "Drain Cleaning & Clogged Drain Services." Describes the real service mechanics, not adjectives: mechanical snaking, hydro jetting, video-camera inspection, "cleaning the entire pipeline from end to end" with a 6-month guarantee. Names the real sub-jobs (storm drain, floor drain, laundry, sewer line). Handles pricing honestly ("varies based on ... type of drain, type of clog, job size") and explicitly critiques competitors' "$69 or $99" bait deals as misleading. Links down to `/locations/` city children.
- Why it converts: the honest-pricing stance is a trust play, the sub-service list captures long-tail intent, and the hub-to-city linking is the correct silo shape (service hub links down; each city child links back up).
- Gate/law: passage-block protocol (each sub-service an extractable answer) + internal-linking silo. Correct place-agnostic altitude: it does NOT fake a local condition it cannot own brand-wide; the localization lives in the city children.

**G2. Abacus water-heater page** - `abacusplumbing.com/plumbing/water-heaters/` (live, fetched 2026-07)
- What it does right: a service page that correctly localizes to its metro. H1 "Water Heaters in Austin - Abacus Your Water Heater Experts." Ties the service causally to a measurable local condition: "Austin's notoriously hard water causes sediment buildup faster than other areas," and again in maintenance ("annual tank flushing removes sediment that Austin's hard water deposits") and lifespan ("tank water heaters typically last 10 to 12 years in Austin"). Real brand depth (Rheem, Bradford White, AO Smith, State, Noritz, Navien, Rinnai, Bosch), tank-vs-tankless split, license `M-20628` (Alan O'Neill), 4.85 / 643 reviews, 24/7.
- Why it converts: hard-water-to-sediment-to-lifespan is the un-fakeable causal chain for water heaters; naming eight real brands signals genuine stocking depth a template cannot fake.
- Gate/law: E-E-A-T Experience + MULS-grade specificity on a service page; correctly a service page anchored to a metro (a legitimate hybrid), not 160 token-swapped clones.

### BAD / penalty-risk

**B1. Jim England Plumbing slab-leak page** - `plumbingkeller.com/slab-leaks` (live, fetched 2026-07)
- What fails: H1 "Slab Leak Detection and Slab Leak Repair in Colleyville, TX" and a 25-city DFW list, but the body is generic contractor boilerplate. Detection is "advanced detection methods to pinpoint the source quickly" and "electronic listening devices, thermal imaging cameras" with no DFW-specific mechanism: no expansive-clay foundation movement, no slab-on-grade prevalence, no hard-water pinhole-corrosion cause, no local water table. Coupon is expired, no reviews on page. License `M12268` is present (the one thing it does right).
- Why it is penalty-risk: slab leaks are the most local-physics-driven service in Texas plumbing (clay soil + slab-on-grade + hard water), and this page names none of the physics. Strip the city list and nothing town-true remains. A city name list is not local substance.
- Gate: strip-the-city test; passage-block protocol (no extractable, causal, local answer).

**B2. Pattern - the self-marked-review service page** (clearly-labeled real technical pattern; per the service-city playbook and Google's 2019 change)
- What fails: a "top-quality water heater repair, our expert technicians" city-agnostic page that then wraps its own `Review` / `AggregateRating` JSON-LD around self-collected reviews to mint star snippets, plus keyword-padded FAQ ("What is the best plumber? We are the best plumber").
- Why: Google has not rendered review rich results for self-serving markup since 2019, so the stars never appear (wasted, and a spam-adjacent signal). The empty benefit-adjective body fails the uniqueness bar. The map-pack stars come from the GBP, never from page schema.
- Gate: schema-library (no self-serving Review/AggregateRating on your own nodes) + thin-content / passage-block.

## 3. Service-in-city page (the money page: one service x one city)

**G1. Roto-Rooter Houston** - `rotorooter.com/houston/` (city hub) with `/houston/emergency-plumber/` and `/houston/drain-cleaning/` children (live, fetched 2026-07)

**G2. Abacus water heater, Austin** - `abacusplumbing.com/plumbing/water-heaters/` (live; see 2.G2)

- As a combo/city page it fails identically: no Round Rock neighborhoods, no local condition, real body is a template with three owner sentences bolted on. Penalty-risk, not penalty-certain, because the owner texture is real.

**B2. Pattern - the suburb-swap / subdomain-per-city combo network** (clearly-labeled real reported patterns)

(b) `austin.yoursite.com`, `dallas.yoursite.com` subdomains funneling to one central form - Google's near-verbatim doorway example. (c) the 2026 version: AI-spun 160 combo pages, each convincingly specific but none tied to a booked job or a lookup-able permit.
- Why: all fail because pages were built for a keyword list, not for real service areas the crew books jobs in. (c) passes a naive strip-the-city read but fails external-verifiability.
- Gate: doorway policy + scaled-content-abuse (now governing AI Overviews too, per the May 2026 update) + external-verifiability test.

- Gate: E-E-A-T (claimed not shown) + real-photos gate (no stock in the proof zone).

## 6. Service-area page (coverage without doorway spam)

Job: present real coverage for a service-area business, link to genuinely substantive destination pages, never fabricate an address.

### GOOD

**G1. The Roto-Rooter model: a coverage list that points to substantive destinations** - the county-and-community blocks on `rotorooter.com/sacramento/` and `/minneapolis/` (live, fetched 2026-07)
- What it does right: names real counties and 11-18 real communities as an honest service-area statement inside a page that carries genuine local substance (grains-per-gallon water, freeze physics, license number). Transparent SAB coverage without a fabricated storefront. The list is a browseable hierarchy to real destination pages, not a doorway funnel.
- Why it works: Google's doorway line is "intermediate vs destination." These are destinations (local phone, license, condition, proof), so the coverage list is legitimate.
- Gate/law: doorway policy (destination, not intermediate) + NAP integrity (honest SAB, no rented address).

**G2. Reliant "Areas We Serve" hub** - `reliantplumbing.com/areas-we-serve/` (live, fetched 2026-07) - a structurally correct hub, with a sharp caveat
- What it does right structurally: ~50 cities organized into browseable regional groups (Austin, San Antonio, Waco, DFW), each city linking to its own subfolder page (`/areas-we-serve/austin/`, `/round-rock/`), on the main domain (not subdomains). Honest "serving ... and beyond." No fabricated address.
- Why it is a qualified winner: the skeleton is exactly right (flat subfolders, browseable hierarchy, one domain). This is how to structure coverage.
- The caveat (why it is only half a winner): the destination pages it links to are thin (see 1.B1 Round Rock). A correct hub pointing to hollow children is a doorway network with good posture. The fix is not the hub; it is filling each child with the MULS bar.
- Gate/law: browseable-hierarchy structure (pass) but destination-substance (fail at the children) - the exact split the doorway policy tests.

### BAD / penalty-risk

**B1. The thin destination behind a good hub** - `reliantplumbing.com/areas-we-serve/round-rock/` and its ~50 siblings (live, fetched 2026-07)
- What fails: a clean hub links to 50 near-identical children, each "[City] Plumbers" + template body + unattributed testimonials, city the only meaningful variable. This is Google's named doorway example ("browseable hierarchy" whose leaves are "substantially similar pages") realized.
- Why penalty-risk: 50 templated leaves read to SpamBrain as scaled content; the good hub structure does not save thin destinations. Watch GSC index coverage across the cohort ("Crawled - currently not indexed" spreading is the smoke detector).
- Gate: doorway + scaled-content-abuse; doorway smoke detector.

**B2. Pattern - the fabricated-address / subdomain SAB** (clearly-labeled real pattern; GBP-guideline and doorway violation)
- What fails: (a) renting a virtual office or UPS box per city to look local, or spinning up multiple GBP listings at fake addresses; (b) `city.yoursite.com` subdomains, one thin clone each, funneling to one central form.
- Why: (a) is a GBP-suspension trigger and a doorway signal; Google detects virtual-office addresses and can suspend all listings. (b) matches Google's doorway example ("multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page") almost verbatim. A true SAB shows service areas, not a fake pin, and never a rented address.
- Gate: NAP integrity (no fabricated/rented address) + doorway (structural).

## The one insight to carry into every plumbing page

**Pair a printed master-plumber license number with a measurable local-physics cause, and you win two games at once.** The license (`M-#####`, `RMP-#####`, `CSLB`, `PM######`) is an externally-verifiable, publicly-lookup-able human-accountability signal that satisfies the trust gate AND the external-verifiability gate by itself; plumbing is the rare vertical where every operator legally has one. The local condition (Sacramento's 16-18 grains-per-gallon hard water, Houston's gumbo clay shearing slab pipe, Minneapolis freeze-burst, Austin sediment shortening water-heater life) is the physical fact that makes the page fail the strip-the-city test on purpose. The winners (Roto-Rooter, Abacus, Huber, Scarpignato) lead with both; every penalty-risk page above omits one or both - it prints "licensed and insured" with no number, or lists cities with no physics. The license and the physics are not decoration. They are the moat, because a spun template can fake neither the number that resolves to a state board nor the water hardness that resolves to the local utility.
