# 05 - Competitive Teardown + E-E-A-T / Quality Bar

Territory: competitive teardown of AI-SEO-content products, the human (agency) bar, and the E-E-A-T / quality / penalty reality that defines the ceiling for a local-SEO writing system.

Research date: 2026-07-20 PKT. All claims cited to URLs fetched this session. Where a review site is the source, it is labelled as secondary; primary Google policy language is quoted from Google's own docs.

Our system in one line: no-API, Claude-Code-native local-SEO writing OS that refuses detector-evasion and builds pages from real, SME-sourced, verifiable local facts (doctrine Law 8). This file measures that positioning against the field.

---

## Part 1 - Competitive teardown (AI-SEO-content tools)

### The shape of the market

The entire commercial AI-SEO-writing category splits into two jobs:

1. **SERP-informed single-article drafting** (Koala, SEO.ai, Surfer AI, Jasper, Writesonic, RankMath Content AI). Input a keyword, the tool scrapes the current SERP, and emits one optimized draft.
2. **Programmatic bulk generation** (Byword, Cuppa). Input a CSV or a variable set, the tool emits hundreds-to-thousands of templated pages.

Not one of them is built around **verifiable, first-party local facts sourced from the business owner.** Every tool sources from the SERP (what already ranks) or from the model's parametric memory. That is the structural gap. They can only ever remix what is already public; they cannot manufacture Experience (the first E of E-E-A-T), because Experience by definition is not on the open web yet.

### Tool-by-tool

**Byword** - purpose-built programmatic SEO. CSV upload, batch processing, custom variables, thousands of articles. Pricing is per-article: ~$99/mo for 25 up to $999/mo for 1000 (secondary: Junia, eesel, Skywork reviews). Honest reviewer verdict: "the best tool for producing large batches of SEO-friendly content quickly, but every article still needs a human pass" and content "still reads like a first draft" (aboahreviews.com, secondary). This is the archetype of what our doctrine Law 8 warns against: scale without per-page value. Byword's strength (volume) is precisely the vector Google's scaled-content-abuse policy targets.
- Sources: https://www.junia.ai/blog/byword-ai-alternatives-in-2025 ; https://aboahreviews.com/byword-ai-review/ ; https://www.eesel.ai/blog/ai-bulk-content-generator

**Koala AI (KoalaWriter)** - the strongest single-article tool by consensus. Real-time SERP integration, real-time web research mode, Amazon affiliate linking. Word-count pricing ($9/mo 15K words up to $350/mo 1M+). Better quality than Byword because it grounds in live SERP data (fonzy.ai, withacta.com, secondary). But SERP-grounding is still second-hand: it reshuffles what competitors already published. No mechanism to inject a fact that only the business owner knows.
- Sources: https://www.fonzy.ai/compare/koala-writer-vs-byword ; https://withacta.com/compare/koala-vs-byword-vs-acta

**SEO.ai** - keyword research + real-time SERP analysis + benchmarking, 50+ languages. Positioned as an end-to-end SEO content platform. Same ceiling: SERP-derived, no first-party fact layer.
- Source: https://seo.ai/blog/koalawriter-alternatives

**Surfer AI** - the most accurate NLP content-scoring on the market for predicting ranking potential; best for agencies producing optimized content at scale. But: "limited local SEO optimization capabilities" and "all AI output has a generic tone regardless of your brand since there's no brand voice training" (baytechconsulting, get-ryze, secondary). Surfer optimizes toward a term-frequency target, which is exactly the "write to the SERP average" behavior that produces homogenized, me-too pages.
- Sources: https://www.baytechconsulting.com/blog/surfer-seo-an-analytical-review-2025 ; https://www.get-ryze.ai/blog/ai-content-optimization-vs-surfer-seo-honest-comparison

**Jasper** - strongest on brand-voice consistency (trains on your tone/style/terminology, 4.7/5 G2), weaker on optimization scoring. Jasper is the one competitor that takes voice seriously - a real threat to any "we do voice better" claim unless our voice layer is demonstrably deeper. But Jasper's brand voice is a style transfer over generic substance; it does not solve the fact-sourcing problem.
- Source: https://www.rivalflow.com/blog/surfer-seo-vs-jasper

**Writesonic** - attempts SEO optimization during drafting but "results require significant editing and manual optimization to compete" (darwinapps, secondary). A generalist; not a serious local contender.
- Source: https://www.blog.darwinapps.com/blog/writesonic-vs-jasper-2025-ai-writing-tools-compared

**RankMath Content AI** - no substantive independent 2025 review surfaced this session. It is a WordPress-plugin optimization assistant (keyword suggestions, structure), not a page builder. Treat as an in-editor optimizer, not a competitor to a full writing OS. (Could not verify current capabilities from a primary source; flag as unverified.)

**Cuppa AI** - the closest direct competitor: explicitly markets "AI-Powered Local SEO Pages" and "programmatic SEO" for hundreds of local service pages. Cites a roofing-company case (45% traffic lift in 4 months) - vendor-published, unverifiable, treat as marketing. Cuppa's own docs concede the tell: "don't just generate content in Cuppa and immediately publish it ... otherwise Google will not consider you for the top spots" and "not a one-click ranking button - its best results come when you pair it with solid keyword research, human editing, and real subject-matter expertise" (cuppa.ai / divramis, secondary). Translation: Cuppa knows the SME-expertise + editing layer is where the value is, but it offloads that labor onto the user. Our system **is** that layer, systematized.
- Sources: https://cuppa.ai/use-cases/ai-for-local-seo ; https://divramis.com/cuppa-ai-review-best-content-creation-tool/ ; https://originality.ai/blog/cuppa-ai-review

### The common failure pattern (the market bar)

Every tool converges on the same admission, in their own reviews and docs: **the AI draft is a first draft; a human must add expertise, local specificity, and editing before publishing.** None of them closes that loop. They sell speed and volume; they externalize quality. The market bar is therefore: "fast, SERP-optimized first drafts that a human must finish." That is the gap our system is designed to own - we systematize the finishing (the SME fact layer + voice + compliance gate) instead of externalizing it.

The recurring named weaknesses:
- Thin / generic / first-draft output (Byword, Writesonic, Surfer's generic tone).
- No real E-E-A-T - nothing sources first-hand experience; all are SERP-remix or parametric.
- No local specificity by construction - Surfer explicitly weak at local; the programmatic tools swap a city token into a template.
- Detector-evasion / "humanizer" gimmicks as a selling point across the wider category - which Law 8 identifies as a false signal (AI-detector score has near-zero correlation with rankings).

## Part 2 - The human bar (productized local-SEO content services)

To be "world's #1" the system must beat not just tools but the agencies clients would otherwise hire.

- UK local SEO runs ~£300-£1.5k/mo for a single location, +£195-£599 setup; multi-location/competitive £1.5k-£4k+/mo. Below ~£300/mo "you're usually paying for automation, not expertise" (semrush, seoprofy, secondary).
- A ~$3,000/mo engagement typically includes dedicated local landing pages, ongoing content, link building, review management, reporting (semrush, secondary).
- White-label reseller floor is ~$178/mo (agencyplatform) - this is the commoditized, automation-heavy tier our output must not resemble.
- Sources: https://www.semrush.com/blog/local-seo-pricing/ ; https://seoprofy.com/blog/local-seo-pricing/ ; https://www.agencyplatform.com/local-seo-services/

Human bar deliverable per local landing page (2026 consensus): unique H1 + meta, 300+ words original, local landmark references, embedded Google Map, JSON-LD LocalBusiness schema (arc4, secondary). Note how low this "human" bar actually is - "300+ words + a map + swapped landmarks" is barely above the doorway line. **Our per-page playbooks already exceed this.** The opportunity: make our output visibly, structurally richer than a $3k/mo agency page, at machine speed, with a compliance report no agency provides.
- Source: https://arc4.com/resources/local-seo-landing-pages/

## Part 3 - The E-E-A-T / quality bar (primary sources)

### Quality Rater Guidelines - the September 11, 2025 version is live

The QRG is a 182-page manual for Google's human raters. Two September 2025 changes matter: YMYL now explicitly includes **Government, Civics & Society** (elections, institutions), and **AI Overview rating examples** were added so raters score AI answers with the same rubric as featured snippets (seroundtable, seo-kreativ, secondary; primary doc at guidelines.raterhub.com).
- Primary: https://guidelines.raterhub.com/searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf
- Sources: https://www.seroundtable.com/google-search-quality-raters-guidelines-update-40092.html ; https://theguidex.com/google-quality-rater-guidelines-summary/

### The four letters, and which one is load-bearing

E-E-A-T = Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trust. **Trust is the foundation; the other three feed it.** The newest and most powerful signal is the first E, **Experience**: raters want proof you actually did the thing - original photos, your own test data, specific results with dates (groundingpage, seozoom, secondary summaries of the QRG).

This is the single most important finding for our system. **Experience cannot be scraped, remixed, or hallucinated.** It exists only in the business owner's head and camera roll until someone extracts it. Our SME interview is the only mechanism in the entire competitive set that manufactures Experience signal on purpose. That is the moat.

### What raters are told to reward vs. mark lowest

- Reward: original screenshots/photos, first-party data, specific dated results, clear authorship, evident why-this-was-made.
- **Lowest Quality: "Purely AI-generated content without human review and unique value."** The classification is about value and provenance-of-effort, not about whether a machine touched it (theguidex / imarkinfotech summaries of the QRG, secondary).
- YMYL pages get "very high Page Quality standards" because low-quality YMYL can harm health, finances, safety, or societal welfare. Many local service verticals brush YMYL (legal, medical, financial, home-safety/roofing/electrical) - so the bar is higher than generic blog content.

### Google's own people-first framing

"Creating Helpful, Reliable, People-First Content" - the who / how / why test: who created it, how (including any AI/automation disclosed), and why (to help people, not to game rankings).
- Primary: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content

## Part 4 - The penalty reality (what actually gets punished)

Law 8 is vindicated by the primary policy language. Google punishes **scaled low-value content and manipulative intent, not AI provenance.**

### Scaled content abuse (Google spam policy, quoted)

"Scaled content abuse is when many pages are generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users." Google explicitly lists "using generative AI tools or other similar tools to generate many pages without adding value for users" as a violation - **regardless of production method.** The abuse is intent + outcome (does it help the user), not the tool.
- Primary: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies

### Doorway abuse (quoted)

"Doorway abuse is when sites or pages are created to rank for specific, similar search queries. They lead users to intermediate pages that are not as useful as the final destination." The distinguishing factor is deceptive user flow + ranking manipulation, not content quality alone. This is the exact risk of programmatic city-page generation (Byword/Cuppa's core use case).
- Primary: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies

### Real case studies (not vendor marketing)

- **Regional HVAC company**: built hundreds of per-suburb pages; after the March 2024 Core Update lost rankings on 80%+ of those doorway pages, a 63% organic-traffic drop in 30 days (bigredseo / securityboulevard write-up, secondary but specific).
- **Site reputation abuse ("parasite SEO") manual actions, Nov 2024**: Google penalized Forbes, The Wall Street Journal, Time, and CNN. The Nov 19 2024 update clarified that "simply having third-party content isn't a violation unless explicitly published to exploit a site's rankings" - meaning first-party involvement or editorial oversight does **not** exempt you if the primary purpose is ranking manipulation. EU has since opened an investigation; Google is defending the policy.
- Sources: https://www.bigredseo.com/doorway-pages-vs-landing-pages/ ; https://securityboulevard.com/2025/11/the-programmatic-seo-paradox-why-your-fear-of-creating-thousands-of-pages-is-both-valid-and-obsolete/ ; https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-defends-parasite-seo-crackdown-as-eu-opens-investigation/560822/ ; https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/11/site-reputation-abuse

### The nuance that protects us

Programmatic scale is **not** inherently penalized. "Programmatic SEO at scale is fine when every generated page is backed by a rich, differentiated dataset that creates real value ... It crosses into doorway territory only when the templated pages are thin, near-identical, and offer nothing useful" (seomatic / guptadeepak, secondary). Our system can scale, provided each page carries a differentiated first-party dataset. The SME interview + real-local-facts research **is** that dataset. This is the reconciliation of "scale" and "penalty-proof."
- Sources: https://seomatic.ai/blog/programmatic-seo-best-practices ; https://guptadeepak.com/the-programmatic-seo-paradox-why-your-fear-of-creating-thousands-of-pages-is-both-valid-and-obsolete/

## Part 5 - AI answer-engine citation (the second ranking surface)

Our CLAUDE.md claims output should be "cited by AI answer engines." What that actually requires in 2026:

- AI engines cite sources they can **find, parse, and trust**: a clear entity (profile, NAP, schema), quotable content that directly answers the question, and corroborating trust signals across the web.
- Platform divergence is real: ChatGPT leans Wikipedia (~48% of sources) + third-party validation; Perplexity leans Reddit (~47%) + recency (content under 30 days gets ~3.2x citations); **Google AI Overviews pull heavily from pages that already rank in organic** - "if Google doesn't trust you, ChatGPT and AI Overviews mostly won't either."
- Only ~11% of domains are cited by both ChatGPT and Perplexity - one-size-fits-all does not work.
- The mechanism that wins: a **consensus signal** - the same entity/facts appearing consistently across your site, reviews, industry pubs, and social. Passage-block, self-contained, directly-answering H2s (which our passage-block protocol already mandates) are the quotable unit these engines extract.
- Sources: https://discoveredlabs.com/blog/chatgpt-claude-perplexity-and-google-ai-overviews-how-each-platform-cites-sources-differently ; https://locafy.com/blog/how-to-get-cited-by-ai ; https://www.leapd.ai/blog/ai-visibility/how-chatgpt-google-ai-overviews-and-perplexity-source-information-in-2026

Implication: our passage-block protocol is already the right primitive for AI citation. The gap is that citation also depends on **off-page consensus** (reviews, entity corroboration) that a page-writing system does not control. We should scope this honestly: we optimize the on-page quotable surface; we cannot manufacture the off-site consensus.

## Part 6 - Where we win, where we are exposed

### The single sharpest differentiation insight

**Experience (the first E of E-E-A-T) is the one ranking signal that cannot be scraped, remixed, or synthesized - it lives only in the business owner and must be extracted. The entire commercial field (Byword, Koala, SEO.ai, Surfer, Jasper, Cuppa) sources from the SERP or the model's memory, so none can manufacture it. Our SME interview is the only mechanism in the category that produces first-party Experience on purpose. That is not a feature; it is the moat. Everything else we do should be organized to widen it: better extraction of first-party facts, provable sourcing of every local specific, and a compliance gate that certifies the Experience is real, not claimed.**

Corollary: our competitors' greatest strength - volume - is precisely the vector Google penalizes (scaled content abuse, doorway abuse). We should market against volume, not toward it: fewer, deeper, fact-backed, certified pages.

### Differentiation to double down on (we already have the seed)
1. SME-interview fact extraction as the Experience engine (nobody else has this).
2. Retrieval-grounded, cite-every-fact drafting with a `sources.md` per page (nobody ships provenance).
3. A pass/fail compliance gate producing a `compliance-report.md` (no tool or agency delivers a per-page Google-policy certification).
4. Law 8 anti-evasion posture (we are correct; the market sells the opposite and admits it doesn't work).
5. Per-page-type deep playbooks (structurally richer than the "300 words + a map" human bar).

### Gaps competitors expose in us (candidly)
1. **Voice depth vs Jasper.** Jasper trains on the client's corpus. We have a voice layer + brand.yaml, but do we ingest and learn from a real corpus of the client's existing copy? If not, Jasper out-voices us on style consistency.
2. **SERP-shape intelligence vs Surfer/Koala.** They compute term-coverage and SERP structure targets. We research the SERP at write time, but we have no deterministic coverage scorer. A raters-aligned page can still under-cover the query space vs. the ranking set.
3. **Experience-proof assets.** Raters reward original photos and dated first-party data. We collect facts via SME interview, but do we systematically capture and place original images, dated results, and named-author bylines? This is the highest-ROI gap because it is exactly what the QRG's "Experience" rewards.
4. **Scale workflow.** Byword/Cuppa do CSV-to-hundreds. We are page-at-a-time. Our differentiation says "don't scale thin," but a multi-location legitimate client still needs many genuinely-differentiated pages. We lack a governed batch mode that enforces per-page uniqueness (a "differentiated-dataset gate") - which would let us scale *safely* where they scale *dangerously*.
5. **AI-citation off-page reality.** We optimize on-page; we have no view of the consensus signal (reviews, entity corroboration) that actually drives AI citations. Risk of over-claiming "cited by AI engines."

## Part 7 - Recommendations, mapped to categories

Format: WHAT - WHY - SOURCE - EFFORT (S/M/L). D = doubles down on differentiation; G = closes a competitor gap.

### [MD knowledge files]
- **`knowledge/foundations/experience-signals.md`** (G/D, effort M). The QRG rewards Experience above all - original photos, first-party data, dated specific results, named authorship. Codify what qualifies as an Experience signal and how to place it on each page type. This is the highest-leverage single addition. Source: QRG Sept 2025 (guidelines.raterhub.com; theguidex summary).
- **`knowledge/doctrine/penalty-casebook.md`** (D, effort S). Concrete penalty case studies (HVAC 80% doorway loss / 63% traffic drop; Forbes/WSJ/Time/CNN parasite penalties) with the quoted Google spam-policy definitions of scaled-content and doorway abuse. Turns Law 8 from assertion into evidence the writer and the client can see. Sources: developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies ; bigredseo ; searchenginejournal.
- **`knowledge/foundations/ai-citation-surface.md`** (G, effort S). Honest spec of how AI Overviews/ChatGPT/Perplexity cite, what we can control on-page (quotable passage blocks, entity/schema/NAP) vs. off-page consensus we cannot. Prevents over-claiming. Sources: discoveredlabs ; leapd ; locafy.
- **`knowledge/reference/competitor-teardown.md`** (D, effort S). This file's Part 1, distilled into a positioning reference the writer can cite when a client asks "why not just use Byword/Cuppa." Sources: as Part 1.

### [laws]
- **Law: "Differentiated-dataset test for every scaled page."** (G, effort S). Codify the primary-source reconciliation: many pages are fine only if each carries a rich, differentiated first-party dataset; near-identical templated pages are doorway abuse. Makes safe-scale an explicit, testable law. Source: Google spam policy + seomatic/guptadeepak.
- **Law: "Experience must be proven, not asserted."** (D, effort S). Elevate the existing "real E-E-A-T not claimed" hard rule to a named law with a pass test: every page must carry at least N first-party Experience artifacts (specific dated result, named person, original asset reference) or it fails the gate. Source: QRG.

### [frameworks]
- **SME Experience-Extraction framework** (D, effort M). Upgrade the SME interview from "facts" to a structured Experience harvest: dated project results, before/after specifics, named team members and credentials, original-photo checklist, hyper-local proof (jobs done on named streets/neighborhoods). This is the moat-widener. Source: QRG Experience signal.
- **Query-coverage framework** (G, effort M). A raters-aligned but SERP-aware method to check the draft covers the sub-questions the ranking set answers, without copying it - closes the Surfer/Koala coverage gap while staying value-first. Source: Koala/Surfer SERP-analysis behavior (fonzy, baytechconsulting).

### [Python scripts]
- **`scripts/experience_gate.py`** (D/G, effort M). Deterministic check that a draft contains the required Experience artifacts (named author present, >=1 dated specific, sources.md has >=1 SME-tagged fact, original-asset placeholders filled). Machine-enforces the new law. Source: QRG.
- **`scripts/duplication_gate.py`** (G, effort M). For multi-page/multi-location clients, compute pairwise similarity across generated pages and fail if any pair exceeds a near-duplicate threshold - the "differentiated-dataset test" made deterministic. This lets us scale where Byword/Cuppa get penalized. Source: doorway spam policy + HVAC case.
- **`scripts/quotability_lint.py`** (G, effort S). Lint each H2 passage block for self-contained, directly-answering, extractable form (question-shaped H2, answer in first sentence, standalone) to maximize AI-citation and featured-snippet capture. Source: AI-citation research + passage-block protocol.

### [skills]
- **`corpus-voice-ingest` skill** (G, effort M). Ingest a client's existing published copy and derive brand.yaml voice parameters + good/off-brand examples, so we match Jasper's corpus-trained voice consistency instead of a hand-written template. Source: Jasper brand-voice benchmark (rivalflow).
- **`serp-shape` skill** (G, effort S). At write time, structure the SERP research into a coverage map (entities, sub-questions, formats the ranking set uses) that feeds the query-coverage framework. Source: Koala/Surfer.

### [agents]
- **`experience-auditor` subagent** (D, effort S). A reviewer that reads a finished draft solely for Experience signal - flags every claim that is asserted-but-unproven and every place a first-party artifact should exist but doesn't. Complements the existing seo-content quality reviewer. Source: QRG Experience emphasis.
- **Extend existing `seo-content` reviewer** (G, effort S) to score against the competitor bar explicitly (is this deeper than a $3k/mo agency page and a Cuppa/Byword draft) and against the "lowest quality = purely AI without human review/unique value" QRG line. Source: QRG + agency-bar research.

### [examples]
- **Best/worst Experience teardowns** (D, effort M). For each of the 6 page types, add a paired example: a generic Byword/Cuppa-style templated page vs. our SME-fact-rich version, annotated to show exactly where Experience signal lives. Makes the doctrine tangible for the writer and sellable to clients. Source: competitor output descriptions + QRG.
- **A worked `sources.md` + `compliance-report.md`** (D, effort S) showing full provenance and a passed Google-policy certification - the artifact no competitor or agency ships, usable as a sales proof asset. Source: our output contract + spam policy.

### Priority ordering (highest leverage first)
1. `experience-signals.md` MD file + the "Experience must be proven" law + `experience_gate.py` - this trio operationalizes the moat.
2. SME Experience-Extraction framework - widens the moat at the source.
3. `duplication_gate.py` + differentiated-dataset law - lets us scale safely where competitors get penalized.
4. `penalty-casebook.md` - turns Law 8 into cited evidence.
5. `corpus-voice-ingest` skill - closes the one place (voice) a competitor (Jasper) genuinely beats us.
