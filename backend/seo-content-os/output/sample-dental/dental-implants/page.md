Clinically reviewed by Dr. Elena Marquez, DDS, Board-Certified Periodontist, at Sunbridge Dental. Last updated July 2026.

A dental implant is a small titanium post that takes the place of a missing tooth root. Once it fuses to your jawbone, it anchors a custom crown that looks and works like a natural tooth. At Sunbridge Dental, the same in-house team plans, places, and restores your implant across our Santa Monica, Pasadena, and Glendale offices, so your care stays with one record and one team from the first scan to the final tooth.

If you are missing a tooth, thinking about replacing a failing one, or tired of a denture that shifts when you eat, this page explains how implants work here, what the process is really like, what it costs, and who is and is not a good candidate. No hype. Just what you need to decide.

## What is a dental implant, and how does it work?

A dental implant replaces the root of a missing tooth with a titanium post, and it has three parts: the implant (the post placed in the bone), the abutment (the connector), and the crown (the visible tooth). Over three to six months the bone grows around the post in a process called osseointegration, which is what makes an implant stable enough to bite and chew normally.

Unlike a bridge, an implant does not rely on grinding down the healthy teeth next to the gap. And because it replaces the root, it helps slow the jawbone loss that tends to follow a missing tooth, a benefit a denture sitting on top of the gum cannot give you.

## Signs you may need a dental implant

You may be a candidate for an implant if any of these is true:

- You have a gap from a lost or extracted tooth and want a permanent replacement.
- A tooth is cracked or badly decayed and your dentist has said it cannot be saved.
- You wear a partial or full denture that slips, clicks, or makes eating and speaking harder than it should.
- You have an older bridge that has failed and you want to stop grinding down neighboring teeth.
- You have lost several teeth in one arch and want a fixed set of teeth instead of a removable plate.

An implant is not the only option, and it is not right for everyone. The honest way to know is a consultation and a 3D scan, which is where we start.

## The implant process at Sunbridge, step by step

Most single-tooth implant cases run 4 to 9 months from consult to final crown, and most of that is healing time, not chair time. Here are the 5 steps.

1. **Consultation and 3D scan.** We take a CBCT 3D scan so we can see your bone, nerves, and sinus in three dimensions and plan the exact implant position digitally before we place anything.
2. **Any groundwork first.** If the tooth is still there and failing, we remove it. If the bone is thin, we may place a bone graft or, in the upper back jaw, a sinus lift, then let it heal.
3. **Guided placement.** Dr. Marquez, our in-house periodontist, places the implant using the plan from your scan. This is usually about 60 to 90 minutes under local anesthesia, with sedation available if you want it. Most patients tell us it was easier than the toothache that brought them in.
4. **Healing (osseointegration).** The post integrates with your bone over roughly three to six months. You leave with a temporary tooth where it matters for appearance.
5. **The final tooth.** Dr. Chen, our prosthodontist, fits the abutment and a custom crown made to match your bite and your other teeth.

Because the surgeon who places the implant and the prosthodontist who restores it are in the same practice, there is no handing your file to an outside office, and no finger-pointing if something needs adjusting. One team owns the result.

## Types of implant treatment we provide

We provide 4 implant treatments, and the right one depends on how many teeth are missing and where.

- **Single implant.** One post, one crown, for one missing tooth.
- **Implant bridge.** Two or more implants supporting a fixed bridge across several missing teeth, without grinding down natural teeth.
- **All-on-4 full arch.** A full set of fixed teeth for an entire upper or lower jaw, supported by four to six implants. In many cases you leave the surgical visit with a temporary fixed set of teeth the same day; the final prosthesis follows after healing.
- **Implant-supported dentures.** Implants that snap and hold a denture in place, so it stops shifting while you eat and speak.

## The honest risks, and who should wait

Every surgical procedure carries real risk, and implants are no exception. The risks we discuss with every patient include infection, the implant failing to integrate with the bone, and, at some sites, involvement of a nerve or the sinus. Implant survival rates reported in the dental literature are high over ten years, but high is not the same as guaranteed, and two things lower the odds: smoking, and diabetes that is not well controlled.

We also stage or decline some cases on purpose. If you have active gum disease, we treat that first, because placing an implant into unhealthy gums sets it up to fail. Telling you to wait when waiting is right is part of doing this properly.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do dental implants hurt?**
The placement is done under local anesthesia, with sedation available, so you should not feel pain during it. Afterward, most patients manage the soreness with over-the-counter pain relief and describe it as milder than the tooth problem that brought them in. Everyone heals differently, and we tell you what to expect for your specific case.

**How long do dental implants last?**
The implant post is designed to be a long-term replacement, and survival rates in the dental research are high over many years. The crown on top can wear over time like any dental work and may need replacement down the road. Good home care and regular checkups are what protect the investment.

**Am I too old, or do I have too little bone, for implants?**
Age itself is rarely the barrier; health and bone are what matter. If your jawbone is thin, a bone graft or a sinus lift often makes implants possible. The only way to know your situation is the 3D scan at a consult.

**Can I get all my teeth replaced in one day?**
For a full arch, an All-on-4 approach can give you a fixed temporary set of teeth on the day of surgery in many cases. The final, permanent teeth are fitted after your implants have healed.

**Which of your offices should I go to?**
Start at whichever is closest: Santa Monica, Pasadena, or Glendale. For full-arch surgical cases we coordinate the visit with our periodontist's schedule across the offices, and your records follow you.
