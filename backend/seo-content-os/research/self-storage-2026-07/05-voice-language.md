## 1. Operator / manager voice profile

### 1.1 Who is talking
The operator voice splits into two registers, and our copy borrows from exactly one of them.

- **Back-of-house register** (Inside Self Storage, Self-Storage Talk, the revenue-management trade): blunt, unsentimental, tenant-vs-operator, money-and-liability first. This is the language of running the asset. We *read* it to sound like we know the business; we do not *write* in it on a public page.
- **Front-of-house register** (the manager at the counter, the owner's reply to a review, the GBP post): plain, direct, locally specific, mildly folksy, allergic to corporate polish. This is the register our operator-voice pages emulate.

### 1.2 Back-of-house tone (what to learn from, not copy)
The trade press and forums treat the tenant relationship as an asset to be optimized and, when it sours, a liability to be discharged. The vocabulary is transactional and slightly adversarial. Evidence from real Self-Storage Talk thread titles (*forum-title evidence*; bodies 403'd): "Tenant bidding on their own unit?", "Auctioned off customer wanting to rent again...", "Tenants Trying to Sell to You!", and the board literally named **"Tales From the Trenches"** ([selfstoragetalk.com](https://www.selfstoragetalk.com/forum/general-self-storage-forums/tales-from-the-trenches/10260-auctioned-off-customer-wanting-to-rent-again)). The day-to-day board covers "software, hardware, kiosks, electronic locks, websites, crime prevention, video surveillance, gate access, and alarm systems" ([selfstoragetalk.com](https://www.selfstoragetalk.com/)).

Revenue management is discussed in flat, clinical terms. Operators distinguish the **move-in rate** ("the average rate paid by a new customer," discounted online) from the **in-place / achieved rate** ("the net rental revenue collected per occupied square foot"), and use **ECRIs** (existing-customer rate increases) to close the gap: "about 70% of self-storage customers rent their units online at a discounted rate. The remaining 30% rent on site and pay the higher in-store rate," and "During the fourth quarter of 2024, Public Storage's average in-place rate was 74% higher than its average move-in rate" ([insideselfstorage.com](https://www.insideselfstorage.com/revenue-management/the-ecri-evolution-modern-self-storage-rate-management-and-its-impact-on-asset-revenue-value-and-underwriting)). This is the vocabulary of the people we write *for*; it never surfaces to the renter.

### 1.3 Front-of-house tone (what our operator-voice pages sound like)
Distilled from the front-of-house register the industry itself models:

- **Plain and declarative.** Short sentences. Facts, not adjectives. "Drive-up units, ground floor, 24-hour gate access." Not "premier storage solutions designed with you in mind."
- **Locally rooted.** Names the town, the road, the cross-street, the gate, the manager. The trade's own critique: generic slogans "shouldn't be copy-paste[d]," and "The self-storage marketing efforts that work brilliantly in one market can fail spectacularly in another" ([insideselfstorage.com](https://www.insideselfstorage.com/marketing/no-more-copy-paste-strategies-a-geo-specific-data-fueled-approach-to-self-storage-marketing)).
- **Operationally specific.** A good operator answers in unit sizes, gate hours, lock types, and dollar amounts because that is how they think. The move-in special, the admin fee, the month-to-month term are stated flatly.
- **Owner-present.** Reviews and posts read like a named human ("I'm Dave, I run the Route 9 location"), not a brand voice. The counter manager is the E-E-A-T asset (Law 16): they have watched what floods, what freezes, what gets broken into, which units a two-bedroom actually fits.
- **Unsentimental about money.** Comfortable stating price, late-fee timing, and lien consequences directly, because the business runs on collections. Honesty about fees reads as competence, not as a downer.

### 1.4 Operator sentence patterns (templates to draw on)
- Fact-stack, no connective tissue: "Ground-floor drive-up. 10x20. $145 a month. First month $1."
- Named-place anchor: "Our Belmont Ave lot sits behind the [landmark]; the gate opens at 6am."
- Manager-as-narrator: "I've cut more locks off 10x10s than I can count, so here's what actually fits one."
- Direct consequence, no euphemism: "Rent's due on the 1st. Late after the 5th. In lien at 30 days, and I don't want to get there any more than you do."
- Proof by specific: "Every unit on the south row is climate-controlled to 55-80F. I check the thermostats on my Monday walk."

### 1.5 The operator lexicon (terms an operator uses fluently)
`unit` (never "space solution"); `move-in` / `move-out` / `vacate`; `move-in special`; `street rate` / `move-in rate` vs `in-place (achieved) rate`; `ECRI`; `revenue management`; `occupancy` (physical, economic, square-footage); `lease-up`; `delinquent` / `in lien` / `under lien`; `lien letter`; `overlock` (second lock for non-payment); `cut the lock`; `auction`; `gate code`; `gate hours` vs `access hours` vs `office hours`; `drive-up`; `climate-controlled` vs `temperature-controlled`; `roll-up door`; `admin fee`; `tenant protection plan` vs `tenant insurance`; `unit mix`; `net rentable square feet`; `prorate`; `month-to-month`; `REIT`; `PMS`; `disc lock` / `cylinder lock`. Full definitions in section 3.

## 2. Renter voice profile

### 2.1 The renter's actual mindset
Renters are not shopping storage; they are solving a life event. The dominant trigger is a move ("moving long-distance, abroad, or downsizing"), then seasonal overflow and life transitions ([hellolanding.com](https://www.hellolanding.com/blog/the-beginner-s-guide-to-renting-a-storage-unit-for-a-move/)). They arrive anxious and time-pressured, and they do not know the vocabulary. They say "space" and "stuff," not "unit" and "net rentable square feet." Our job is to meet their words on the page and translate into the operator's terms without talking down.

### 2.2 The five questions every renter is really asking
Renters phrase their needs around five worries, in roughly this priority. Question phrasing below is verbatim from Bear River's pre-rent checklist ([brsutah.com](https://brsutah.com/questions-to-ask-before-renting-a-secure-self-storage-unit/)).

1. **What fits / what size do I need.** "What type of storage unit do I need?" This is the single highest-intent renter question. They think in rooms and truckloads, not square feet: a 5x5 holds "a large closet or small bedroom," a 5x10 suits "a studio apartment... queen-sized mattress set, 15-20 boxes, or a motorcycle," and a 10x10 holds "the contents of a one-bedroom apartment" (a 2-bedroom typically fits a 10x10, larger ones a 10x15) ([extraspace.com size guide](https://www.extraspace.com/self-storage/storage-unit-size-guide/); [purelystorage.com](https://purelystorage.com/blogs/storage-unit-size-guide)). *These room-to-size mappings are the industry-standard pattern, drawn from search summaries of the size-guide pages, not a single fetched authority; treat the exact box counts as typical, not universal.*
2. **How much / any hidden fees.** "Are there any hidden fees or long-term commitments?"; "When is rent due each month, and what fees apply if you pay late?" Renters have been burned by move-in-rate-vs-later-bill surprises and fee stacking; they now ask about the total up front.
3. **Is my stuff safe.** "Does the property have gated access with individual entry codes?"; "Are there security cameras monitoring the facility 24/7?" Security is emotional, not technical: they want to know it will not be stolen.
4. **When can I get in.** "When and how can you access your belongings?"; "Does the facility offer 24/7 access or limit entry to business hours?" This is where the gate-hours / access-hours / office-hours confusion lives; Bear River's own advice is to "always confirm the difference between access hours and office hours."
5. **Will my stuff survive.** "Is climate control available, and do I need it?"; "What temperature and humidity range do they maintain?" Plus "What items are not allowed in storage?"

Secondary but real: "Can I upgrade or downgrade my unit later?"; "Is tenant insurance required or included? What does the insurance cover: theft, fire, water damage, natural disasters?"; "What happens if I miss a payment?" ([brsutah.com](https://brsutah.com/questions-to-ask-before-renting-a-secure-self-storage-unit/)).

### 2.3 The renter's fear vocabulary (what a great page defuses)
Renters' worst-case scenarios are concrete and visceral, and they read exactly like this in the wild (verbatim from a compilation of Reddit accounts, [aol.com](https://www.aol.com/lifestyle/shocking-storage-unit-horror-stories-170013329.html)):
- Break-in then denied claim: "After the unit was broken into they attempted to file a claim with the storage unit company and were told the coverage did not apply."
- Fees then auction: "they added extra fees [of] $100... they said no" to a payment plan before auctioning items.
- Pests: "roach eggsacks attached to things... rat feces everywhere."
- Water: "went back to their storage unit to find it covered in an inch of water. Around $15,000 of amplifiers, speakers, guitars... were reportedly ruined," and "He said I was stuck out of luck."

The pattern: renters fear (a) theft, (b) water/mold/pests ruining irreplaceable things, (c) surprise fees, and (d) losing everything to an auction they did not see coming. First-time renters are warned that companies "are happy to charge extra and even auction off belongings if renters are late on rent" ([hellolanding.com](https://www.hellolanding.com/blog/the-beginner-s-guide-to-renting-a-storage-unit-for-a-move/)). A page that names how *this* facility prevents each of these, with a dated first-party detail (Law 16), converts. Vague reassurance ("your belongings are safe with us") does the opposite: it pattern-matches to the exact companies that let them down.

### 2.4 How renters phrase needs (map their words to ours)
| Renter says | Operator term (translate to, don't correct) |
|---|---|
| "space," "spot," "a place to put my stuff" | unit |
| "the size of a one-car garage" | 10x20 drive-up |
| "getting in after work" / "24-hour access" | access hours / gate hours |
| "temperature controlled," "AC unit," "won't get moldy" | climate-controlled (vs temperature-controlled) |
| "drive right up to it" | drive-up unit |
| "no long contract" | month-to-month, no deposit |
| "the deal," "first month free thing" | move-in special |
| "the extra fee they didn't tell me about" | admin fee, tenant protection plan |
| "they raised my rent for no reason" | ECRI (never say this to them; explain the increase plainly instead) |
| "will they steal my stuff / can someone break in" | gated access, individual door alarms, cameras, disc lock |

## 3. Shared trade vocabulary (the glossary the copy must never fumble)

Getting one of these wrong is an instant credibility tell to both an operator reviewing our work and a renter who has rented before. Definitions verbatim or near-verbatim from the sources cited.

**Unit** - the storage space itself; the atomic noun of the business. Never "space solution."

**Drive-up unit** - "A unit type easily accessible from the ground level. Tenants are able to directly drive up to the storage unit door for easy loading and unloading" ([openunit.com](https://www.openunit.com/glossary)).

**Climate control vs temperature control** - climate control "regulates both temperature and humidity"; temperature control "regulates temperature only" ([storagepug.com](https://www.storagepug.com/blog/self-storage-dictionary)). Storable: "temperature and humidity regulation in storage units" ([storable.com](https://www.storable.com/self-storage-glossary/)). Do not use them interchangeably; the humidity distinction is exactly what a worried renter is asking about.

**Roll-up door** - the overhead door on a drive-up unit (the garage-style door); contrast with swing/hinged doors on interior units. (Industry-standard term; appears across facility copy.)

**Occupancy** - three distinct measures, and mixing them is an operator tell:
- **Unit (physical) occupancy** - "The percentage of units rented at a facility" ([openunit.com](https://www.openunit.com/glossary)); "percentage of rented storage units compared to total available" ([storable.com](https://www.storable.com/self-storage-glossary/)).
- **Square-footage occupancy** - "percentage of total space being rented" ([storagepug.com](https://www.storagepug.com/blog/self-storage-dictionary)).
- **Economic occupancy** - "the percentage of rent that is being collected versus the maximum total possible rent" ([openunit.com](https://www.openunit.com/glossary)).

**Lease-up** - "period when a newly built or acquired facility is filling units" ([storable.com](https://www.storable.com/self-storage-glossary/)); "stabilization" is 75% physical or economic occupancy ([openunit.com](https://www.openunit.com/glossary)).

**Street rate / move-in rate vs in-place (achieved) rate** - move-in rate is "the average rate paid by a new customer" (discounted); in-place/achieved rate is "the net rental revenue collected per occupied square foot" ([insideselfstorage.com](https://www.insideselfstorage.com/revenue-management/the-ecri-evolution-modern-self-storage-rate-management-and-its-impact-on-asset-revenue-value-and-underwriting)).

**ECRI (existing-customer rate increase)** - raising rent on a current tenant toward market. "Self-storage leases are month-to-month, which gives operators the flexibility to frequently adjust a tenant's rent" ([insideselfstorage.com ECRI evolution](https://www.insideselfstorage.com/revenue-management/the-ecri-evolution-modern-self-storage-rate-management-and-its-impact-on-asset-revenue-value-and-underwriting)). Operator-side term only; it is the practice renters resent, so on a public page you explain the increase, you never wave the acronym.

**28-day billing** - billing every 28 days (13 cycles/year) rather than monthly, which quietly raises effective annual rent. *Term to know; the defining source (Slate) was unreachable this run, so treat as a pattern to watch for, not a sourced definition.*

**Admin / administrative fee** - "a one-time charge applied when a tenant signs a new lease... typically covers the cost of processing paperwork" ([storable.com](https://www.storable.com/self-storage-glossary/)). Commonly ~$29 at the big chains (see `04-conversion-cro.md`).

**Delinquent / in lien / under lien** - a delinquent account is one "with overdue rent or unpaid fees" ([storable.com](https://www.storable.com/self-storage-glossary/)); a defaulted unit is said to be "in lien" or "under lien" pending the auction process ([storagepug.com dictionary](https://www.storagepug.com/blog/self-storage-dictionary)).

**Lien** - "legal right an operator has to a tenant's stored property if rent goes unpaid" ([storable.com](https://www.storable.com/self-storage-glossary/)); the "right of lien" is "the legal right of the storage facility to hold or sell a tenant's belongings due to non-payment" ([explorage.com](https://explorage.com/blog/the-language-of-self-storage)).

**Lien letter** - "Legally required notice via certified mail informing tenants their unit is being placed under lien" ([storagepug.com dictionary](https://www.storagepug.com/blog/self-storage-dictionary)).

**Overlock** - "Applying a second lock when tenant hasn't paid rent" ([storagepug.com dictionary](https://www.storagepug.com/blog/self-storage-dictionary)); the tenant keeps their own lock but cannot open the door.

**Auction / storage auction** - selling a delinquent unit's contents to the public when rent goes unpaid; bidders buy "without examining items beforehand" ([storagepug.com dictionary](https://www.storagepug.com/blog/self-storage-dictionary); [explorage.com](https://explorage.com/blog/the-language-of-self-storage)).

**Gate code** - "a numeric or alphanumeric combination, used to gain access to a secured area through a gate or entry point" ([explorage.com](https://explorage.com/blog/the-language-of-self-storage)).

**Gate hours / access hours vs office hours** - access hours are "the specific time frame during which... tenants... are allowed to enter the premises and access their individual storage units"; office hours are when staff are present. "24hr access" is entry "at any time of the day or night" ([explorage.com](https://explorage.com/blog/the-language-of-self-storage)). Confusing the two is the #1 renter grievance vector; keep them distinct in copy.

**Disc lock vs cylinder lock** - disc locks are round with a shrouded shackle "specifically designed to resist bolt cutters"; cylinder locks are "flush-mounted" into the door, "drill-proof and impossible to pick." Many facilities "require tenants to use" disc locks ([moving.selfstorage.com](https://moving.selfstorage.com/best-locks-for-self-storage-units/); [thebetterwebmovement.com](https://thebetterwebmovement.com/storage-unit-security-disc-locks-vs-cylinder-locks/)). A page that specifies which lock the facility sells/requires reads as expert.

**Tenant protection plan vs tenant insurance** - insurance is "a policy that protects renters' belongings... against risks" ([storable.com](https://www.storable.com/self-storage-glossary/)); a protection plan is the operator-billed alternative. StoragePug's split: protection plans are "purchased by [the] operator; insurance purchased directly by [the] tenant" ([storagepug.com dictionary](https://www.storagepug.com/blog/self-storage-dictionary)). Coverage is effectively mandatory at the big chains; margins and disclosure traps are covered in `04-conversion-cro.md`.

**Reservation vs rental** - reservations are "commitments without payment"; rentals "involve actual money transfer" ([storagepug.com dictionary](https://www.storagepug.com/blog/self-storage-dictionary)). See `04-conversion-cro.md` for the CTA-choice consequences.

**Month-to-month** - the standard term; "no long-term commitment" is the renter-facing benefit. A genuine trust asset; state it near the CTA.

**Prorate** - "Charging tenants only for days actually using a unit" ([storagepug.com dictionary](https://www.storagepug.com/blog/self-storage-dictionary)).

**REIT** - "company that owns and operates income-producing real estate" ([storable.com](https://www.storable.com/self-storage-glossary/)); the big four (Public Storage, Extra Space, CubeSmart, and the U-Haul/Amerco storage arm) set the category's UX and pricing conventions. Independent operators define themselves against them.

**PMS (property management software)** - "software that runs self storage operations" ([storagepug.com dictionary](https://www.storagepug.com/blog/self-storage-dictionary)); the system live pricing/availability is pulled from.

**Unit mix** - the facility's blend of sizes and types (drive-up vs interior, climate vs standard). (Industry-standard term; operator plans lease-up around it.)

## 4. The self-storage cliche blocklist (BAN THESE)

This appends to `knowledge/voice/vocabulary-blocklist.md`. Every entry below is a phrase that (a) appears verbatim in the mass-produced self-storage slogan corpus, and (b) is empty: it names no size, no price, no gate hour, no local fact. If one "fits perfectly," the sentence has no real content and must be rewritten from a specific premise. The slogan examples cited are real, pulled verbatim from published self-storage slogan lists.

### Tier 1 - hard ban (zero tolerance)

**The "peace of mind" family** (the single most overused storage cliche). Real published examples: "Peace of Mind Storage," "Peace of Mind, Piece by Piece," "Unboxing Peace of Mind," "Beyond Storage, It's Peace of Mind," "Secure Storage, Peace of Mind!" ([soocial.com](https://www.soocial.com/storage-unit-slogans/); [grindsuccess.com](https://grindsuccess.com/storage-slogans/)). Ban:
- peace of mind (already Tier-1 in the universal list; reaffirmed and hard for storage)
- rest easy / rest assured / sleep easy knowing your stuff is safe
- worry-free storage, stress-free storage, all space no worry ("Stress-Free Space Solutions," "All Space, No Worry," "Paving Way for Worry-Free Storage" - [soocial.com](https://www.soocial.com/storage-unit-slogans/))

**The "safe and secure" family** (pattern-matches to the facilities that got renters robbed; see 2.3). Real examples: "Safe, Secure, Stored," "Safe Harbor for Your Belongings," "Safe Place for Safe Keepings," "Keeping Your Belongings Safe and Sound," "Where Security Meets Serenity" ([soocial.com](https://www.soocial.com/storage-unit-slogans/)). Ban:
- clean, safe and secure (and any 2-of-3 permutation: "safe and secure," "clean and secure")
- your belongings are safe with us / your stuff is safe and sound / safe and sound
- safe haven / safe harbor for your belongings
- where security meets serenity
- keeping your belongings safe (as a bare claim with no camera count, gate spec, or lock type)

**The "space solutions" family** (the generic-noun tell). Real examples: "Space Solution for Every Situation," "Stress-Free Space Solutions," "Spacious Solutions for Your Needs," "The Ultimate Space Solution," "Simplicity in Storage Solutions!" ([soocial.com](https://www.soocial.com/storage-unit-slogans/); [namefatso.com](https://namefatso.com/blog/self-storage-slogan-ideas)). Ban:
- space solutions / storage solutions / your storage solution / self-storage solutions
- space solution for every situation
- your space, your way
- room to breathe (as a tagline)
- more than just storage / not just a storage facility (the "more than just" anti-pattern, storage variant)

**The "made easy / we've got you covered" family.** Real examples: "From Home to Storage We've Got You Covered!" ([soocial.com](https://www.soocial.com/storage-unit-slogans/)). Ban:
- storage made easy / storage made simple / renting made easy
- we've got you covered / we've got the space you need
- whatever you need to store, we've got room for it (the bare version)
- storage you can trust / trust your belongings with us / where your belongings belong

**The "your belongings" possessive filler.** Real examples: "Trust Your Belongings With Us," "Where Your Belongings Belong," "Your Belongings' Best Friend" ([soocial.com](https://www.soocial.com/storage-unit-slogans/)). Ban the sentimental-object framing: "your treasured belongings," "your precious memories," "your prized possessions" as decoration. Name what actually gets stored (the mountain bike, the seasonal inventory, the estate furniture) instead.

**The bare superlatives** (already Tier-1 universally; storage-specific reaffirmation): "premier storage," "the ultimate space solution," "your #1 storage provider," "the area's most trusted storage."

### Tier 2 - context-banned (allowed only when specific and load-bearing)

Pass only when immediately backed by a real number, gate hour, size, price, or dated first-party fact.

- **secure / security** - banned as a bare adjective ("secure storage"); allowed as "gated access with individual door alarms and 14 cameras, recorded 24/7" (name the mechanism).
- **climate-controlled** - allowed only with the actual range and how it is verified ("held at 55-80F, checked on the Monday walk"), never as a bare feature-word decoration.
- **affordable / competitive rates / low prices** - banned bare; name the number or the range (universal list, reaffirmed).
- **convenient / convenient location** - banned bare; name the cross-street, the highway exit, the drive time.
- **state-of-the-art security / facility** - banned unless you name the actual system.
- **clean** - banned as a bare adjective triad member; allowed as a specific ("swept and pest-treated monthly," "no standing water on the drive after the last storm").
- **spacious / roomy** - banned as decoration; give the dimensions.
- **flexible** - allowed only tied to the real term ("true month-to-month, no deposit, prorated move-out").

### Tier 3 - structural anti-patterns (storage-specific)

- **The generic size-guide answer.** "The size you need depends on how much you have." Banned. Lead with the mapping: "A 10x10 holds a one-bedroom apartment: bed, sofa, dresser, and about 20 boxes. A 5x10 handles a studio." (Passage-block direct-answer rule.)
- **The vague security paragraph.** "Your security is our top priority." Banned. Replace with the audited spec: camera count, gate-hour window, lock requirement (disc vs cylinder), individual door alarms, on-site manager hours.
- **Reassurance that echoes the horror story.** Any sentence that would sound identical coming from the facility in section 2.3 that flooded a renter's $15,000 of gear. If a renter who was burned would read your line as the same empty promise, cut it and state the mechanism.
- **The "whether you're moving, downsizing, or decluttering" opener.** The storage variant of the banned "whether you're A or B" tricolon. Name the one primary use this page is for.
- **Fabricated urgency stacked on the move-in special.** "Limited units left, act now!" without a real, dated inventory count (Law 20, no-fabricated-urgency). If the unit count is real and live from the PMS, show it; otherwise cut.

### The reframe rule (for every banned line)
Reaching for a storage cliche means the sentence lacks a fact. The fix is upstream: pull the size, the gate hour, the camera count, the lock type, the price, the manager's name, the dated observation from the SME interview or `brand.yaml`. "Clean, safe and secure" becomes "Gated, 24-camera lot; the manager lives on-site and cuts the lights at 10pm; every unit takes a disc lock we sell at the counter for $12." Only this business can truthfully write the second version, which is the entire point (Law 16).

## 5. Handoff notes for the writer

- **Two registers, never cross them.** Read the back-of-house lexicon (ECRI, in-place rate, overlock, delinquency) to sound competent; write in the front-of-house register (plain, local, specific, owner-present). The acronyms operators love are exactly the words that make a renter feel handled.
- **Translate, don't correct.** Meet renters at "space" and "stuff," then anchor to "unit," "drive-up," "climate-controlled" without condescension.
- **Every reassurance carries a mechanism.** Because the renter's fears are concrete (theft, water, pests, fees, auction), every trust claim must name the specific defense, or it reads as the same empty promise that burned them.
- **The size question is the money passage.** "What fits in a 10x10" is the highest-intent renter query; answer it in rooms-and-boxes, not square feet, as a self-contained passage block.
- **Fee honesty is a voice asset.** Stating the admin fee, the required protection plan, the late-fee timeline, and true month-to-month up front reads as operator competence and pre-empts the #2 renter fear.
