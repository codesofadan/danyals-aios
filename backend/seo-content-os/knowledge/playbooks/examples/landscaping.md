# Example Library: Landscaping / Lawn-Care Vertical

Real, live-verified teardowns for the six local-SEO page types, tuned to the landscaping and lawn-care reality. Every GOOD and BAD example below was fetched live in July 2026 unless flagged. Where a page could not be fetched live (bot-block, 403) or where naming a single victim network would be fabrication, the entry describes the real, documented pattern and is labeled `LABELED PATTERN` or `SECONDARY`. This obeys the CLAUDE.md hard rule: cite real URLs, never fabricate a source.

Read this alongside the page-type playbooks (`service-city-page.md`, `location-page.md`, `service-area-page.md`, `homepage.md`, `about-team-page.md`, `service-page.md`) and Doctrine **Law 8** (humanize with real facts and specificity, never a paraphraser or a detector-evasion gate).

## The landscaping reality (what makes this vertical different)

Five facts reorder every page in this vertical. Hold them the way the service-city playbook holds the doorway rule:

1. **The project gallery is the moat, not a decoration.** For design/build, hardscape, and outdoor-living work the buyer is buying a look. Real, dated, geotagged photos of *this crew's* completed local jobs are the single hardest thing a template competitor can fake and the fastest trust tell. A landscaping homepage with no real portfolio has already lost, regardless of copy quality. Stock aspirational lawns are the reverse: an instant tell a rater and a competitor both catch with a reverse-image search.
2. **The service lines are distinct buyers with distinct intent.** Design/build (considered, high-ticket, portfolio-led), maintenance (recurring contract, reliability-led), hardscape (project, material and craftsmanship-led), irrigation (technical, water-and-code-led), lawn care/treatment (recurring, results-led). One service page per line, each described specifically. Do not blur them into a 20-item mega-list.
3. **Climate and plant-zone specificity is the un-fakeable local substance.** Freeze-thaw cracking of hardscape (Denver, Chicago), gumbo/clay soil movement (Houston, Georgia), serpentine coastal soil and salt spray (Marin), semi-arid xeriscape and monsoon timing (Phoenix), USDA hardiness zone, native species by name, municipal water restrictions. This is landscaping's equivalent of the plumber's "gumbo clay soil" line: it collapses the moment you swap the city, which is exactly the target.
4. **Seasonal intent is real and cyclical.** Spring cleanup, summer install, fall planting, winter snow/ice and dormant pruning. The page and the offer shift with the season. A branded, cadence-specific maintenance program beats "we offer lawn care."
5. **Service-area coverage is where the doorway hazard lives.** A landscaper serving 15 suburbs is one lazy template away from a doorway network. The honest move is one substantive city page naming real neighborhoods, or genuinely distinct pages tied to areas the crew actually books jobs in, never 15 find-and-replace clones.

## 1. Homepage (entity anchor + primary conversion)

### GOOD

**G1. Lifescape Colorado - `https://lifescapecolorado.com/` (verified live 2026).**
Why it wins: the H1 anchors the entity to place and category ("Landscape Architecture in Denver, CO for Luxury Outdoor Living"), establishes longevity that is externally checkable ("Since 1976"), and leads with a real six-project portfolio (named: Architectural Harmony, Evergreen Estate, Belcaro Gardens, Hillside Beauty, Perfection in The Pines, High Style in The Pines). Climate specificity is present and causal: "Colorado's unique climate," "semi-arid climate," xeriscaping tied to water conservation. Trust stack is verifiable: NALP Gold/Silver awards (2013-2025), ALCC ELITE awards, INC 5000, BBB accreditation. Clean single CTA ("Schedule a Consultation"). Gate: E-E-A-T (real portfolio + verifiable awards + founding year) and homepage-as-entity-anchor.

**G2. Grunder Landscaping Co. - `https://grunderlandscaping.com/` (verified live 2026).**
Why it wins: a real branded promise ("where service is always in season(R)"), founding proof ("Since 1984"), a quantified and itemized award record ("more than 50 local and national awards"), a working before/after project gallery ("browse all projects"), clearly separated service lines (residential, commercial, lawn care), named testimonials, and a live Google-review link. Serves Dayton and Cincinnati from a real staffed address (1900 Old Byers Road, Miamisburg, OH). Gate: passes the homepage trust-bar and real-photo-proof requirements; the gallery is the moat, present and real.

### BAD

**B1. The stock-hero, no-gallery template homepage - `LABELED PATTERN` (documented across landscaping web-design roundups, e.g. muffingroup.com/blog/landscaping-websites, contractorgorilla.com/blog/best-landscaping-websites).**
The common failure: a brand-voice headline naming neither service nor place ("Comfort, Reimagined," "Your Dream Yard Awaits"), a Getty stock lawn as the hero, generic value-prop triads ("quality, reliability, affordability"), and no real project gallery. Why it fails: in a vertical where the buyer is buying a look, a homepage with no real portfolio and stock imagery gives zero Experience proof. Fails the E-E-A-T gate and the real-photo gate. The same stock crew photo reused across a franchise's markets is caught by reverse image search.

**B2. Franchise homepage/landing with cross-market copy reuse - `https://uslawns.com/locations/az/phoenix/` (verified live 2026).**
This Phoenix page opens "Let our experienced Denver professionals..." - a live copy-paste error from another market that proves the page is a franchise template with the city string swapped. Even though it later names real local owners (Chris and Jack Hallenbeck) and genuine climate detail ("Valley of the Sun," monsoon timing), the leaked "Denver" is textbook scaled-content: the wrong token survived because a human never wrote the page for Phoenix. Fails the strip-the-city test at the seam. Use as the canonical live proof that template reuse leaves fingerprints.

## 2. Service page (one service, brand-wide)

### GOOD

**G1. Grunder Landscaping - Residential Landscape Maintenance - `https://grunderlandscaping.com/residential-services/landscape-maintenance/` (verified live 2026).**
Why it wins: a trademarked, ownable program name ("LandKeeping(TM)") instead of "we offer maintenance"; a specific, verifiable cadence ("dedicated team horticultural visits every two weeks," "April-November"); concrete per-visit scope (prune, weed, apply herbicides, edging, mulching, leaf cleanup); seasonal color rotations named by season (spring bulbs, summer annuals, fall plantings); snow removal for the off-season; and a real Experience signal ("Mike V... has been leading a maintenance crew... for nearly 30 years") plus background checks, drug testing, E-Verify. Gate: service specificity + Experience. Caveat for the builder: it still lacks maintenance-specific before/after galleries and testimonials, which would take it from strong to un-copyable.

**G2. Lifescape Colorado - Hardscapes - `https://lifescapecolorado.com/landscape-services/hardscapes/` (verified live 2026).**
Why it wins the local-substance test: it names real materials (Belgard pavers, Mirage porcelain tile, permeable pavers) and ties them causally to the local climate ("the freeze-thaw climate creating cracks in concrete," materials that "hold up to our harsh climate, not crack or separate"), plus permeable pavers for local groundwater. Portfolio links and BBB badge present. Gate: the freeze-thaw causal link is the hardscape equivalent of "gumbo clay soil." Caveat: process detail is thin and it omits fire features/outdoor kitchens/walls, so it reads more promotional than educational - a real "even a good page leaves depth on the table" teaching case.

### BAD

**B1. Xeriscaping service page, locally framed but generically executed - `https://512landscaping.com/services/xeriscaping/` (verified live 2026).**
Near-miss, not a penalty, and that is the lesson. It correctly frames the Austin climate ("Austin, TX, climate demands smart landscaping choices," "dry spells") and lists real service areas, but it repeats "native Texas plants" and "drought-tolerant plants" with **zero species named** (no Texas sage, no esperanza, no native grasses), omits Austin's actual water-restriction stages, never addresses the local clay-heavy alkaline soil it claims to prep, and shows no testimonials or project counts. Fails the specificity bar: locally grounded in structure, generic in execution. This is how most real landscaping service pages fail - not spam, just thin.

**B2. National-brand "service in [city]" boilerplate - `https://www.belgard.com/find-contractor/las-vegas-nv/` (verified live 2026).**
City-swappable sentences ("...or you need paver stones in Las Vegas, NV," "transform your Las Vegas backyard living area") with no desert-climate, UV, soil, or local-project substance. The page reads identically if "Las Vegas, NV" becomes "Denver, CO." Fails the strip-the-city doorway test. It is really a contractor-directory landing dressed as a service page.

**B3. The 20-service mega-list - `LABELED PATTERN`.** One page (or a homepage) listing every service at equal weight with a sentence each, no depth on any. Dilutes topical focus and gives no service its own rankable, extractable page. The playbook fix is one dedicated page per service line.

## 3. Service-in-city page (the money page, highest doorway risk)

Landscaping reality: genuine service-in-city pages are rare and usually done badly, because most landscapers are single-location design/build firms and the brands that do run them at scale (franchises, manufacturer directories) template them. So the strong examples here are the firms that earn the combo through real per-place substance, and the bad examples are the templated networks.

### GOOD

**G1. Cityscape Landscape - "Landscaping Chicago" - `https://www.cityscapelandscape.com/areas-served/chicago-landscaping` (verified live 2026).**
The landscaping-x-Chicago money page done right. H1: "Landscaping Chicago: Design, Build & Maintenance Experts Since 1984." Real local substance that fails the strip-the-city test on purpose: freeze-thaw tied to material selection, 27+ named Chicago neighborhoods (Bucktown, Lincoln Park, Lakeview and more), architecture-aware design language (bungalows, brownstones, lofts, rooftop constraints of urban density), a real West Wabansia Avenue address, 4.6-star Google rating, and a portfolio link. Gate: passes the Minimum Unique Local Substance bar (named anchors + local condition + verifiable proof). Caveat: the testimonial is generic and it lacks frost-date/soil-pH hard data.

**G2. Heritage Landscapes - Sausalito - `https://www.heritagelandscapes.net/neighborhoods/sausalito` (verified live 2026).**
Design/build tied to one coastal town with un-fakeable microclimate substance: "cool, moist, and windy environment," "significant amount of fog, especially in summer," soil "ranging from rocky serpentine to loamy deposits," "coastal winds and salt spray limit plant choices." Real portfolio proof resolving outside the page ("Sausalito" and "Sausalito II" projects, designed with Arterra Landscape Architects). Gate: the serpentine-soil and salt-spray sentences are the coastal equivalent of "gumbo clay soil." Caveat (and teaching point): the "Local Lifestyle/Community" sections drift generic (artists, laid-back, waterfront cafes could describe any coastal town), so it is a *borderline* pass - the environmental substance carries it, the lifestyle filler does not.

### BAD

**B1. Franchise combo template with a leaked city - `https://uslawns.com/locations/az/phoenix/` (verified live 2026).**
The "experienced Denver professionals" copy-paste error on a Phoenix page is the live smoking gun of a service-in-city template. Even a page with real owner names and real climate copy is scaled content if the build process swaps a token instead of writing the page. Fails the strip-the-city test at the seam.

**B2. Manufacturer directory city pages - `https://www.belgard.com/find-contractor/las-vegas-nv/` (verified live 2026).**
Belgard runs one of these per city (Las Vegas, Indianapolis, and dozens more). Each is city-swappable boilerplate funneling to "find a contractor." This matches Google's named doorway example ("pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page") in structure. Verified fails strip-the-city.

**B3. The AI-spun suburb network - `SECONDARY` / `LABELED PATTERN`.** A landscaper generating 8 services x 20 suburbs = 160 combo pages by injecting two tokens, each convincingly specific but none tied to a real booked job or verifiable record. The documented enforcement analogue: a regional HVAC company built hundreds of one-per-suburb pages with swapped locations; after the March 2024 core update over 80% lost rankings and the site saw a reported 63% organic drop in 30 days (upnorthmedia.co/blog/doorway-pages-seo; manningmarketing.com; secondary, verify). Fails scaled-content-abuse and the external-verifiability gate.

## 4. Location page (whole business in one city)

### GOOD

**G1. Cityscape Landscape - Chicago - `https://www.cityscapelandscape.com/areas-served/chicago-landscaping` (verified live 2026).**
Doubles as the model city location page: whole business, one city, 27+ named neighborhoods on one substantive page rather than 27 thin ones, founding year, rating, portfolio, real address, freeze-thaw and architecture specifics. Gate: location page carrying genuine per-city value, not a doorway.

**G2. Heritage Landscapes - Sausalito - `https://www.heritagelandscapes.net/neighborhoods/sausalito` (verified live 2026).**
A town-level page with real microclimate and soil substance plus resolvable portfolio projects. It is one node of a neighborhood network (San Anselmo, Mill Valley, Belvedere, Ross, Point Reyes) where the environmental sections are genuinely town-true, which is the right way to run a multi-town network. Gate: passes because the local substance is real and non-transferable, with the lifestyle-section caveat noted above.

### BAD

**B1. "Landscaping Services in [City] and Surrounding Areas" thin page - `https://www.turfconceptslandscaping.com/trenton-mi/` (real URL; `LABELED` - returned HTTP 403 to live fetch, so pattern described from its indexed title and the documented class).**
The title formula "Landscaping Services in Trenton, MI and Surrounding Areas" is the canonical thin-city pattern: city name plus "and surrounding areas" as the only variables, generic body underneath. This is exactly the shape RicketyRoo flags as location-page spam (ricketyroo.com/blog/location-page-spam/). If the body names no Trenton neighborhoods, no local soil/climate mechanism, and carries no city-tagged proof, it fails the strip-the-city test. Verify the live body before citing as a confirmed failure; the *pattern* is documented and real.

**B2. National franchise branch pages - `https://www.trugreen.com/local-lawn-care/` network (real, live; e.g. `.../north-carolina/durham-municipality`).**
TruGreen runs city/metro pages nationwide, each routing to the servicing branch (Durham is serviced via the Morrisville branch). The national-template body with a branch address swapped in is the scaled version of a location page. National link/entity authority lets the brand survive it; a single-operator who copies the structure inherits none of that and gets treated as doorway content. Structure is necessary, not sufficient. (Live fetch of the Durham page was bot-blocked 403; the network and routing pattern are confirmed via TruGreen's own local-lawn-care index and search.)

## 5. About / team page (the E-E-A-T and trust surface)

### GOOD

**G1. Grunder Landscaping - About - `https://grunderlandscaping.com/about-us/` (verified live 2026).**
Best-in-class landscaping E-E-A-T. A concrete, memorable origin ("Marty Grunder founded Grunder Landscaping Co. in 1984 with a $25 lawnmower he bought at a garage sale"), a named founder, an itemized and externally checkable award record ("more than 50 local and national awards," "Two-time recipient of the Better Business Bureau's Eclipse Integrity Award"), team credentials shown not claimed ("some holding horticulture degrees," background checks and drug tests), a mission, and local roots proven by action (2019 Memorial Day tornado cleanup response). Gate: real Experience and Expertise with specifics only this business has, exactly what CLAUDE.md demands over "family-owned since 1998."

**G2. Lifescape Colorado - Our Team - `https://lifescapecolorado.com/our-team/` (verified live 2026).**
Named humans with titles (LeAnn Ostheimer, President; Matt McNaul, CFO; Michael Hupf, Mountain Region President; named designers and branch managers), a real Denver address (455 South Platte River Dr., Denver, CO 80223), "decades of experience," and a "Lifescape Turns 50" longevity signal. Gate: named responsible humans (the un-fakeable trust anchor). **Real caveat (teaching case):** it lists *no certifications, degrees, or license numbers per person* and leans on "decades" and "award-winning" without the specifics. Even a strong page leaves E-E-A-T on the table when it names people but not their credentials. The fix is one certification or credential per named leader.

### BAD

**B1. "Family-owned since 1998" with no humans - `LABELED PATTERN`.** An about page that claims heritage but names no founder, shows a stock team photo, and lists no credentials. Per the CLAUDE.md hard rule, "family-owned since 1998 is worthless without the specifics that prove it." Fails the E-E-A-T gate: claimed, not shown.

**B2. The mission-statement fog page - `LABELED PATTERN`.** All aspiration ("our passion is creating beautiful outdoor spaces," "we believe in quality and integrity"), zero founder, zero named team, zero awards, zero local roots. Common on template landscaping sites. Reads like a machine because it is empty (Doctrine Law 8, voice notes): fails E-E-A-T and the anti-AI-tell voice gate.

## 6. Service-area page (coverage without doorway spam)

### GOOD

**G1. Georgia Outdoor Landscaping - Service Areas - `https://georgiaoutdoorlandscaping.com/service-areas/` (verified live 2026).**
The honest service-area move: five named counties (Jackson, Oconee, Clarke, Barrow, Hall), each with a dedicated paragraph of real substance rather than a bare link list. Local mechanism is causal and county-specific: "clay-heavy soil" and drainage, "Georgia's heat, rain, and changing soil conditions," and Lake Lanier's "long slopes, soil movement" for lakefront properties, backed by "20 years" of claimed local experience. Gate: low doorway risk because each area carries contextual value, not swapped-token boilerplate. Caveat: no per-county reviews or named projects yet, which is the next depth layer.

**G2. Cityscape Chicago's neighborhood coverage on one page - `https://www.cityscapelandscape.com/areas-served/chicago-landscaping` (verified live 2026).**
The single-page answer to breadth: 27+ named neighborhoods covered inside one substantive city page instead of 27 thin per-neighborhood pages. This is the doorway-safe pattern for a dense metro. Gate: usefulness and uniqueness over page count (Doctrine and the service-city doorway rule).

### BAD

**B1. Manufacturer/franchise "find a contractor in [city]" network - `https://www.belgard.com/find-contractor/las-vegas-nv/` (verified live 2026), and the `https://www.trugreen.com/local-lawn-care/` branch network (real, live).**
Dozens of near-identical city pages varying only by the location token, funneling to one central action. Matches Google's named doorway example almost verbatim ("multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page," developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies). Belgard Las Vegas verified fails strip-the-city; TruGreen branch pages were bot-blocked at fetch but the network is confirmed.

**B2. The suburb-swap clone network - `SECONDARY` / `LABELED PATTERN`.** A landscaper publishing one thin page per suburb it lists (not per suburb it books jobs in), each identical but for the city name. Documented enforcement: the reported HVAC suburb-swap case (80%+ of doorway pages lost rankings, 63% traffic drop in 30 days post-March-2024; secondary, verify) and the reported 42,000-page doorway manual action that required deleting the pages to recover (secondary, verify). The lesson every source draws: "if your only unique element is a city name, you are creating doorway pages." Fails doorway and scaled-content-abuse.

## Sharpest landscaping-specific insight

**The real project gallery is the one asset that simultaneously wins conversion, E-E-A-T, and the doorway defense - and it is the asset templated landscaping sites can never fake.** In plumbing or legal, the local moat is a mechanism you can describe in words (gumbo clay soil, a courthouse judge count). In landscaping the buyer is buying a *look*, so the moat is visual and photographic: dated, geotagged photos of *this* crew's completed jobs on *these* named streets. That single asset does three jobs at once. It proves first-hand Experience (the December-2022 E-E-A-T addition Google rewards). It is externally verifiable in a way an LLM cannot spin across 40 towns (the 2026 differentiation gate). And it is precisely what collapses the strip-the-city test for a doorway network, because a real Sausalito serpentine-hillside build or a Chicago rooftop install cannot be regenerated by swapping a token. Every winning example above leads with real portfolio proof (Lifescape's six named projects, Grunder's before/after gallery, Heritage's resolvable Sausalito I/II builds, Cityscape's neighborhood work); every failing one substitutes stock imagery, a leaked city string, or "and surrounding areas." So the operative rule for this vertical: **build the page around the real gallery first, then write the climate-and-neighborhood copy to caption it. If the crew has not booked and photographed a job in that town, do not build the page for that town yet.** That single discipline is what separates a converting local landscaping page from a deindexed doorway clone.
