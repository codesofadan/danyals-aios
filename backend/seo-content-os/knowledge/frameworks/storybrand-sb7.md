# StoryBrand SB7 (Donald Miller)

Canonical spec. Defined once here; playbooks reference this file.

## What it is
Seven-part narrative order: a **Character** (the customer) has a **Problem**, meets a **Guide** (the business), who gives them a **Plan** and a **Call to Action**, that helps them avoid **Failure** and reach **Success**. The load-bearing discipline: **the customer is the hero, the business is the guide.** SB7 2.0 (released 7 Jan 2025) revised the exercises; the seven elements are unchanged.

## When to use for local (emergency vs considered)
- **Best on homepage and about-page narrative order** for either intent, because those pages must orient a mixed audience in five seconds (the grunt test).
- **Considered work benefits most:** a remodel or planning buyer is choosing a partner for a journey, which is exactly what SB7 models.
- **Emergency work uses only the spine, compressed:** hero has a problem, you are the guide with a three-step plan, act now. Do not run the full seven-beat arc on a burst-pipe page; PAS is faster there.

## Local adaptation
- **Name the three problem layers:** external (broken water heater), internal (stressed, worried about being overcharged), philosophical (a homeowner should not be taken advantage of). The **internal problem** is where VoC language and Cialdini's Unity live.
- **Make the Plan explicit and risk-lowering.** A service is invisible, so a named three-step plan ("1. Call. 2. We arrive on time with upfront pricing. 3. We fix it, guaranteed.") converts the intangible into something the buyer can picture and trust.
- The **grunt test** is the entity-clarity and conversion test at once: a first-time visitor states what you do, who for, and the next action in five seconds.

## PASS test
- The page passes the **grunt test in five seconds** (what, for whom, next action).
- The business **never positions itself as the hero**; the customer is the hero, the business is the guide.
- A named **Plan** (2-4 explicit steps) is present and lowers perceived risk.

## Anti-pattern
- The business as hero ("we are the award-winning leader in...") instead of the guide.
- No explicit plan, leaving the intangible service unpictured.
- Running the full seven-beat story on an emergency page where the buyer wants the phone number, not a narrative.

## Evidence grade
**Craft consensus.** High explanatory power, no controlled-study proof of lift. Structural. Do not quote a lift figure to a client.
