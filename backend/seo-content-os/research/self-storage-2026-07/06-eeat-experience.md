# Self-Storage E-E-A-T and First-Party Experience Signals (US)

Research run: 2026-07-23 PKT. Market: United States.
Scope: what genuine, un-copyable first-party Experience proof looks like for a self-storage facility, the storage-specific Experience-marker taxonomy (real signal vs bare claim), the storage equivalent of plumbing's "license number" (the printable proof a facility can put on a page that a competitor or an LLM cannot fake), and the exact fields the SME interview must harvest. This file is the storage specialization of `knowledge/foundations/experience-signals.md` and resolves to the same gates (G1 first-hand specificity, G2 E-E-A-T, G10 source resolution, G11 schema/NAP) and to Law 16 (the moat).

Sourcing note: Extra Space Storage (`extraspace.com`) and the NYC.gov DCWP checklist page return HTTP 403 to automated fetches, as does the `nyc-business.nyc.gov` portal. Those points are corroborated via clean-fetching third parties (`intro.nyc`, industry press, insurer guides) and are labeled as such. Every number below traces to a page actually read this run, or is explicitly labeled a search-surfaced pattern I could not confirm at page level. Nothing here is invented.

## 1. Why Experience is the whole moat for storage specifically

Storage is a near-commodity local purchase: a 10x10 unit at Facility A holds the same boxes as a 10x10 at Facility B, and the winning pages are the ones showing live unit-level price and availability pulled from the PMS (see `04-conversion-cro.md`, section 1.1, citing Modern Storage Media: "Facilities with live inventory outperform those without"). When the product is that undifferentiated, the only durable ranking-and-trust edge is proof of the specific physical place and the specific operating practice, which is exactly the first E of E-E-A-T and exactly what a SERP-remix or parametric content tool structurally cannot produce.

Google's helpful-content self-assessment question is verbatim: "Does your content clearly demonstrate first-hand expertise and a depth of knowledge (for example, expertise that comes from having actually used a product or service, or **visiting a place**)?" (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content). For storage, "visiting a place" is not a metaphor. It is the gate camera count, the hallway that is actually climate-held at a real temperature, the manager who actually walks the aisles. A competitor can copy your amenity checklist in an afternoon. They cannot copy your 40 cameras, your dated hallway photo, your manager's certificate number, or your 5,420-day break-in-free gate log.

The governing test (Law 16, provability principle): **if the sentence stays true when pasted on the facility across town, it is asserted and it fails.** "Clean, safe, secure storage" survives the paste and is dead. "Over 40 HD cameras recording continuously across entry points, hallways, and parking lots" (a real spec Big Tex Storage publishes, https://www.bigtexstorage.com/the-big-tex-difference/security/) breaks on the paste and is proven.

## 2. The storage Experience-marker taxonomy (real signal vs bare claim)

Eight marker types, storage-specialized. For each: the bare claim that fails, the proven form that passes, the artifact that proves it, and where the SME/brand.yaml supplies it. Markers 1, 2, and 8 are the primary G1 (first-hand specificity) carriers; markers 3, 4, 5, and 6 are the primary G10 (no-fabricated-fact) carriers.

### Marker 1. Original photos of the actual facility (the cleanest un-fakeable artifact)

- **Bare claim (fails):** stock photos of a generic orange-door corridor, or a manufacturer's roll-up-door render.
- **Proven (passes):** the operator's own dated photos of *this* facility: the specific gate and keypad, the real hallway with the unit numbers visible, a drive-up row, the loading bay with the courtesy carts, the camera domes on the corners, the well-lit night aisle, the climate-unit corridor. Captioned with the real location.
- **Why it is the strongest storage artifact:** original media is the single hardest thing for a scaled operation to fake, because it requires having physically been at the place. It is the direct answer to "visiting a place." A geotagged photo of your keypad answers Who/How/Why in one asset. The Quality Rater Guidelines reward original photos and first-party media as the differentiating Experience signal (`knowledge/foundations/experience-signals.md`, marker 1).
- **The storage photo shot-list (what the SME must confirm exists on their phone):** entry gate + keypad; a hallway with real unit doors and numbers; a drive-up/exterior unit row; the loading area with carts/dollies; visible cameras; a well-lit aisle at night; the climate-controlled corridor; the on-site office; the manager on site. Every image on the page must resolve to a real first-party asset in `sources.md` with a caption tying it to this facility. Any stock or unattributed image is not Experience and fails.

### Marker 2. The security stack as concrete specs, not adjectives

This is the storage equivalent of a service trade's "street-level local detail" marker: the anti-commodity, anti-doorway proof that this is a real, specific, operated facility. "Secure storage" is asserted. The following are the printable specs that break on the paste. Every one is a real published spec, sourced in section 4 below:

- **Camera count + recording behavior:** not "video surveillance" but "over 40 HD cameras, continuous live recording, footage stored for review" (Big Tex, https://www.bigtexstorage.com/the-big-tex-difference/security/).
- **Individual door alarms:** each unit "equipped with its own personal door alarm," "individually monitored" (Big Tex). Distinct from a single perimeter alarm.
- **Gated keypad entry with unique per-tenant codes, logged on entry AND exit:** "unique key code both when entering and exiting" (Big Tex); "a gate that only opens with a unique code... personalized for each individual renter" (storage.com, https://www.storage.com/blog/self-storage-security-features/). The per-tenant code is what turns the gate into an audit log.
- **Perimeter fencing, motion-activated lighting, resident vs office-hours manager, disc locks:** all defined at storage.com (above).
- **The break-in-free counter and the gate log as living proof:** Capitola Self Storage publishes "5,420 days without security breach" and "4,366 satisfied customers" as on-page counters (https://capitolaselfstorage.com/). A dated, incrementing safety counter is a first-party operational fact a competitor cannot copy without lying.

### Marker 3. Climate specs as real numbers, with the honest caveat

- **Bare claim (fails):** "climate controlled units available."
- **Proven (passes):** the actual held range. Capitola states its indoor units hold "between 50 to 80 degrees fahrenheit and maintains a constant humidity level year-round" (https://capitolaselfstorage.com/). StoragePug's operator guidance gives the working spec as "between 50 F and 80 F" with humidity "45% or below" (https://www.storagepug.com/blog/climate-controlled-storage). A commonly cited range surfaced in search (Extra Space blog, Neighbor) is 55-80 F with 30-50% humidity; label this a pattern, not this facility's number, until the operator confirms it.
- **The honest-caveat lever (information gain):** "'climate controlled' is not a regulated or industry-standardized term" (StoragePug, above; corroborated by the storage.com/ Neighbor search results). True climate control means "an HVAC system AND dehumidifiers," distinct from temperature-only ("cooled/heated") or humidity-only. A page that states its *own* held range and explains that the term is unregulated is doing exactly what Google's helpful-content guidance rewards and what a template cannot: it demonstrates first-hand operating knowledge. The proof artifact is the facility's actual thermostat setpoint and dehumidifier spec, harvested from the SME.

### Marker 4. Live unit availability and real prices (proof the facility is operating)

- **Bare claim (fails):** "affordable units, call for pricing."
- **Proven (passes):** the actual per-size web rate and unit count from the PMS, dated. Live inventory is both the top conversion lever (`04-conversion-cro.md`, section 1.1) and an Experience signal: a real-time availability feed proves a real, occupied, operated facility in a way no scraped page can fake. The un-fakeable artifact is the PMS feed itself (Storable, StorageMart, StoragePug ecosystems). Where the client does not feed live inventory, show the honest current published web rate with a dated stamp, never an invented one (Law 20, no fabricated urgency/price).

### Marker 5. Access hours and gate hours as specific published facts

- **Bare claim (fails):** "convenient access."
- **Proven (passes):** the real access window, distinct from office hours. StorageMart states the distinction precisely: "Office hours are when the manager is onsite and available to assist you. Access hours are when you can access your storage unit" and "6 am to 10 pm is common, but access hours vary by location," with "some locations do offer 24-hours access" (https://www.storage-mart.com/self-storage/faqs/access-hours). Capitola publishes "Gate Access 6am to 10pm Everyday" plus its exact office hours (https://capitolaselfstorage.com/). The proof is the facility's actual gate schedule; the operator-grade page never conflates the two and never claims 24-hour access the gate does not grant.

### Marker 6. The named facility manager + the CSSM credential (the person, and the paper)

- **Bare claim (fails):** "our friendly team," "professional management."
- **Proven (passes):** the actual manager's name and one true detail, ideally with a credential. The self-storage credential that functions like a printable license is the **CSSM (Certified Self Storage Manager)** from the Self Storage Association: a "15-session course," pass quizzes at 70%+ and a final exam at 70%+, yields a "printable certificate," the trademarked "CSSM(c)" designation, renewed "every two years with 4 continuing education credits... by December 1" (https://www.selfstorage.org/Events-Education/Certified-Self-Storage-Manager-CSSM-Certification). It is genuinely distinctive: the industry average is "1.5 CSSMs per facility" (https://www.modernstoragemedia.com/news/moove-in-self-storage-has-most-cssms-in-the-industry). A named manager who is a CSSM, with the cert date, is a checkable Experience fact the facility across town usually cannot match. Proof artifact: the certificate itself + the person's name/photo, mapped to Person schema (G11).

### Marker 7. Years in operation, local ties, and community proof

- **Bare claim (fails):** "serving the community for years," "locally owned."
- **Proven (passes):** the founded year as a real number, "Owned and operated by locals," and a specific local artifact. Capitola shows "15 Years in Business" and an on-site "historical photo exhibit" of local history (https://capitolaselfstorage.com/) - a physical, un-copyable local tie. The proof is `client.founded_year` consistent with the business registration and GBP, plus a specific community fact (a named local partnership, a real neighborhood the facility serves, the local landmark it sits behind), harvested from the SME.

### Marker 8. Reviews with attribution and count (customer-voice Experience)

- **Bare claim (fails):** "highly rated," "trusted by thousands."
- **Proven (passes):** the real count and rating, on a named platform, quoted with a first name. Google reviews are the load-bearing off-page E-E-A-T signal for storage, and the numbers are large: per a SOCi study of nearly 5 million reviews cited by StoragePug, "every tenth of a star increases your conversion rate by 4.4%," "every 10 new reviews... improve the conversion of your Google profile by 2.8 percent," responding to all reviews vs none boosts conversion "16.4%," and top-three local-pack businesses earn "126% more traffic"; the average facility "generate[s] 5.8 new reviews a month" (https://www.storagepug.com/blog/google-reviews-worth). Search also surfaced a Local-Falcon-attributed figure of a ~194-review median for local-3-pack storage businesses and a 50+ reviews / 4.5-star fill-rate threshold; I could not confirm these at page level, so treat them as patterns. The proof is the live Google Business Profile count/rating recorded in `sources.md`; every quoted review must match what is publicly visible and respect the review-PII rule.

## 3. The storage "license number": the un-fakeable printable proof stack

Storage has no single universal state license the way plumbing has a contractor license number. "Unlike professions such as plumbing or electrical contracting, self storage businesses typically **do not require specialized certifications**" (https://www.wexfordins.com/post/do-i-need-a-license-or-certification-to-operate-a-self-storage-business). So the storage answer is not one number, it is a **stack** of printable, third-party-issued, verifiable artifacts. Rank them by how un-fakeable each is:

1. **The state Self-Service Storage insurance/agent license (the closest true analog).** A facility that sells tenant protection/insurance to renters (nearly all do; coverage is effectively mandatory per `04-conversion-cro.md`) needs a state-issued license with a real, verifiable license number. California: the "Self-Service Storage Agent" license under CA Insurance Code section 1758.75 authorizes a facility "to offer or sell hazard insurance coverage to renters," issues a license number, costs a $323 filing fee, and runs a two-year term (https://www.insurance.ca.gov/0200-industry/0050-renew-license/0200-requirements/self-service-storage/). Parallel self-service-storage licensing programs exist in Oregon (https://dfr.oregon.gov/business/licensing/insurance/license-types/pages/self-service-storage.aspx), Washington (https://www.insurance.wa.gov/producers-adjusters/licensing/get-licensed/self-service-storage), Massachusetts (https://www.mass.gov/doc/licensing-requirements-for-self-service-storage-agent-limited-lines-license/download), and Alabama (https://aldoi.gov/licensing/selfservicestoragereq.aspx). **This is the number to print** where the operator holds it: it is state-issued, publicly verifiable, and false use is a real liability, exactly the properties that make plumbing's ROC number a strong trust signal.

2. **Jurisdiction-specific facility licenses.** New York City now licenses storage directly: under Local Law 171 of 2025, DCWP began issuing a **Self-Storage Facility License** (a separate license per building), with fees reported around $590/$443 per period and a $295 annual fee (https://intro.nyc/local-laws/2025-171; DCWP checklist at https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/businesses/license-checklist-self-storage-facility.page, which 403s to automated fetch). Where a city licenses the facility, that license number is a printable proof.

3. **The CSSM credential of the named manager** (section 2, marker 6): a printable, trademarked, renewable certificate from the SSA. Not a government license, but third-party-issued, dated, and distinctive (1.5 per facility industry-wide).

4. **The operational un-fakeables** (no external issuer, but impossible to fake without lying): the live PMS availability feed, the per-tenant gate-access log, dated original facility photos and camera footage, and the incrementing break-in-free counter (Capitola's "5,420 days"). These are the storage-native equivalents of a tradesman's job-count-from-invoices: proof of real operation that a scraper cannot manufacture.

5. **Baseline registrations** (weakest as trust copy, still real): general business license, sales-tax registration, zoning/occupancy, and ADA-accessible-unit compliance ("5% of the first 200 units must be wheelchair accessible" and "2% of units beyond 200," per Wexford, above). These rarely go on the page as trust copy but confirm the entity is real.

**Operator takeaway:** the honest storage "license line" is usually a combination: the state storage-insurance license number where held, the manager's CSSM, the founded year and business registration, and the live-inventory feed. Never invent a license number to fill the slot; an empty slot is a missing-evidence flag, not a fabrication (Law 16, G10).

## 5. What the SME interview must harvest (the storage question set)

Map each answer to a marker above and to a `brand.yaml` field. If a field comes back empty, the marker does not exist yet: write around it or flag it, never invent it.

1. **Photos (marker 1).** "What real photos do you already have of *this* facility on your phone: the gate and keypad, a hallway with the unit numbers, a drive-up row, the loading bay and carts, the cameras, a well-lit night aisle, the climate corridor, you or your manager on site? List what exists; we caption each to this location." -> `eeat.media[]`.

2. **Security stack (marker 2).** "Give me the exact security spec: how many cameras and are they recording 24/7 and stored for how long? Does every unit have its own door alarm, or is it a perimeter alarm? Is the gate a unique per-tenant code logged on entry and exit? Perimeter fencing, motion lighting, resident or office-hours manager? Any break-in-free record you track?" -> body copy + `eeat.proof`.

3. **Climate (marker 3).** "What temperature range do your climate units actually hold, what is the humidity target, and is it HVAC-only or HVAC plus dehumidifiers? What is the honest difference between your 'climate controlled' and any 'cooled' or standard units?" -> `eeat.proof` / product specs.

4. **Availability and price (marker 4).** "Do you feed live unit availability and web rates from your PMS? If not, what are today's real published web rates by size, and when were they last updated?" -> live feed or dated rate table.

5. **Access hours (marker 5).** "What are the exact gate/access hours versus office hours? Any 24-hour access, and under what condition?" -> `hours` / `access_hours`.

6. **The manager and credentials (marker 6).** "Who is the actual facility manager, one true thing about them, and are they a CSSM or working toward it? What is the cert date? Any other certifications?" -> `eeat.team[]`, `schema.same_as`.

7. **The license stack (section 3).** "Do you hold a state self-service storage insurance/agent license, and what is the number? Any city facility license (e.g. NYC DCWP)? Founded year and business registration?" -> `eeat.credentials[]`, `client.founded_year`.

8. **Years and local ties (marker 7).** "What year did this location open, is it locally owned, and what is one specific local tie: a neighborhood you serve, a local partnership, a landmark you sit behind, a piece of local history on site?" -> `client.founded_year`, `eeat.differentiators[]`, body.

9. **Reviews (marker 8).** "What is your real Google review count and star rating right now, and is there a standout review with a first name and the unit type we can quote?" -> `eeat.reviews[]` (verify against live GBP).

10. **Operator judgment (border of Experience/Expertise).** "What is the mistake renters make choosing a unit that you have learned to steer them away from, and what do customers say is the real reason they picked you over the cheaper facility?" -> `eeat.differentiators[]`.

## 6. The asserted-vs-proven test (the gate the writer applies)

Every storage Experience claim must pass the paste test: re-point it to the facility across town; if it stays true, it is asserted and it is cut or replaced. Quick reference:

| Asserted (cut) | Proven (keep) |
|---|---|
| "Clean, safe, secure storage" | "Over 40 HD cameras recording 24/7; every unit individually alarmed" |
| "Climate controlled units available" | "Held at 50-80 F with dehumidification; 'climate controlled' is unregulated, here is what we actually control" |
| "Convenient access hours" | "Gate access 6am-10pm daily, per-tenant code logged on entry and exit" |
| "Affordable units" | "10x10 at $[live web rate], dated [date], from our PMS feed" |
| "Professionally managed" | "Managed by [name], CSSM certified [date], on site daily" |
| "Locally owned, serving the community for years" | "Opened [year], locally owned; local-history exhibit in the lobby" |
| "Highly rated by our customers" | "[X] Google reviews, [Y]-star average, [platform link]" |
| "Fully licensed and insured" | "CA Self-Service Storage Agent license #[number]; tenant protection filed under CA Ins. Code 1758.75" |

## 7. The anti-fabrication contract (storage edition)

No marker is invented at the keyboard. A camera count, a temperature range, a review count, a license number, a manager's cert, a founded year, a break-in-free counter: each traces to `brand.yaml`, a tagged SME answer, or the live GBP/PMS recorded in `sources.md`, or it does not appear. A fabricated local specific (an invented camera count, a made-up license number, a fictional break-in-free streak) is the fastest trust penalty and, for the state storage-insurance license, a real regulatory liability. Under thin proof, the correct behavior is a missing-evidence flag to the operator, never a plausible-sounding number.

The moat, restated for storage: fewer, deeper, artifact-backed pages built from the one facility's real cameras, real thermostat, real gate log, real manager, real license, and real reviews, which is precisely the axis on which every SERP-remix and parametric content tool structurally cannot compete, because none of them have been to the place.
