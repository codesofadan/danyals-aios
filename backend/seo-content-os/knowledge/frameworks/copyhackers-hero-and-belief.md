# Copyhackers - 5-Element Hero, Rule of One, Belief Sequencing (Joanna Wiebe)

Canonical spec. Defined once here; every page type uses a hero, so this lives once.

## What it is
- **Rule of One:** one reader, one dominant idea, one action. Every page and every section resolves to a single job.
- **5-element hero:** the fold must state, in five seconds, (1) the outcome, (2) who it is for, (3) one trust signal, (4) one action, and (5) do it without a second co-equal CTA. The headline is the outcome, not a category label.
- **Belief sequencing:** each block earns the belief the next block requires before it asks for more. This is the governing sequence a PAS or AIDA spine rides on top of.

## When to use for local (emergency vs considered)
- **The hero and Rule of One apply to every page, both intents.** They are structural, not intent-specific.
- **Emergency heroes** compress to outcome + one trust signal + click-to-call, high in the fold.
- **Considered heroes** can carry a picturable outcome and a differentiator subline, but still one action.

## Local adaptation
- **Headline is the service outcome in the buyer's words:** "Get a roof that lasts 30 years, installed in a day" beats "Roof Replacement Services."
- **One fold trust signal:** aggregate rating with a real count, years in business, licensed/insured, or a warranty figure. The number on the page must match the schema (`AggregateRating`).
- **Belief sequence for a service page:** answer for the ready buyer -> problem -> service/mechanism -> proof -> price -> objections -> risk reversal -> close. Each block clears the objection that would block the next.
- The head service term appears in the H1 for entity clarity; do not city-stuff it (that is the city spoke's job).

## PASS test
- A first-time reader states, in **five seconds**: what the service is, who it is for, one reason to trust it, and the single next action. Any of the four missing or ambiguous = FAIL.
- Exactly **one dominant idea and one primary action** per page (Rule of One).
- Each block earns the next block's required belief before asking for more.

## Anti-pattern
- A category-label H1 ("Professional Roofing Services") or a hero image with no message.
- **Two co-equal primary buttons** in the fold ("Call" and "Learn more" at equal weight) splitting intent.
- An unquantified fold claim ("the area's best roofers"); the trust signal buried below the fold.
- Asking for the sale before the belief that justifies it has been earned (out-of-order sequencing).

## Evidence grade
**Craft consensus**, from a practitioner (Copyhackers) whose method is VoC-grounded; no controlled study isolates the 5-element hero's lift for local pages. Structural. Do not quote a lift figure to a client.
