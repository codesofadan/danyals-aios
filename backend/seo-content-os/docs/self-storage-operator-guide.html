SEO-CONTENT-OS Self-Storage Operator Guide

The 5-minute version

How to run your storage content system

This system writes the web copy for your storage facilities - facility pages, unit-size pages, climate and drive-up pages, your homepage, your FAQ. You run it by typing a /command to Claude Code. This guide tells you which commands to run, and in what order, for whatever you are trying to do today.

You do not need to know how it works underneath. Find your situation below, run the steps in order, answer the questions it asks about your facility, and it produces finished, publish-ready pages that are built to rank and that stay on the right side of Google's rules and storage advertising law.

Two things are true of every page it writes. Nothing is made up - every camera count, price, temperature range, and gate hour comes from you, never invented. And nothing is a copy - each page is built from facts about your specific building, which is exactly what makes it rank when the national brands' generic pages do not.

First, once per facility: set up your profile

Before any page can be written, the system needs your facility's real facts. You do this one time per business. It is the most important step - the quality of everything downstream depends on it.

  1. /new-client
    Builds your brand profile. It will interview you: your address and phone, your unit sizes and prices, your real security (how many cameras, gate hours, individual alarms), your climate range, your move-in special and admin fee, your manager's name. Answer honestly - blanks are fine, guesses are not.
  2. Answer the SME interview it opens
    "SME" just means you, the expert on your facility. This is where your un-copyable advantage gets captured: the dated break-in-free record, the real held temperature, why a certain unit size is in demand in your town. Fill in what you can; it flags what is missing.

Done once, this profile feeds every command below. If a fact changes (a new price, a new camera), you update the profile, not each page.

Scenario A - I just need one page

You want a single page written now - say a climate-controlled storage page for one city, or a 10x10 unit page, or your About page.

  1. Make sure your profile exists
    If you have never run /new-client for this facility, do the setup above first. If you have, skip to step 2.
  2. Make sure this page is in your plan
    Every page must be an approved page in your plan (the topical map). If you have not built one yet, run /build-topical-map once (Scenario D). This is what stops the system from producing thin, look-alike pages that get penalized.
  3. Run the writer for that page type
    Match the page to its command from the reference table. Examples: a 10x10 page is /write-unit-size-page <facility> 10x10 <city>; a climate-in-a-city page is /write-service-city-page <facility> "climate controlled storage" <city>; your homepage is /write-homepage.
  4. Answer any questions it pauses to ask
    It stops to ask you for real specifics it does not have (a price, a local detail). Give real answers; it will not invent them for you.

You get: a finished page package - the copy, the meta title and description, the structured data, the internal links, a sources list, and a report showing every quality and compliance check passed.

If it refuses to write the page, that is the system working. It means the page would be a near-duplicate of another one, or you do not yet have a real fact that makes that page worth existing. It will tell you exactly what it needs.

Scenario B - Plan a few months of content

You do not want to write everything at once. You want a prioritized plan: what pages to publish, in what order, over the next few months.

  1. /new-client
    Set up the profile if you have not (skip if done).
  2. /build-topical-map
    Builds your page plan: it researches real demand in your markets, crosses your unit sizes, storage types, and cities into every page that could exist, then filters that down to the pages you actually have the evidence to win. It marks which to build first (your money pages) and which to hold.
  3. Review the plan it writes
    It saves a plan you can read: which pages are approved ("page"), which are held for later ("index-only"), and what fact each held page is waiting on. This is your roadmap.
  4. Build a brief for the next page when you are ready
    /brief <page type> <target> gives you the angle for one page before committing to write it - a cheap way to sanity-check the plan a page at a time.
  5. Work down the plan over the weeks
    Publish the money pages first (Scenario A, one at a time), then the trust pages. Re-run /build-topical-map whenever you add a new fact (a new price, a documented job) - it promotes the pages that just earned their place.

You get: a written, prioritized content roadmap tied to real evidence, so you are never guessing what to publish next - and never publishing a page that would hurt you.

Scenario C - Build the whole website

New facility, or a full rebuild. You want every page the site should have, built out.

  1. /new-client
    Profile + SME interview. For the whole site, be thorough here - the more real facts, the more pages qualify.
  2. /build-topical-map
    Produces the full approved page list, ordered. This is your build queue.
  3. Build the pages in the plan's order
    Start with the homepage (/write-homepage), then each facility (/write-location-page), then the money pages (/write-service-city-page, /write-unit-size-page), then trust pages (/write-about-page, /write-faq-page), then link assets (/write-local-asset for your size and cost guides). Run them one at a time.
  4. Add reviews and profile content
    Reviews move storage rankings more than almost anything on the page. Use /write-review-requests (how to ask, compliantly) and /write-review-responses (reply to what comes in). /write-gbp-posts writes your Google Business Profile posts.
  5. Check anything you are unsure about
    /qa <draft> runs the full quality and compliance check on any page and tells you pass or fail, with the reason.

You get: a complete, internally-linked site where every page carries real proof of your specific facilities - the thing the national chains structurally cannot copy.

Single facility? Your homepage is your facility page. The system knows this - it will build one deep homepage with your unit sizes as sections, not a pile of thin separate pages. That is correct, not a shortcut.

Scenario D - Build the page plan first

You just want the plan - which pages should exist - before writing anything. This is the input to Scenario A, B, or C.

  1. /new-client
    The plan is built from your real footprint, so the profile comes first.
  2. /build-topical-map
    Builds and saves the plan. It will not invent pages for cities you do not serve or sizes you do not stock.
  3. Read the coverage note at the bottom
    It tells you plainly what it is NOT doing for you - reviews, your Google Business Profile setup, proximity - because those matter as much as pages and are your operator work, not content.

You get: a saved, evidence-gated page plan you can hand to any of the writer commands whenever you are ready.

Scenario E - Pick up in the middle

You already have a plan, or some pages, and want to add to it, refresh it, or measure it - not start over.

Add one more page to an existing plan

Run the writer for that page type (Scenario A, step 3). It reads your existing plan and slots the new page in. No need to rebuild anything.

A page has gone stale or slipped

  1. /refresh <facility>
    Finds pages that have decayed, picks one, and updates it - only with a real change (it will not just stamp a new date on the same page, which Google ignores).

You want to know if it is working

  1. /report <facility>
    Turns your exported Google Search Console / Analytics / Business Profile numbers into a plain monthly scorecard with the one next action for the month.

You just want to check a page you have

  1. /qa <draft>
    Runs every quality and compliance gate and reports pass/fail with the specific fix.

What the system will never let you publish

These are automatic. You do not have to remember them - it refuses, and tells you why. They are why the pages are safe to publish.

  • Made-up facts. No invented camera count, temperature, price, or review. If you do not have it, it stays off the page.
  • Look-alike pages. No "swap the city name" pages across your facilities or sizes. That is the fastest way to a Google penalty, and it is blocked.
  • "Safe and secure" with nothing behind it. Storage security claims must name the real mechanism (the cameras, the gate code, the alarms). An empty "your belongings are safe" claim can even void your own lease protection in court, so it is out.
  • Calling your protection plan "insurance." If you sell an in-house protection plan, the page will never call it insurance - that is a regulated word and using it wrong has drawn fines.
  • "Climate controlled" that overpromises. It only pairs "climate controlled" with "keeps things dry / no mildew" if you actually control humidity, and it states your real temperature range.
  • Fake urgency. No "only 1 left" or countdown timers unless the number is real and live. Your "first month free" always shows the admin fee that is still due.
The one habit that makes this work: when it asks you a question, give it a real, specific answer - the actual gate hours, the actual price, the thing you have watched happen at your facility. The whole system is built to turn what only you know into pages your competitors cannot copy. Generic answers get generic pages; specific answers get pages that win.
