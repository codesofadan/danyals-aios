# Electrical Vertical: The Example Library

Real, live-verified examples for every local-SEO electrical page. Each entry is a live URL (verified 2026-07) or, where a live named victim cannot be responsibly cited, a documented real pattern labeled as such. Good examples name the gate they clear; bad examples name the gate they fail. Governing law throughout: `knowledge/doctrine/seo-system-doctrine.md` (especially **Law 8**: humanize with real facts, never with detector-evasion) and the six page-type playbooks.

## Why electrical is its own vertical (read before writing a line)

Electrical is **YMYL by physics, not by opinion.** A bad plumber floods a basement; a bad electrician burns the house down. The NFPA attributes tens of thousands of US home fires a year to electrical failure. That single fact reorders the whole page hierarchy:

- **Trust is the dominant E-E-A-T aspect** ("Of these aspects, trust is most important," Google's own guidance). On an electrical page, the un-fakeable trust anchor is the **master-electrician license number as a literal, lookup-able string** tied to a **named responsible human.** Every state runs a public license-verification database (Texas TDLR/TECL, Florida DBPR EC-numbers, Virginia DPOR, Maryland DLLR, etc.), so a printed license number is the strongest external-verifiability signal this vertical has. Printing it is free; competitors who fake it get caught on the first lookup.
- **Permit and inspection reality is local-proof gold.** Panel upgrades, service changes, EV-charger circuits, and rewires almost always require a pull-a-permit-and-pass-inspection step from the local Authority Having Jurisdiction (AHJ). "We pull the [City] permit and schedule the [City/County] inspection" is both a conversion de-risker and an externally verifiable local claim.
- **The money services are named and specific:** 100A-to-200A panel upgrades, whole-home rewiring (knob-and-tube / aluminum-branch-circuit remediation), EV-charger (Level 2, NEC 625 / load calc NEC 220.82), and standby generator installs. Federal Pacific Stab-Lok and Zinsco panels are the recognized fire-hazard failure brands: naming them is instant expertise signal.
- **Code is the shared language.** NEC citations (220.82 load calc, 625 EV, 210.12 AFCI, 406.4 tamper-resistant, 230 service) are the electrician's equivalent of a lawyer's case citation. Real depth cites the article; fake depth says "up to code."

## 1. Homepage (`/write-homepage`)

### GOOD

**G1. Malco Electric, Austin - `https://www.malcoelectric.com/austin-electrician`**
Why it wins: the H1 itself carries the trust proof ("Austin Electrician | Malco Electric | Licensed Since 1983"). It names the responsible human and his license as a verifiable string (Randy Mallory, Master Electrician, **Texas TECL #6328**, master since 1972), states three-generation family ownership, and anchors the brand to real Austin geography (Oak Hill, Circle C, Barton Creek, Tarrytown, Zilker, Hyde Park, West Lake Hills). It also frames a real local mechanism: "Many established Austin neighborhoods have homes built in the 1950s-1980s with outdated electrical systems." Clears the E-E-A-T "named credentialed human" gate and the external-verifiability gate (TECL # resolves in TDLR lookup).

**G2. Eau Gallie Electric, Melbourne FL - `https://eg-electric.com/`**
Why it wins: "Florida's #1 Electrician since 1948" (75+ years is the un-fakeable experience signal), a full stack of **real, lookup-able license numbers** (EC13003441, EC13007245, CGC1526728, plus PE74016 professional-engineer), service-disabled-veteran-owned as a differentiated trust angle, real address (162 S. Wickham Road), and honest financing ("$0 money down"). Clears the trust-before-benefit and external-verifiability gates. The license stack is stronger than any adjective.

### BAD / penalty-risk

**B1. National-franchise homepage with no verifiable local trust anchor - real pattern, live at `https://www.mistersparky.com/`**
Why it fails (for a single-location operator copying it): the corporate homepage says techs are "trained, licensed, insured, and local" but prints **no license number, no rating with count, no years, no named human.** "Licensed and insured" as a floating claim with no number is the single most common electrical trust-gate miss. A franchise survives this on national brand authority; an independent copying the pattern inherits none of it and fails the "named credentialed human / externally verifiable" E-E-A-T gate. Fix: print the master-electrician license number and a real named licensee above the fold.

**B2. Stock-hero, adjective-stack homepage - documented real pattern (labeled).**
The Getty smiling-electrician-in-a-headset hero, an H1 like "Powering Your Home, Powering Your Life," and a trust bar of "Quality. Integrity. Service." with zero number, zero license, zero name. Reverse-image search exposes the stock photo; the copy fails the strip-the-brand test (swap the logo and nothing changes). Fails Law 8 (empty voice, not humanized by real facts) and the E-E-A-T gate.

## 2. About / team page (`/write-about-page`)

### GOOD

**G1. Malco Electric about/story surface (same domain, `https://www.malcoelectric.com/austin-electrician`)**
Why it wins: the E-E-A-T surface is concrete and dated. Named founder (Randy Mallory), his credential and its start date (Master Electrician since 1972), the license number, three generations, and a founding year (1983) that is old enough to be checkable against BBB (A+ accredited). This is "shown, not claimed" experience. Clears the E-E-A-T Experience gate (Google added "Experience" in Dec 2022 to reward exactly this has-actually-done-it proof).

**G2. Eau Gallie Electric company page - `https://eg-electric.com/`**
Why it wins: 1948 founding, veteran-owner story, a named external recognition (Inc. Best Workplace 2026), and the license/PE stack that ties named humans to public records. The recognition is third-party and verifiable, not self-asserted. Clears the trust and external-verifiability gates.

### BAD / penalty-risk

**B1. "Family owned since [year]" with no specifics - documented real pattern (labeled).**
CLAUDE.md hard rule verbatim: "'Family-owned since 1998' is worthless without the specifics that prove it." An about page that asserts a founding year, "our team of certified professionals," and a values triad, but names no person, no license, no photo of the real crew, fails the E-E-A-T gate. Nothing on it resolves to an external record.

**B2. Manager-omitted about page - real, live at `https://www.mikeselectrical.com/`**
Mike's Electrical (Virginia Beach, since 1985) is a legitimate 40-year operator, but the site **does not display a master-electrician license number or any owner/manager name** on-page (verified live; 4.2 Google rating, A+ BBB). It is not spam, but it leaves its single strongest YMYL trust asset on the table and fails the "named credentialed responsible human + verifiable license string" bar this vertical demands. Use as the "solid business, self-inflicted trust gap" teaching case: the fix is one line of copy (the VA DPOR license number and the master's name).

## 3. Service page (`/write-service-page`)

### GOOD

**G1. Master-electrician-authored panel/EV service content - `https://evchargeright.com/blog/panel-upgrade-guide`**
Why it wins: the gold standard for electrical E-E-A-T depth. Authored by **Jason Walls, Master Electrician, EVITP Certified, KY Electrical License EE642643, IBEW Local 369.** It cites the actual code path (**NEC 220.82** load calculation), gives real amperage specifics (50A/60A circuits, 100A/200A service), names the recognized fire-hazard panel brands (**Federal Pacific, Zinsco**), and reframes the buyer's default assumption with a real result ("Running a load calculation showed they only needed a $500 circuit addition"). This is how a panel-upgrade or EV-charger service page proves expertise: named licensed author + code citations + honest load-calc math. Clears the E-E-A-T Expertise gate and is maximally AI-citable (self-contained, code-grounded passages).

**G2. Malco panel-upgrade / EV service descriptions (`https://www.malcoelectric.com/austin-electrician`)**
Why it wins: services are described specifically, not as an equal-weight mega-list: "panel upgrades (100A-200A conversions)," EV (Tesla Wall Connector / Level 2), whole-home rewiring tied to the 1950s-1980s housing stock. The specificity ("100A-200A conversion" not "panel services") is the difference between a real service page and boilerplate. Clears the dedicated-page-per-service and specificity gates.

### BAD / penalty-risk

**B1. Credential-free "informational" service post - real, live at `https://beaconelectricalservices.com/2025/07/09/is-your-electrical-panel-ready-for-an-ev-charger/`**
Why it fails the E-E-A-T bar: covers a genuine YMYL topic (panel readiness for EV) but has **no named author, no stated credential, and only generic "current safety codes / national safety standards" with zero NEC article citations and no load-calc math.** Compared head-to-head with G1 (Jason Walls, NEC 220.82), it demonstrates the exact gap: same topic, but no verifiable expertise. Fails the "named credentialed author on YMYL content" gate. Fix: attribute it to the licensed electrician and cite the real code.

**B2. Keyword-stuffed single service page - documented real pattern (labeled).**
A "Panel Upgrade" page whose body is "Looking for the best panel upgrade? Our panel upgrade experts provide panel upgrade services. For your panel upgrade, call our panel upgrade team." Says nothing to a human, no code, no amperage, no permit reality. Matches Google's named scaled-content-abuse example ("pages where the content makes little or no sense to a reader but contains search keywords"). Fails Law 8 and the scaled-content gate.

## 4. Service-in-city combo page (`/write-service-city-page`)

This is the money page and the highest doorway risk. See `service-city-page.md` for the full rule set.

### GOOD

**G1. Mister Sparky, Kansas City - Electrical Repair (`https://www.mistersparky.com/kansas-city/services/emergency-repairs/`)**
Why it wins (structurally): the H1 carries exact service + city ("Electrical Repair Service in Kansas City, MO"); it names 11 real service areas (Independence, Liberty, Blue Springs, Overland Park, Odessa, Buckner, etc.); it shows a rating with count and source (**4.7 across 2,239 reviews**), a two-year satisfaction guarantee and a real On-Time Guarantee ("if our electrician is late...your repair is absolutely free"), and 5 FAQs. Clears the hero, trust-bar, and conversion-path gates. **Caveat (teach this):** the local-proof block is thin (no KC-specific mechanism, no named local job), so it only survives on national brand + review authority. A single-location operator must add the real local-proof block (named neighborhoods + a KC-specific condition + a verifiable job/permit) to pass 5.4 without that authority.

**G2. Panel-upgrade-in-city pattern grounded in Malco's Austin model.**
Why it wins as the template: a "Panel Upgrade in [Austin neighborhood]" page built from Malco's real substance passes the strip-the-city test - the 1950s-1980s housing-stock mechanism, the named neighborhoods (Tarrytown, Hyde Park, Rollingwood), and the Austin permit/inspection path collapse the moment you swap the city. That collapse is the pass condition. Clears the strip-the-city test (5.3) and the Minimum Unique Local Substance bar (5.4). Pair the on-page master license (TECL #6328) for the external-verifiability gate.

### BAD / penalty-risk

**B1. Templated city page, near-empty local-proof block - real, live at `https://nicevillemistersparky.com/service-areas/panama-city-fl/`**
Why it fails: H1 "Electricians in Panama City, FL" but the business is physically in Niceville, and the body is generic fear-copy ("your heart racing," "feeling scared," "electrical emergencies happen anytime") with **zero named Panama City neighborhoods, landmarks, or local conditions** and no visible review text. Strip the city name and the page reads identically for any town. Fails the strip-the-city test (5.3) and the Minimum Unique Local Substance bar (5.4); the location mismatch also flirts with the deceptive-local-presence signal.

**B2. AI-spun combo network - documented 2026 pattern (labeled).**
An electrician generates "[service] in [city]" pages for 8 services x 30 towns = 240 pages in one AI pass, each convincingly specific but none tied to a real booked job, a real permit, or a lookup-able license. Passes a naive read, fails the **external-verifiability test.** This is the exact "using generative AI tools to generate many pages without adding value" example in the live scaled-content-abuse policy (now governing AI Overviews too, per the May 2026 update). Documented sibling penalty: the March 2024 core update dropped 80%+ of a home-services suburb-swap network's doorway pages with a reported 60%+ traffic loss in 30 days (secondary, verify).

## 5. City / location page (`/write-location-page`)

### GOOD

**G1. Mister Sparky, Kansas City location surface (`https://www.mistersparky.com/kansas-city/...`)**
Why it wins: a genuine per-city entity with a local number, a real named service-area list (11 towns), rating+count, and franchise-local licensing. It functions as a browseable destination, not an intermediate funnel. Clears the doorway "destination vs intermediate" test. Same brand-authority caveat as 4.G1: the independent must add real local substance.

**G2. Eau Gallie Electric, Melbourne FL (`https://eg-electric.com/`)**
Why it wins as a location anchor: real address (162 S. Wickham Road, Melbourne), the license stack, 75-year local history, and Central-Florida-specific service framing (generators + solar + storage, which is a genuine hurricane-coast local reality, not boilerplate). Clears the NAP-integrity and external-verifiability gates.

### BAD / penalty-risk

**B1. Keyword-stuffed "cities we serve" block - documented real pattern (labeled), matching RicketyRoo's named spam tell.**
A location page whose "local" content is a block listing 40 city names ("electrician in Springfield, electrician in Jacksonville, electrician in..."). Google's spam policy names this verbatim: "blocks of text that list cities and regions that a web page is trying to rank for" is a keyword-stuffing example. RicketyRoo's teardown (`https://ricketyroo.com/blog/location-page-spam/`) calls the general form: "swapping city names across boilerplate copy is duplication, not localization." Fails the keyword-stuffing and doorway gates.

**B2. Orphaned JS store-locator city page - documented real pattern (labeled).**
A city page reachable only through a JavaScript store-locator that renders no crawlable `<a href>`, "no internal links, no site context" (RicketyRoo). It shows "Crawled - currently not indexed" in GSC (the doorway smoke detector). Fails the internal-linking / not-orphaned gate. Fix: flat crawlable subfolder links from a real browseable hierarchy.

## 6. Service-area page (`/write-service-area-page`)

Service-area pages are for SABs (drive-to, no public walk-in office). Highest fabricated-address and doorway risk in the vertical.

### GOOD

**G1. Malco Electric neighborhood-level coverage (`https://www.malcoelectric.com/austin-electrician`)**
Why it wins: instead of a thin "surrounding areas" list, it names coverage at neighborhood and sub-market altitude (Oak Hill, Circle C, Barton Creek, Steiner Ranch, Bee Cave, West Lake Hills, Lakeway, plus Travis and Hays counties) and ties it to a real operational reality (older-home rewiring). Named-street-level coverage that maps to real jobs is what separates a service-area page from a doorway. Clears the honest-SAB-coverage and Minimum-Unique-Local-Substance gates.

**G2. Mister Sparky KC service-area list (`https://www.mistersparky.com/kansas-city/services/emergency-repairs/`)**
Why it wins as a coverage model: an explicit, real, dispatchable town list (Independence, Liberty, Blue Springs, Overland Park...) tied to one staffed hub and a local number, transparent about being a dispatch radius. Clears the SAB-transparency gate (no invented office in each town).

### BAD / penalty-risk

**B1. Fabricated / rented address to look local - documented real pattern (labeled).**
An SAB electrician spins up a "virtual office" or UPS-box address in each target town to appear as a storefront. This is both a GBP-suspension trigger and a doorway signal, and CLAUDE.md names it a hard rule: "Never invent or rent a street address to look more local." RicketyRoo's exact tell: "fakes an address or implies location deceptively." Fails NAP-integrity and doorway gates.

**B2. Subdomain-per-city SAB network - documented real pattern (labeled).**
`austin.electricianco.com`, `dallas.electricianco.com`, each a thin clone funneling to one central form. Matches Google's named doorway example ("multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page") almost verbatim. Structural doorway signal independent of copy quality. Documented penalty class: a 42,000-page doorway network took a manual action, ~96% traffic drop, 8-month recovery requiring deletion of the pages (secondary, verify). Fix: flat subfolders on one domain, one page per town the business actually books.

## Cross-cutting electrical gate reminders

- **Never self-mark your own reviews** with `Review`/`aggregateRating` JSON-LD to mint stars (renders nothing since 2019; the map-pack stars come from GBP). The one place the correct schema move is to add nothing.
- **License number = the vertical's best external-verifiability proof.** Print it; it resolves in a state board lookup. Never print insurance policy numbers or coverage limits (fraud/impersonation vector).
- **Permit + inspection line doubles as local-proof and conversion de-risk:** "We pull the [City] permit and schedule the [County] inspection."
- **Name the failure brands and the code** (Federal Pacific/Zinsco; NEC 220.82/625/210.12) to signal real expertise on YMYL service pages.
- **No detector-evasion, no humanizer chain, ever** (Law 8). An electrical page reads human because it is made of real license numbers, real neighborhoods, real code, and real permit reality, not because it was laundered through a paraphraser.
