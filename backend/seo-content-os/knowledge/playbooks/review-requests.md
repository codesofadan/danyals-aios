# Review Requests - Deep Playbook

**Artifact:** the copy a business uses to ask a real customer for a review - the post-job email, the SMS, the in-person script the crew or front desk says, and the short instructions / landing asset that gets the customer to the right review surface in one tap. Not a web page. A set of short, reusable, policy-compliant scripts in the owner's voice.

**Command:** `/write-review-requests`. Load order per `CLAUDE.md`: doctrine -> google-compliance-spine -> `foundations/review-content-strategy.md` -> this playbook -> voice -> client `brand.yaml`.

**The one governing fact.** Review *recency and velocity* are the review sub-lever that actually moves local rankings (Sterling Sky), and they are a lever this content system **cannot pull directly** - we cannot post a review. The only honest, compliant thing a writing system can do is make the *ask* effortless, well-timed, and sent to *everyone*, so the operator asks more and sooner. Every word of this playbook serves that, inside the hard boundary of Google's and the FTC's review policies. The moment a request funnel starts filtering by sentiment or dangling a reward, it stops being growth and becomes a policy violation that can get the profile penalized.

**Reading contract.** No gating, no incentivizing, no sentiment-filtering, ever - these are cited policy rules, not preferences (section 2). No fabricated urgency. Owner voice. This is Law 8 and Law 20 territory. Figures below are directional priors from the sources named inline; re-verify before quoting to a client.

---

## 1. Why the ask is worth writing well

- **Recency is the ranking lever.** Sterling Sky's near-me study: recent review volume beats total; an 18-day pause dropped a client that had been posting 60+/month (https://www.sterlingsky.ca/what-gets-you-ranking-for-near-me-2025/, fetched 2026-07-20 PKT). Review recency jumped into Darren Shaw's personal top-5 for 2026 (Whitespark, via https://www.soci.ai/blog/local-memo-local-ranking-factors-of-2026-have-arrived/, secondary/directional). A steady cadence of fresh, text-carrying reviews is worth more than a big stale total.
- **There is a real conversion base to work.** BrightLocal: 45% of consumers left a Google review in the past year (https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/). People will review when asked well and asked easily. The bottleneck is usually that the business does not ask, or asks badly.
- **The friction is the enemy, not the willingness.** The single biggest lift is removing steps: a direct link to the review surface, a pre-framed reason, sent at the moment satisfaction is highest (right after the job is done well).

What we can move: the friction and the frequency. What we cannot move: whether the customer actually writes it, and what they say. The honest client framing: "We make asking effortless and compliant; the reviews themselves are earned by your work."

## 2. The compliance boundary (cited, non-negotiable)

This is the section that keeps the client's profile safe. Every request asset obeys all of it.

- **No review gating / no sentiment filtering.** Do not write, and do not help build, any flow that first asks "how was your experience?" and routes only happy customers to the public review while diverting unhappy ones to a private form. Google's review policy prohibits "discouraging or prohibiting negative reviews or selectively soliciting positive reviews from customers." Every asset here asks *all* customers the same way and links straight to the public review surface. Source: Google Business Profile prohibited and restricted content / review policies, https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091 and https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy (verify live before quoting to a client).
- **No incentivizing.** No discount, gift card, entry into a draw, cash, free service, or any reward offered in exchange for a review. Google prohibits review incentives. The FTC's final Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (16 CFR Part 465, effective October 21 2024) makes buying reviews, and providing compensation contingent on the sentiment of a review, a violation carrying civil-penalty exposure. Source: FTC final rule, https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/rule-use-consumer-reviews-testimonials (verify live).
- **Ask everyone, not just the ones you think are happy.** Selecting who to ask *based on predicted sentiment* is gating. The compliant selection is by *job completed*, not by predicted rating.
- **No fake, no employee, no family, no self-reviews** solicited.
- **Disclose nothing false.** The ask does not imply the review is required, monitored for content, or tied to service quality going forward.

The one compliant lever, restated: **ask everyone, ask soon, make it one tap, and offer nothing but ease.**

## 3. The timing and channel map

The single largest variable in review conversion is *when* you ask. Ask at peak satisfaction, which is immediately after the value is delivered and confirmed.

| Channel | Best moment | Why | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **In-person / verbal** | At job completion, when the customer says they are happy | Highest-intent moment; a warm human ask converts best | Never scripted to sound canned; a real sentence the crew/front desk says |
| **SMS** | Same day, within a few hours of completion | Highest open + tap rate; the direct link is one tap from the phone | Requires the customer opted in to texts; keep it short |
| **Email** | Same day to 48 hours after | Room for a warmer frame and the link; good for higher-ticket jobs | Subject line does the work; one clear button |
| **Printed / QR (leave-behind, invoice, receipt)** | Handed over at completion | Works where no phone/email captured | QR/link goes straight to the review surface |

Speed matters: the further from the moment of satisfaction, the lower the conversion. A same-day ask beats a next-week ask.

## 4. The copy patterns (owner voice, direct link, zero friction)

Every script below pulls voice from `brand.yaml`. These are patterns, not fill-ins to ship verbatim; the writer localizes them to the real business, the real service, and the real review link.

### 4.1 In-person / verbal script (the highest-converting ask)

Keep it short, warm, specific, and give the path:
> "Really glad we got that sorted for you. If you've got 30 seconds, it would genuinely help a small local business like us if you left a quick review - I'll text you the link right now so it's one tap. No pressure at all."

Notes: names no incentive, asks everyone the same way, makes the path immediate ("I'll text you the link"), and is honest about why (helps a local business). The "no pressure" keeps it clean.

### 4.2 SMS

Under ~160 characters, one link, owner voice, no incentive:
> "Hi [First name], thanks for having us out today. If you were happy with the work, a quick Google review would mean a lot: [direct review link]. - [Owner/first name], [Business]"

Rules: only to customers who opted in to SMS; the link is the direct "write a review" URL for the GBP (the operator supplies the real place-ID link); no reward mentioned; "if you were happy" is a genuine framing, not a gate (it does not divert unhappy customers anywhere - they simply may not click, which is their choice, and they retain the same public link).

Note on "if you were happy": this phrasing is acceptable because it does not *route* anyone. It is not a gate. It becomes gating only if unhappy customers are sent to a different, private destination. Keep the same public link for everyone.

### 4.3 Email

Short, one button, warm subject:
- **Subject:** "Thanks from [Business], [First name]" or "How did we do, [First name]?"
- **Body:**
> "Hi [First name],
>
> Thanks for trusting us with [the real job, e.g. your water heater install] this week. We're a local team and reviews are how people find us, so if you've got a minute, we'd really appreciate a quick review of your experience.
>
> [Leave a review button -> direct link]
>
> Either way, thank you - we're here if you ever need us again.
>
> [Owner first name]
> [Business], [phone]"

Rules: one primary action, the real job referenced (grounds it, no fabrication), no incentive, easy out ("either way, thank you"). NAP-consistent signature.

### 4.4 Printed leave-behind / QR

One line plus a QR to the direct link:
> "Happy with the work? A quick review helps our small local team more than you'd think. Scan to leave one - thank you. [QR -> direct review link]"

### 4.5 The review-instructions / landing asset

A tiny asset (a short page section or a printed card) that removes the "where do I even go" friction: one line of why, the single button/QR, and a two-step instruction ("Tap the link, tap the stars, add a sentence"). No sentiment pre-screen. Same destination for everyone. If built as an on-site page section, it obeys the page playbooks and never gates.

## 5. Voice and craft notes

- **Sound like the owner, not a CRM autoresponder.** Pull `brand.yaml.voice`; contractions, warmth, brevity. Kill "We value your business and would appreciate it if you would take a moment to provide feedback regarding your recent experience."
- **Reference the real job** when known (from the SME/CRM context the operator supplies) - it grounds the ask and lifts response. Never fabricate the job detail.
- **One ask, one link, one tap.** Every extra step costs conversions. No login walls, no multi-question survey before the review.
- **No fabricated urgency.** No "review in the next 24 hours" pressure, no fake deadline (Law 20).
- **No em dash** (U+2014); the hook blocks it.
- **Segment by channel opt-in.** SMS only to opted-in numbers; respect the customer's contact preferences and local anti-spam law (the operator owns consent; the writer flags it).

---

## 6. Vertical overlays

- **Medical / dental (HIPAA):** the ask itself is fine, but never reference a diagnosis or treatment in the request; keep it to "your visit." Confidentiality first.
- **Legal (bar rules):** review solicitation is permitted in most jurisdictions but some bar rules restrict testimonial solicitation; flag for the firm's compliance review, and never imply a review affects the matter.
- **Financial:** no reference to the customer's accounts or situation in the ask.
- **Home services (core lane):** most latitude; reference the real job freely, still no incentive.

## 7. Output contract

`/write-review-requests` emits to `output/<client>/review-requests/`:

- `scripts.md` - the in-person script, SMS, email (subject + body), and printed/QR copy, all in the client's voice with the direct-link placeholder marked for the operator to insert the real GBP review URL.
- `landing.md` - the review-instructions asset copy (and, if on-site, the section spec).
- `compliance-note.md` - a short confirmation that every asset asks everyone, gates no one, incentivizes nothing, references the cited policies, and lists the operator responsibilities (real review link, SMS/email consent, cadence).

A set is not done until the compliance note confirms no gating and no incentive, and every asset carries the direct-link placeholder rather than an invented URL.
