# Example Library: Personal-Injury Law (YMYL, highest trust bar)

Real, live-verified teardowns for the personal-injury-law vertical, one set per page type. Personal injury is the hardest local-SEO vertical on the internet: it is Your-Money-or-Your-Life (YMYL) at maximum altitude (a stranger is deciding who handles the biggest legal event of their life), it is the single most competitive and most spammed local niche (highest cost-per-click in all of legal), and it is governed by state-bar advertising rules on top of Google's spam policies. So a PI page must clear three gates at once: Google's doorway and scaled-content policy, Google's YMYL trust bar, and the state bar's advertising rules.

Governing law: `knowledge/doctrine/seo-system-doctrine.md` (especially Law 8: humanize with real facts, never with detector-evasion) and `knowledge/doctrine/google-compliance-spine.md`. This file adds the legal-advertising compliance layer that is specific to this vertical.

**Verification standard.** Every URL below was checked live in July 2026 and the specific facts quoted were read off the page. Where a bad pattern is a documented industry case study rather than a page I will name-and-shame, it is labeled `(reported case study, secondary, verify)`. No firm, verdict, or URL here is fabricated. Re-verify perishable facts (verdict figures, review counts, page structure) at build time, because firms redesign and refresh these pages.

## The one legal-reality insight that reorders everything

For every other local vertical the moat in the local-proof block is a physical mechanism a competitor cannot fake (Roto-Rooter's "gumbo clay soil"). For personal injury the moat is the **verdict-and-settlement record tied to the named local jurisdiction, authored by a named bar-admitted attorney**. Real dollar figures from real cases in the local county court are the single most uncopyable proof asset in local SEO, because they resolve to public court records, they cannot be spun across 40 towns by an LLM (each county's verdicts are different and lookup-able), and they carry the YMYL trust signal Google weights hardest. Miller & Zois's "2021: $34,770,292 Verdict" in the Circuit Court for Baltimore City is the legal equivalent of "gumbo clay soil": strip the city and the sentence collapses, and a skeptic can confirm it in the public record. That is the whole game for this vertical. The bad examples below all fail the same way: they swap the city token and keep the boilerplate, carrying no jurisdiction-specific verdicts and no named responsible attorney.

## 1. Homepage (entity anchor + primary conversion)

### GOOD

**G1 - Breit Biniazan** (`https://www.bbtrial.com/`, verified live 2026). Why it wins: the homepage functions as the firm's entity anchor and its YMYL trust surface at once. It names 12 attorneys with roles (co-founders Jeffrey Breit and Kevin Biniazan, Managing Partner), stacks real award badges (Best Lawyers, Super Lawyers, Law360, Top 20 Verdicts 2024), and leads with uncopyable case proof carrying full context: "$1,000,000,000" (Deepwater Horizon, State of Alabama), "$360,000,000" (sexual-abuse civil trial), "$60,000,000" (head-on commercial-vehicle collision, traumatic brain injury), "Over $2 Billion Won and Counting." Contingency transparency is explicit and correctly framed: "We work on a contingency fee basis, meaning we only get paid if we win your case." NAP is real and multi-office (Virginia Beach, Richmond, Portsmouth VA, Phoenix AZ). Gate/law: E-E-A-T Trust (the dominant YMYL aspect), named-responsible-human requirement, real-results-as-proof. This is the homepage as entity hub done right.

**G2 - Gair, Gair, Conason (Gair Gair)** (`https://www.gairgair.com/`, verified live 2026). Why it wins: 100+ years of Experience stated as verifiable fact ("Founded in 1919"), named attorneys each tied to an external credential a skeptic can check (Ben Rubinowitz "ranked #1 attorney in New York Metro by Super Lawyers for third consecutive year," Diana Carnemolla in Forbes "Best Car Accident Lawyers in NYC 2023"), and case results with human context ("$17,500,000 settlement" for a grandmother and grandchild killed by a left-turning vehicle; "$85 million verdict"). Contingency terms are stated with the honest detail most firms omit: "usually one third of the net recovery after disbursement." Gate/law: E-E-A-T Experience + Authoritativeness via externally-verifiable third-party credentials, contingency-fee transparency.

### BAD

**B1 - the "justice reimagined" template homepage** (labeled real pattern; this exact shape is sold by dozens of legal web-template vendors, see the "best PI websites" roundups at dnovogroup.com/blog/15-best-personal-injury-lawyer-websites and thomasdigital.com/industry/personal-injury-websites for the genre). Why it fails: a brand-voice hero ("Fighting For The Justice You Deserve") over a stock gavel or skyline image, no named attorney above the fold, zero verdict figures, and "millions recovered" with no case, no court, no year. It reads identically for any firm in any city. Fails the YMYL trust gate (no named responsible human, no verifiable results) and the FTC/bar substantiation gate ("millions recovered" is an unsubstantiated results claim). A homepage that could belong to any of 10,000 firms is a trust vacuum in the one vertical where trust is everything.

**B2 - the directory-profile-as-homepage** (labeled real pattern: auto-generated firm landing pages on aggregators like Justia, Avvo, FindLaw, `https://www.justia.com/lawyers/personal-injury`). Why it fails as a firm's primary entity surface: these are database-generated, templated across every firm in the directory, carry the directory's branding not the firm's, and cannot host the firm's own verdicts, named-attorney authorship, or contingency terms. A firm that lets a directory profile stand in for a real homepage has no controllable entity anchor and no E-E-A-T surface it owns. Gate: E-E-A-T ownership + entity clarity. (Directories are fine as `sameAs` citations; they are not a homepage.)

## 2. Service page (single practice area, brand-wide)

### GOOD

**G1 - Gair Gair, Automobile Accidents** (`https://www.gairgair.com/automobile-accidents.html`, verified live 2026). Why it wins: it is a true brand-wide practice-area page (car accidents across the whole NYC metro, not one borough) that still carries un-fakeable proof: "$5 billion recovered for injury victims and over 60 verdicts exceeding $1 million," recent 2025 outcomes with case context ("$7,250,000 settlement" for a "27-year-old NYC Corrections Officer" in a motorcycle collision with a paratransit van), named attorney-authors, and a clear contingency explanation ("one third of the net recovery after disbursement"). It links down to the borough/city-specific children rather than trying to rank for every place at once. Gate/law: dedicated-page-per-practice-area (a rising ranking factor), E-E-A-T Expertise, results-substantiation.

**G2 - Dolman Law Group, Florida Car Accident** (`https://www.dolmanlaw.com/car-accident-lawyer/florida/`, verified live 2026). Why it wins as a state-level service hub: it names the responsible attorney (Matthew Dolman), states Florida-specific law that is genuinely load-bearing for the service (the two-year statute of limitations, comparative-fault rules, no-fault/PIP framing), and sits atop a browseable location hierarchy (`/car-accident-lawyer/florida/[city]/`) rather than a flat pile of city clones. Gate/law: intent-matched service hub feeding a clean silo (doorway policy: "clearly defined, browseable hierarchy," not "closer to search results"). Caveat for the builder: the deeper you go into Dolman's city children the thinner they get (see Section 3 B1), so this is a good hub with a mixed downstream, not a blanket endorsement.

### BAD

**B1 - the keyword-padded practice page with no attorney and no results** (labeled real pattern, endemic across low-budget PI sites). Why it fails: an 800-word "Car Accident Lawyer" page that repeats "car accident lawyer" and "personal injury attorney" a dozen times, lists generic injury types, promises "aggressive representation," and never names the attorney who would handle the case, never cites a single verdict, and never states the fee. It is the exact "content makes little sense to a reader but contains search keywords" example in Google's scaled-content-abuse policy, and it fails the YMYL named-responsible-human gate. In a YMYL vertical, an unsigned practice page is a trust failure before it is an SEO failure.

**B2 - the self-referential FAQ-padded practice page.** Why it fails: sections built purely to capture query strings with circular answers ("Who is the best car accident lawyer? We are the best car accident lawyer in [State]."). RicketyRoo names this exact pattern as a spam tell (`https://ricketyroo.com/blog/location-page-spam/`). For legal it compounds into a bar-advertising problem too: an unqualified superlative ("the best") can be a false-or-misleading communication under ABA Model Rule 7.1 and many state equivalents. Gate: scaled-content-abuse + bar Rule 7.1 (no unsubstantiated comparative/superlative claims).

## 3. Service-in-city page (the money page, highest doorway risk)

### GOOD

**G1 - Miller & Zois, Baltimore personal injury** (`https://www.millerandzois.com/claims-by-maryland-jurisdictions/baltimore-personal-injury-lawyer/`, verified live 2026). The definitive model for a YMYL combo page. Why it wins: it substitutes the physical local mechanism with the legal local mechanism, at depth that resolves to public records. It names the Circuit Court for Baltimore City ("one of the largest in the state, with 33 judges") and comments on that court's actual facilities and procedures; names a specific dangerous intersection (Gwynns Falls Parkway and Reisterstown Road, "46 accidents in a year") and a second (East 25th and North Calvert); ties malpractice claims to named local hospitals (Johns Hopkins, Mercy, Union Memorial, Harbor Hospital, UMMS); and lists jurisdiction-specific verdicts with year and dollar figure ("2024: $8,310,172 Verdict," "2021: $34,770,292 Verdict," firm aggregate "Over $100 Million"). Every one of those sentences collapses if you swap the city, and every one resolves to an external record. Gate/law: passes the strip-the-city test AND the external-verifiability test; E-E-A-T Trust at YMYL altitude. This is the single best legal combo-page teardown in the corpus.

**G2 - Nolan Law / autoaccident.com, Sacramento car accident** (`https://autoaccident.com/sacramento-car-accident-lawyer.html`, verified live 2026). Why it wins: genuine hyper-local anchors (named freeways I-80, Highway 99, I-5, the Capital City Freeway; the Sacramento County Superior Court as the actual venue; UC Davis Medical Center and Sutter General as the local trauma centers) and a genuinely local procedural passage a competitor cannot generically reuse ("If you're involved in a crash within Sacramento city limits, contact the Sacramento Police Department. Major collisions on state highways are handled by the California Highway Patrol"). Gate/law: Minimum Unique Local Substance bar (named anchors + local procedural condition). Honest caveat: its case results are not city-tagged, so the verdict proof is weaker than Miller & Zois; the fix is to add Sacramento-County-tagged results. It wins on local procedure, not on local verdicts.

### BAD

**B1 - Dolman Law Group, Tallahassee personal injury** (`https://www.dolmanlaw.com/personal-injury-lawyer/florida/tallahassee/`, verified live 2026; a real live AT-RISK page, not a fabricated victim). Why it is borderline-to-failing: it does carry real local anchors (a real office at 2707 Killarney Way, named attorney Matthew Dolman, Leon County crash stats "6,218 total traffic accidents... 35 fatalities," named intersections Monroe/Orange and Tennessee/Monroe, local ER addresses) - but a genuine majority of the body is transferable boilerplate (generic "5 Things to Do Following an Injury Accident," non-local settlement examples, comparative-fault and statute-of-limitations explainers, a copy-paste "Common Types of Personal Injury Accidents" list). The city-specific share is well under the working 50% floor. It sits on the doorway line: strip the anchors and most of the page still reads for any Florida city. Gate: the proportional-floor + strip-the-city test. The lesson is that a few real local facts do not rescue a page that is mostly template; the local substance has to be the majority.

**B2 - Whitlock Law, Ellicott City car accident** (`https://www.whitlocklawllc.com/ellicott-city-car-accident-lawyer`, verified live 2026; real live failing page). Why it fails, and why it is the sharpest contrast in this file: it is a Maryland PI firm in the same state as Miller & Zois, targeting the same query shape, and it does the opposite of everything G1 does. No named roads, no named courthouse, no Howard County procedure, no verdicts or settlements (only "300+ five-star reviews" and a vague "success... in all of Maryland's county courts"), and the named attorneys (Morgan Whitlock, Amy Harber) carry no bar credentials on the page. It leans on keyword-stuffed location lists instead of substance, so it fails the strip-the-city test outright: remove "Ellicott City" and nothing local remains. Same state, same query, opposite outcome - this pair is the cleanest proof that the vertical rewards jurisdiction-specific substance, not city-token swapping. Gate: strip-the-city + Minimum Unique Local Substance + YMYL named-credentialed-human.

## 4. City / location page (whole firm in one city)

### GOOD

**G1 - Miller & Zois, Baltimore jurisdiction page** (same URL as 3-G1, `.../baltimore-personal-injury-lawyer/`). It doubles as the firm's whole-firm-in-Baltimore page: it is filed under a clean jurisdiction subfolder (`/claims-by-maryland-jurisdictions/`), covers the full practice in that jurisdiction, and anchors on the county court, local intersections, local hospitals, and jurisdiction verdicts. Why it wins as a location page specifically: the organizing spine is the jurisdiction (the court system), which is the correct spine for a legal location page, and it is a browseable node in a real hierarchy of Maryland jurisdictions, not an orphaned clone. Gate/law: browseable hierarchy (doorway policy), jurisdiction-anchored E-E-A-T.

**G2 - Nolan Law / autoaccident.com, Sacramento** (same URL as 3-G2). As a location page it names the real coverage constellation (Sacramento County and its constituent cities), the local courts, and local procedure. Gate/law: Minimum Unique Local Substance via named local anchors and local procedural detail.

### BAD

**B1 - the city-swap network** (documented industry pattern: a regional firm builds one near-identical page per city it "serves," only the city name changing). Why it fails: after the March 2024 core-plus-spam update, a real multi-location service business of this shape lost rankings on 80%+ of its doorway pages and dropped a reported 63% in organic traffic within 30 days (reported case study, secondary, verify: upnorthmedia.co/blog/doorway-pages-seo). Ahrefs found 15% of penalized domains were hit for doorway violations, averaging a 75% traffic drop in 30 days. Every page fails the strip-the-city test simultaneously, and SpamBrain reads the cohort as scaled content. For PI firms this is the single most common way a whole site gets deindexed. Gate: doorway abuse + scaled-content abuse.

**B2 - the 42,000-page manual action** (reported case study, secondary, verify). A site received a manual action for doorway pages roughly four months after building them; traffic fell a reported 96% and recovery took eight months and required deleting 42,000 pages, then rebuilding fewer genuinely location-specific pages with per-area reviews. The source's own lesson: "if your only unique element is a city name, you are creating doorway pages." Gate: manual-action doorway penalty. The recovery path (consolidate, not multiply) is the tell for how to build correctly the first time.

## 5. About / team / attorney-bio page (the E-E-A-T and bar-compliance surface)

This is the load-bearing YMYL page for legal: it is where Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trust are proven by a named, bar-admitted human, and it is where bar advertising rules bite hardest (specialist claims, credential accuracy).

### GOOD

**G1 - Miller & Zois, Ronald V. Miller Jr. bio** (`https://www.millerandzois.com/our-team/ronald-v-miller-jr/`, verified live 2026). Why it wins: it proves all four E-E-A-T aspects with checkable facts. Expertise: J.D. magna cum laude, University of Baltimore School of Law; Executive Editor of the Law Review. Experience: 26 years teaching Insurance Law as a law professor; a two-volume treatise ("Insurance Settlements"); a career arc from defending pharma (Bayer, GlaxoSmithKline) to plaintiff work. Authoritativeness: Million Dollar Advocates Forum, Best Lawyers in America 2026, Maryland Super Lawyers 2026, Avvo 10.0, named bar associations (Baltimore City, Maryland State Bar, ABA and others). Trust: firm aggregate "Over $100 million in settlements and verdicts." Gate/law: full E-E-A-T stack with externally-verifiable credentials; this is what "named credentialed responsible human" looks like when done right.

**G2 - Gair Gair attorney profiles** (linked from `https://www.gairgair.com/`, verified live 2026; individual bios such as Ben Rubinowitz). Why it wins: each attorney is tied to an independent, checkable distinction - Rubinowitz "ranked #1 attorney in New York Metro by Super Lawyers for third consecutive year" and teaches trial advocacy; Carnemolla named by Forbes. Third-party, dated, verifiable recognition beats self-declared adjectives, which is exactly the E-E-A-T Authoritativeness signal and is also the bar-safe way to make a distinction claim. Gate/law: Authoritativeness via third-party credentials; bar Rule 7.4 safe (recognition, not a self-styled "specialist" claim). (Re-verify the exact live bio URL at build time; the firm's profile paths have changed across redesigns.)

### BAD

**B1 - the auto-generated directory profile as the firm's bio** (labeled real pattern: Avvo/Justia/Martindale/FindLaw database profiles, e.g. avvo.com and martindale.com attorney listings). Why it fails as the firm's owned E-E-A-T surface: templated, thin, directory-branded, no narrative of first-hand Experience, no firm-authored verdicts, no contingency terms. It cannot carry the YMYL trust signal because the firm does not control or author it. Fine as a `sameAs` citation, useless as the bio a searcher and an answer engine should find on the firm's own domain. Gate: E-E-A-T ownership + named-responsible-human on a controllable surface.

**B2 - the credential-vague or specialist-overclaiming bio.** Two failure modes, both real and both gated. (a) The empty bio: a name, a stock headshot, "dedicated advocate with years of experience," no law school, no bar admission, no jurisdiction, no results - it proves nothing and fails the YMYL Expertise/Trust gate (the Whitlock Ellicott City page in 3-B2 shows this exact gap: named attorneys, zero credentials on the page). (b) The overclaiming bio: calling an attorney a "specialist" or "expert" or "board certified" without the actual approved-board certification. Many state bars (Texas TBLS, Arizona, Florida) restrict "specialist"/"certified" to attorneys who hold that state's board certification, and ABA Model Rule 7.4 governs the claim; an unearned "specialist" label is a false-or-misleading communication and a bar-discipline exposure on top of a trust problem. Gate: YMYL Expertise + bar Rule 7.1/7.4 (accurate, non-misleading credential claims).

### GOOD

**G1 - Nolan Law / autoaccident.com Sacramento coverage** (same URL as 3-G2; the coverage passage). Why it wins as a service-area statement: it names the real constituent coverage inside one genuinely-served region ("Sacramento County including the City of Sacramento, Arden-Arcade, Carmichael, Citrus Heights, Elk Grove, Fair Oaks, Folsom, Galt, North Highlands, Orangevale, and Rancho Cordova") on a page that also carries real local substance, rather than minting a thin standalone page per suburb. Coverage is expressed as one substantive page that lists its named areas, which is the honest, non-doorway way to state a service area. Gate/law: service-area transparency without per-suburb doorway multiplication.

**G2 - Dolman Law Group locations hub** (`https://www.dolmanlaw.com/locations/`, verified live 2026). Why it wins as structure (the hub, specifically): it is a browseable, crawlable index of real offices with real addresses, sitting at the top of a clean `/personal-injury-lawyer/florida/[city]/` hierarchy - the "clearly defined, browseable hierarchy" Google's doorway policy explicitly contrasts against doorway pages. Gate/law: browseable-hierarchy exception to the doorway policy. Caveat carried forward from 3-B1: the hub's structure is compliant; several of its city children are template-heavy, so the hub is the lesson here, not the leaves.

### BAD

**B1 - the "we serve [City] and 200 surrounding areas" link farm** (labeled real pattern, endemic on multi-location legal sites). Why it fails: a page (or footer) listing 50-200 city names, each linking to a thin auto-generated city clone that funnels to one central intake form. This matches Google's named doorway example almost verbatim - "multiple... pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" - and is a structural doorway signal independent of any single page's word count. Gate: doorway abuse (the funnel-to-one-destination test).

**B2 - the subdomain-per-city network** (labeled real pattern: `tampa.firmname.com`, `orlando.firmname.com`, each a thin clone). Why it fails: it is the textbook doorway-network shape ("multiple domain names... targeted at specific regions") and a structural spam signal on its own. The compliant alternative is flat subfolders on one domain, two clicks or fewer from home (Section 10.1 of the service-city playbook). Gate: doorway abuse (network shape).

## The legal-advertising compliance layer (YMYL, on top of Google's spine)

PI pages must clear state-bar advertising rules, which Google's spine does not cover. Verify against the specific state bar per client and market; the rules below are the common core (ABA Model Rules 7.1-7.5 and their state analogs). Do not treat this as legal advice; the firm's own counsel signs off.

- **No false or misleading communications (Rule 7.1).** No unsubstantiated superlatives ("the best," "#1") unless externally verifiable and attributed (a Super Lawyers or Forbes ranking is safe because it is a third party's claim, not the firm's). This overlaps the FTC substantiation gate on any "millions recovered" claim.
- **Results disclaimer.** Many states (and the safe default everywhere) require a disclaimer near displayed verdicts and settlements that prior results do not guarantee a similar outcome. A verdict page without it is a bar-compliance gap even when the figures are true. Figures must be accurate and current (an outdated or inflated verdict figure is both a bar and a YMYL-trust violation).
- **Contingency-fee transparency (done correctly).** "No fee unless we win" is standard and good, but in many states it must be paired with a note that the client may still be responsible for case costs and expenses. Gair Gair's "one third of the net recovery after disbursement" is the transparent version; a bare "no fee, ever" can be misleading.
- **Specialist / certification claims (Rule 7.4).** Do not call an attorney a "specialist," "expert," or "board certified" unless they hold the actual approved-board certification (Texas TBLS, state equivalents). Unearned, it is a misleading claim and a discipline exposure.
- **Testimonial / endorsement rules.** Several states restrict client testimonials or require disclaimers; some prohibit testimonials that create unjustified expectations. Check the state before publishing client reviews as marketing copy (this is stricter than the general local-review guidance in the service-city playbook).
- **Required labels / identifiers.** Some jurisdictions require an "Attorney Advertising" label and the name and office address of at least one responsible attorney. The named-responsible-human is thus both an E-E-A-T signal and a bar requirement.

The through-line: in personal injury the YMYL trust bar and the bar-advertising rules push in the same direction as good SEO. Name the credentialed attorney, cite real and current jurisdiction-specific results with the required disclaimer, state the fee honestly, and never overclaim. The page that is compliant is also the page that converts and ranks, because all three systems reward the same thing - a real, accountable, provable human doing real work in a real place.
