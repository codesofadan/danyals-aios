# Sentence Patterns for Local Copy

Named, teachable sentence structures for punchy, clear local-service writing. These are the shapes that land a claim: short, blunt, specific. Adapted from direct-response copy craft (the Hormozi pattern set) into structures that fit a plumber's service page, a dentist's about page, or an HVAC city page.

Use them where they earn their place. Do not force all of them onto one page. Two or three well-placed patterns beat a page that reads like a list of tricks. And a pattern only works when it carries a real fact: the shape is the frame, the local specific is the picture.

---

## 1. The "If you [situation], [action]" command

Strips the message to a verb. No softening. Good for hero lines and emergency pages.

- "If your water heater is leaking, shut the valve and call us. We answer 24/7."
- "If your roof lost shingles in last night's storm, get the free inspection before you call insurance."
- "If it is an emergency, do not fill out a form. Call the number."

## 2. The "X is not Y, it is Z" reframe

Negate, redefine. The reframe is shorter and blunter than the setup.

- "A cheap roof is not a bargain. It is a second roof in eight years."
- "This is not a franchise. It is two brothers and one truck, since 2011."
- "That noise is not normal. It is a bearing about to go."

## 3. The single-sentence paragraph

A whole idea ends with a period and a return. Lands an argument. Resets rhythm.

- "We show up when we say we will."
- "No trip charge inside the loop."
- "The estimate is the price. It does not move."

## 4. The contrast couplet

Two sentences, near-identical syntax, one positive and one negative. Draws the line the reader is deciding on.

- "Most companies quote high and hope you do not shop it. We quote the real number and expect you to."
- "Some crews leave your yard a mess. Ours leaves it cleaner than we found it."

## 5. The arithmetic proof

Specific math that proves a point. The most persuasive pattern in local copy because it converts a vague benefit into a number the reader can check.

- "A $189 annual tune-up. A skipped tune-up that kills the compressor: $2,400. The math is not close."
- "We have been on 6,000 Austin roofs. That is not a slogan, it is 15 years times about 400 roofs a year."
- "Fixing a $300 slab leak now beats a $9,000 foundation repair in three years."

## 6. The "You [verb], you [verb]" parallel

Two or three short clauses, same subject, different objects. Clean and rhythmic.

- "You call, you get a person. You book, you get a time window. You show up, we show up."
- "You pick the shingle. We handle the permit, the tear-off, and the county inspection."

## 7. The pre-empted question

Ask the reader's real question, then answer it in the next breath. Doubles as a passage-block direct-answer lead and a PAA match.

- "How fast can you get here? Most of Round Rock, under 90 minutes."
- "Do you charge to come out? For diagnostics, $59, and it comes off the repair if you book."

## 8. The "X. That's it." closer

Drop the mic on a section. Ends on the one thing that matters.

- "One crew, start to finish. That is it."
- "We fix it right the first time, or the second trip is free. That is the whole warranty."

## 9. The metaphor with a number tag

A vivid, plain image followed immediately by a concrete figure. The image makes it memorable; the number makes it credible.

- "Your AC is a car that never turns off in July. A $189 tune-up is the oil change."
- "A clogged gutter is a bucket bolted to your roofline. One $180 cleaning beats one $4,000 fascia repair."

## 10. The "There is no..." absolute

A flat, confident claim with no qualifier. Use sparingly, and only when it is literally true.

- "There is no trip charge. Ever."
- "There is no subcontracting. The person who quotes it is on the crew that does it."

## 11. The first-person operator proof

"I / we [specific thing we did]." Carries first-hand experience, which is the E-E-A-T lever. Pull the specific from the SME interview.

- "I have replaced more Rheem water heaters in Cedar Park than any other brand, because that is what the builders put in from 2008 to 2015."
- "We caught 40 storm-damage claims last spring that the homeowners did not know they had."

## 12. The slow-then-fast cadence

A longer parallel setup, then a short kicker. Good for an about-page or a differentiator.

- "We could tell you we are licensed, bonded, insured, and family-owned, like every other company on this page does. Here is what actually matters: you get the owner's cell number."

---

## How to use these patterns

- Pick 2 to 3 per page, not all twelve. Overuse turns craft into a tic.
- The arithmetic proof plus the single-sentence paragraph plus a pre-empted question is a strong service-page combo.
- The "X is not Y, it is Z" reframe is the differentiator shape: use it once, high on the page, to draw the line against local competitors.
- Every pattern needs a real local fact inside it. An arithmetic proof with an invented number is a fabrication (fails the first-hand specificity and source gates). Pull the numbers from `brand.yaml` and the SME interview, or research a real benchmark and cite it.
