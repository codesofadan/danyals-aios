# Homepage - Local Service Business - Definitive Build Playbook

Command: `/write-homepage`. Page type: the single homepage of a local service business (plumber, HVAC, roofer, electrician, dentist, law firm, cleaning service, pest control, mover, and the like) that serves customers from a physical location or a defined service radius.

Read first: `knowledge/doctrine/seo-system-doctrine.md` (the build standard, Law 8 binding), `knowledge/doctrine/google-compliance-spine.md`, and the foundations `nap-consistency`, `schema-library`, `passage-block-protocol`, `internal-linking`, `eeat-framework`, `local-gbp-signals`. This playbook is the contract for the page; those files are the mechanics it assumes.

Scope guard. A local service homepage is not a national brand homepage, a SaaS homepage, or a DTC store. The buyer is choosing a human being or a crew to enter their home, fix something expensive, or handle something they cannot handle themselves, and they are choosing under stress, on a phone, comparing three tabs. The conversion event is a phone tap, a booked appointment, or a quote form, never an add-to-cart or a free trial. Every pattern borrowed from another category is flagged as a pattern, never as proof it converts for local services. All benchmark figures are directional and dated; none were live-tested against this specific client.

Method note. Named frameworks (Dunford, Schwartz, Dry, Miller, Gardner, McGlaughlin) are craft conventions with explanatory power, not controlled-study evidence. Where a rule holds only because "the framework says so," it is marked structural. Vendor teardowns (Hook Agency, Skill Mammoth) are content-marketing assets, cited as illustrative patterns. The two homepage teardowns in section 5 were fetched live this session and are quoted from the real pages. Every external claim carries its source URL. No local specific in a finished page is ever invented; it comes from the client profile, the SME interview, or cited research.

## 1. Purpose: the four jobs a local homepage carries, and how to rank them

A local service homepage is the only page on the site that has to do four different jobs at once. Most homepages fail because they optimize one job and starve the other three, or because they try to do all four with equal weight and end up doing none. The playbook's first decision is priority order.

**Job 1 - Entity anchor (the machine job).** The homepage is the canonical, self-declared identity of the business for Google, Bing, and the AI answer engines. It is where the business states, in human text and in structured data, exactly who it is: legal name, the one address, the one phone, the service categories, the service area, the hours, and the authoritative profiles that corroborate all of it. This is the job every other page depends on. If the entity is muddy here, no amount of conversion polish rescues the site, because the business will not be recognized as one stable thing in the Knowledge Graph, and AI answers will either skip it or describe it wrong. Entity clarity is upstream of both rankings and citations ([clickrank.ai Knowledge Graph guide, 2026](https://www.clickrank.ai/knowledge-graph-seo-guide/)).

**Job 2 - Primary conversion surface (the money job).** The homepage receives the largest single slice of traffic on the site: brand searches, "near me" searches that land on the domain, GBP click-throughs, direct type-ins, referral clicks, and paid traffic that is not routed to a dedicated landing page. For a local service business the homepage is usually the highest-traffic page and often the highest-converting one, because brand and near-me intent is warm. Its job is to convert that intent into a phone tap, a booked slot, or a quote request, fast, on mobile, under stress.

**Job 3 - Trust and E-E-A-T surface (the "should I let these people into my house" job).** A service is uninspectable. The buyer cannot test-drive a drain cleaning. So the homepage has to substitute proof for the missing product: real reviews with counts, licenses and insurance, years in business, real faces and real trucks, guarantees, and named local specifics. Trust is not a section; it is a property distributed across the whole page. See `knowledge/foundations/eeat-framework`.

**Job 4 - Internal-link hub (the architecture job).** The homepage is the top of the site's link graph. It passes the most authority and it sets the crawl priority. It must link cleanly down into the two silos that actually rank and convert: the service pages (one service, brand-wide) and the location / service-area pages (one service or the business in one city). A homepage that does not deliberately link into those silos leaves the money pages orphaned. See `knowledge/foundations/internal-linking` and `cluster-graph-protocol`.

**Priority order (the rule).** When two jobs conflict for the same pixels, resolve in this order: conversion in the fold, entity + trust immediately below, internal-link hub through the body. Rationale: the fold has one job, earn the call or the scroll, and cramming entity data or a link grid into the fold costs conversions. But entity and trust are not deferred, they are compressed into the fold (one rating block, one location line) and expanded below. The internal-link hub is a body-and-footer job; it never competes with the hero. This ordering is a cognitive-load decision, not an A/B-proven law, and it is stated as such.

**PASS test for section 1:** name, in one sentence each, how this page does each of the four jobs. If any job has no sentence, the page is not designed, it is decorated.

## 2. Target intent: what the homepage actually has to rank and support

The homepage is not the page that wins "emergency drain cleaning Round Rock." That is a service-in-city page's job. Being clear about what the homepage ranks for prevents the single most common local-SEO mistake: stuffing every service and every city into the homepage and turning it into a thin, unfocused doorway that ranks for nothing and dilutes the silo.

**The homepage owns three query classes:**

1. **Brand and brand-plus-category queries.** "Genz-Ryan," "Genz-Ryan plumbing," "Bear's Plumbing Spring TX." These are navigational, highest-intent, and the homepage must win them outright, including the branded pack and the Knowledge Panel. A business that does not rank #1 for its own name has an entity problem, not a content problem.

2. **The primary "[service] [city]" query for the home base.** The one head term for the business's main service in its primary city: "plumber Spring TX," "HVAC Twin Cities," "roofing contractor Tampa." The homepage is a legitimate contender for the head term of the primary city because that is where the entity is anchored. It competes here alongside the GBP listing and the map pack. It does NOT try to own every service in every city; those are delegated.

3. **"[primary service] near me" and generic category queries** from within the service area, where Google resolves "near me" to the searcher's location and the homepage's entity signals (NAP, GBP, reviews, proximity) decide inclusion in the local pack.

**How the homepage supports the whole silo.** The homepage is the hub; it does not do the silo's work, it routes to it. It links down to each primary service page with descriptive anchor text ("furnace repair," not "learn more"), and it links to the top-priority location / service-area pages. It states the full service-area coverage in text and links the covered-city pages. This is how homepage authority flows to the money pages and how Google understands the site's topical and geographic scope. The anti-pattern is a homepage that tries to rank for the long tail itself; the correct pattern is a homepage that makes the long-tail pages rankable. See `cluster-graph-protocol`.

**PASS test for section 2:** the homepage targets exactly one primary "[service] [city]" head term plus the brand terms, and every other service and city on the page is a link to a dedicated page, not a block of body copy competing for its own ranking.

## 3. The 2026 SERP, AI-answer, and entity reality

The surface the homepage is written for in 2026 is not ten blue links. It is a stacked result: a Knowledge Panel or brand card on the right, an AI Overview at the top for many informational and comparison queries, a local map pack for geo-modified queries, and organic below. Doctrine Law 13 is binding here: share of answer is a first-class outcome alongside rankings. The homepage is the single most important page for establishing the business as an entity these systems can recognize, describe, and cite.

**How the homepage establishes the entity.** Three mechanisms, all of which live on or are declared by the homepage:

1. **A single, unambiguous NAP.** One legal name, one primary address as a `PostalAddress`, one primary phone. Google builds a confidence score from the density and coherence of consistent facts across the web; inconsistent NAP makes Google treat variants as different entities and suppresses the panel and map ([lseo.com NAP consistency, 2026](https://lseo.com/blog/uncategorized/nap-consistency-why-it-matters-for-local-seo-rankings-in-2026/)). Businesses with consistent NAP across the top directories are reported to be roughly 3x more likely to appear in AI-generated local recommendations ([cited.so AI SEO for local business, 2026](https://cited.so/blog/ai-seo-for-local-business)). The homepage is the canonical source those directories should match.

2. **`sameAs` corroboration.** The homepage's `Organization` / `LocalBusiness` schema links out via `sameAs` to the authoritative profiles that confirm the same entity: the Google Business Profile, Facebook, LinkedIn, Yelp, the BBB, the licensing body, industry directories (Angi, HomeAdvisor for trades; Avvo for legal; Healthgrades for medical), and Wikidata if it exists. `sameAs` tells Google that these scattered references are one thing ([stackmatix.com Organization schema and Knowledge Graph, 2026](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/organization-schema-knowledge-graph)). This is how a small local business earns a Knowledge Panel; size and budget are not the deciding factors, entity-signal clarity is, and local businesses often qualify faster because Google can verify them through location data, reviews, and profiles.

3. **Machine-readable claims via schema and extractable text.** `LocalBusiness` structured data with the correct subtype, plus passage-block copy (self-contained, extractable answers, see `passage-block-protocol`), gives the AI engines clean facts to lift. Google's AI systems lean most heavily on the Google Business Profile to describe a business in local queries ([bippermedia.com AI Overviews citations checklist, 2026](https://bippermedia.com/seo/ai-overviews-citations-2026/)), so the homepage's job is to be perfectly consistent with the GBP, not to contradict it.

**The honest boundary.** Schema is AI-ingestion insurance and entity plumbing; it is not a magic ranking lever, and Google has stated AI Overviews do not require structured data. Knowledge Graph recognition typically takes 6 to 12 months of consistent entity building, not weeks ([clickrank.ai, 2026](https://www.clickrank.ai/knowledge-graph-seo-guide/)). Do not sell the schema block as an overnight rankings play. Sell it as what it is: the machine-readable declaration of identity that everything else compounds on.

**PASS test for section 3:** the homepage's stated NAP matches the GBP exactly, the schema declares the correct `LocalBusiness` subtype with `sameAs` to at least the GBP plus two authoritative profiles, and the money facts (service area, hours, phone) appear as extractable text, not only inside images.

## 4. Section-by-section architecture

Every section below carries: must-contain, the named framework that governs it, the local-SEO requirement, the schema requirement, a real-example precedent, the anti-pattern, and a PASS test. Assemble in the narrative order given. The order is a cognitive-load decision (lead with the buyer, prove, then ask), not an A/B law.

### 4.1 Hero (who / what / where / why-you in five seconds)

- **Must-contain.** In the first viewport, on a 375px phone: the service category, the primary city or area, the primary CTA, and one glance of proof. A first-time visitor must be able to say what you do, who it is for (people in this area), and why you over the next tab, in five seconds. The headline names the outcome, the service, or the objection, plus the place. Never the company name alone, never "Welcome to," never an abstract tagline.
- **Framework.** Donald Miller's grunt test (Building a StoryBrand) for the five-second comprehension bar; Eugene Schwartz's stages of awareness (Breakthrough Advertising) to set directness. Brand and near-me traffic is warm, so a short direct offer plus place beats a cold-traffic hook. For high-ticket, high-anxiety trades (roofing, HVAC replacement, legal), an objection-led or trust-led hero is a defensible default because risk, not desire, is the bottleneck. Treat it as a hypothesis to check against the client's real sales-call objections.
- **Local-SEO requirement.** The primary city or service area appears in the H1 or the immediate subhead as natural language, and the H1 is real server-rendered `<h1>` text, not baked into the hero image, for both LCP and AI extraction. Target the one primary "[service] [city]" term here (section 2), not a keyword-stuffed list.
- **Schema requirement.** None specific to the hero, but the H1 text should be consistent with the schema `name` + `areaServed`.
- **Precedent (real, live).** Genz-Ryan: "Preferred Twin Cities Heating, Cooling, Plumbing, & Electrical Provider" over "Putting Your Family's Comfort First," with the phone and "Book Appointment!" in the fold ([genzryan.com](https://genzryan.com/)). It names the four services, the metro, and the emotional benefit in one glance. Bear's Plumbing Services: "Plumbing Services in Spring, TX" over "Our Best Advertisement Is Your Satisfaction," with "(281) 350-BEAR" tap-to-call in the fold ([bearsplumbing.net](https://bearsplumbing.net/)). Plainer, but it passes the grunt test and the vanity number is memorable.
- **Anti-pattern.** "Welcome to [Company], Your Trusted Partner in Quality." A hero with no city, so "near me" relevance and instant local recognition are both lost. Four co-equal buttons. An H1 that is a logo image. A hero video that pushes the offer and CTA below the fold on mobile.
- **PASS test.** Show the mobile fold to someone for five seconds; they can state the service, the area, and the next action. The H1 is real text and contains the primary service and city.

### 4.2 Primary and secondary CTA (the ask, engineered)

- **Must-contain.** One visually dominant primary CTA repeated at every scroll-depth decision point, phrased as the highest-intent action for the vertical: "Call now" / tap-to-call for emergency trades, "Book online" / "Schedule service" for appointment businesses, "Get a free estimate" / "Request a quote" for project work. One lower-emphasis transitional CTA is allowed for the not-ready majority ("See our work," "View pricing," "Check your area"). Never two co-equal buttons.
- **Framework.** Oli Gardner's Conversion-Centered Design attention ratio (Unbounce): drive toward one action, keep the count of clickable things near the number of goals in the hero. The transitional CTA catches researchers without competing for the click.
- **Local-SEO requirement.** On mobile, the primary phone CTA is a real `tel:` link, and a thumb-reachable sticky call button is present at every scroll depth. In 2026 most home-service decisions are made on mobile in under a minute, so the tap-to-call cannot be plain footer text ([skillmammoth.com plumber website design, 2026](https://skillmammoth.com/blog/plumber-website-design)).
- **Schema requirement.** The `telephone` in schema matches the CTA phone exactly, in E.164 international format (`+1-512-555-0137`).
- **Precedent (real).** Genz-Ryan pairs "Book Appointment!" (primary) with "Free Instant Quote" (transitional) and a persistent phone ([genzryan.com](https://genzryan.com/)). Bear's pairs "Book Now" with "Call (281) 350-BEAR" ([bearsplumbing.net](https://bearsplumbing.net/)).
- **Anti-pattern.** Three-plus equal buttons ("Get Started," "Learn More," "Contact," "Services"). The CTA color reused on decorative elements so the button stops standing out. A mobile hero whose only path is an eight-field form. Phone number as un-tappable plain text.
- **PASS test.** Exactly one dominant CTA, repeated; a live sticky `tel:` on mobile; the phone in the CTA matches the schema and the GBP.

### 4.3 Primary services grid (the internal-link hub to service pages)

- **Must-contain.** A scannable grid of the core service categories, each a card with a specific label (the service, not a slogan) and each linking to its dedicated service page with descriptive anchor text. Cap at the real primary categories (usually 3 to 8); this is a router, not a sitemap.
- **Framework.** Information scent (Nielsen Norman Group): each card's label must predict its destination so the buyer self-selects. Structural.
- **Local-SEO requirement.** This is the homepage's primary internal-link job: descriptive, keyword-bearing anchors ("water heater repair," "furnace installation," "roof replacement"), never "learn more" x8. These anchors pass homepage authority into the service silo and tell Google the site's topical scope. See `internal-linking`.
- **Schema requirement.** Optionally emit `Service` items or an `OfferCatalog` on the `LocalBusiness`, but the load-bearing signal is the linked, labeled HTML grid. Do not fabricate `Offer` prices in schema.
- **Precedent (real).** Genz-Ryan presents four categories (Heating, Cooling, Plumbing, Electrical) each expanding to subservices with individual quote links ([genzryan.com](https://genzryan.com/)). Bear's groups 32 services under Plumbing / Lines-Pipes-Sewer / Water Heaters with the most-popular three surfaced ([bearsplumbing.net](https://bearsplumbing.net/)).
- **Anti-pattern.** A wall of 40 undifferentiated service links dumped in the fold. Cards that say "Quality Work" instead of naming the service. Services described in body copy that try to rank on the homepage instead of linking to a real page.
- **PASS test.** Every core service is one labeled card linking to a real dedicated page with descriptive anchor text; no card competes to rank the service on the homepage itself.

### 4.4 Local proof / trust bar (the compressed trust glance)

- **Must-contain.** A single compressed trust strip high on the page: the third-party rating with its real count and platform ("4.8 on Google, 512 reviews"), plus the two or three hardest local trust signals for the vertical (licensed and insured, years in business, certifications, recognizable brand affiliations). A number, not a paragraph.
- **Framework.** NN/g credibility research: external, third-party proof outranks self-hosted quotes. Visual hierarchy governs how much proof sits high (not "attention ratio," which is a CTA metric).
- **Local-SEO requirement.** The rating and count should match the GBP and the third-party profiles; the vertical-appropriate platform is what matters (Google/Angi for trades, Avvo for legal, Healthgrades for medical), not Trustpilot-by-default. If the rating is below ~4.5 stars, it is a conversion liability and the strategy is to earn more reviews, not to display a weak average prominently ([skillmammoth.com, 2026](https://skillmammoth.com/blog/plumber-website-design)).
- **Schema requirement, critical.** Do NOT emit self-serving `aggregateRating` / `review` markup on your own `LocalBusiness`. Google's documentation explicitly states these properties are "only recommended for sites that capture reviews about other local businesses," and Google has refused to render self-serving review stars since the September 2019 restriction ([Google LocalBusiness structured data docs](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business)). Display the rating as static text plus a link out to the real profile for verification; do not manufacture SERP stars that will not render.
- **Precedent (real).** Genz-Ryan surfaces "4.7 stars, 8,297 reviews" and "Family Owned & Operated Since 1950" ([genzryan.com](https://genzryan.com/)). Bear's shows dual "4.8 Google / 4.7 Angi," PHCC Gulf Coast membership, and family-owned ([bearsplumbing.net](https://bearsplumbing.net/)).
- **Anti-pattern.** A decorative five-star glyph with no count, platform, or link. A live third-party review widget in the LCP region (a top INP/LCP killer). A manufactured 5.0 created by filtering negatives (a trust and FTC violation). Displaying a lapsed or unheld license mark.
- **PASS test.** One static rating with real count, platform, and outbound link; two-plus hard local trust signals; no self-serving rating schema; no live review widget in the fold.

### 4.5 Problem / empathy band

- **Must-contain.** Immediately below the hero, name the buyer's real situation in their own words: the tangible problem (no hot water, a leak spreading, an AC dead in July) and how it feels (stressed, unsure who to trust, worried about being overcharged), in verbatim language mined from the client's reviews and sales calls.
- **Framework.** StoryBrand Problem plus PAS (problem-agitate). Structural: a problem-aware service buyer must see you understand the situation before you earn the pitch.
- **Local-SEO requirement.** This is where genuinely local, first-person specificity enters: the real local conditions (hard water in the county, ice-dam season, clay-soil foundation shifts) that only an operator here would know. This specificity is the humanization method (Law 8), not detector evasion.
- **Schema requirement.** None.
- **Precedent (illustrative).** Home-service teardowns that lead with the homeowner's stress and stakes rather than the company's history ([hookagency.com home service examples](https://hookagency.com/blog/home-service-website-examples/)).
- **Anti-pattern.** Jumping straight to a services list. Generic pain ("plumbing problems are stressful") with no local or verbatim specificity. A company-history paragraph in the empathy slot.
- **PASS test.** The band names a real, local, customer-verbatim problem, not a generic one, and it precedes any feature list.

### 4.6 Process / how it works

- **Must-contain.** A three-step process (four maximum), each a short verb phrase framed around the customer's experience: "1. Call or book online. 2. We arrive on time and give upfront pricing. 3. We fix it, guaranteed."
- **Framework.** StoryBrand Plan. Mechanism: service delivery is invisible, so a visible plan makes "what happens if I hire you" concrete and small, lowering perceived risk.
- **Local-SEO requirement.** Fold real local operating specifics into the steps where true: same-day / next-day availability, the actual service window, upfront-pricing or free-estimate policy. These are extractable trust facts.
- **Schema requirement.** None required; the availability can echo `openingHoursSpecification`.
- **Precedent (illustrative).** The three-clear-paths pattern on high-converting HVAC and plumbing homepages ([skillmammoth.com HVAC website design, 2026](https://skillmammoth.com/blog/hvac-website-design)).
- **Anti-pattern.** A seven-step process. Steps that describe your internal dispatch workflow instead of the customer's experience. No process at all, leaving "what happens after I call" unanswered.
- **PASS test.** Three or four customer-experience steps, each a short verb phrase, with real local availability or pricing policy embedded.

### 4.7 Service-area coverage (the internal-link hub to location pages)

- **Must-contain.** An explicit statement of the geographic coverage: the primary city plus the named cities, towns, neighborhoods, or counties served, with the covered-city pages linked. This is the homepage's second internal-link job.
- **Framework.** Information scent again, plus proximity relevance. Structural.
- **Local-SEO requirement.** Name the real cities in text (they are entity-defining `areaServed` signals), and link each to its dedicated location or service-area page with a city-bearing anchor. This is where the homepage feeds the location silo. The hard line from doctrine and the location-page playbook: the linked pages must each carry unique, locally-specific value; a homepage linking to 40 templated near-duplicate city pages is pointing at a doorway-page violation, so the homepage coverage list is only as safe as the pages it links to.
- **Schema requirement.** `areaServed` on the `LocalBusiness` (as `City` / `AdministrativeArea` entries or a `GeoCircle` with `geoRadius`), consistent with the text list and the GBP service area.
- **Precedent (real).** Bear's names a 15-city service area (Conroe, Cypress, Houston, Jersey Village, Magnolia, Montgomery, Oak Ridge North, Pinehurst, Porter, Shenandoah, Spring, The Woodlands, Tomball, Willis, Woodbranch) ([bearsplumbing.net](https://bearsplumbing.net/)). Genz-Ryan states the Twin Cities metro with named office locations in Burnsville and Blaine ([genzryan.com](https://genzryan.com/)).
- **Anti-pattern.** "We serve the greater metro area" with no named cities (no `areaServed` signal, no link equity to city pages). A comma-separated list of 60 cities linking to thin doorway pages. A city list that contradicts the GBP service area.
- **PASS test.** Named real cities in text, each linking to a genuinely unique page, matching the schema `areaServed` and the GBP.

### 4.8 Reviews / testimonials (the full proof stack)

- **Must-contain.** Below the compressed trust bar, at least three testimonials rewritten to Named + Faced + Specific: full name, city or service, a real photo where consent exists, and a specific outcome ("Fixed our burst main line in Spring the same afternoon"), not "Great service, 5 stars." At least one talking-head video testimonial if available. Link the aggregate out to the live third-party profile.
- **Framework.** NN/g: users discount on-site quote walls and trust external-profile reviews more, so the load-bearing rating links out; on-site quotes are supporting. Match the platform to the vertical.
- **Local-SEO requirement.** Local specificity in the testimonial (the city, the actual job) doubles as content relevance and as human proof. Reviews are a weighted factor in AI-citation eligibility, judged on volume, recency, and response rate ([bippermedia.com, 2026](https://bippermedia.com/seo/ai-overviews-citations-2026/)).
- **Schema requirement.** Same critical rule as 4.4: no self-serving `review` / `aggregateRating` markup on your own business. Display and link out; do not mark up for stars that will not render.
- **Precedent (illustrative).** External Google-rating surfacing linked to the real profile, the pattern Hook Agency cites for Smock HVAC's "1,000+ five-star reviews" done as a linked display, not a live widget ([hookagency.com](https://hookagency.com/blog/home-service-website-examples/)).
- **Anti-pattern.** "Great service! - J.D.," initials-only, stock headshots, faceless quotes. A self-hosted quote wall with no link to any external profile. Fabricating testimonials (a direct FTC violation, section 8).
- **PASS test.** Three-plus named, specific, locally-grounded testimonials; the aggregate links to the real third-party profile; no fabricated or filtered proof; no self-serving review schema.

### 4.9 About snippet (the entity + E-E-A-T compression)

- **Must-contain.** A short "who we are" block: the real founding year or years in business, ownership (family-owned, veteran-owned, local since), the primary credential, and one line of genuine local rootedness, linking to the full About page. Two to four sentences, not an autobiography.
- **Framework.** StoryBrand Guide (empathy plus authority). Authority substitutes for the demoable product a service cannot show.
- **Local-SEO requirement.** The founding year, the ownership, and the local roots are entity facts that should match the schema (`foundingDate`) and the About page. This snippet is the homepage's compressed E-E-A-T; the About page is the full surface (see the about-page playbook).
- **Schema requirement.** `foundingDate` and `founder` (optional) on the `Organization`, consistent with the text.
- **Precedent (real).** Genz-Ryan: "Family Owned & Operated Since 1950" ([genzryan.com](https://genzryan.com/)). Bear's: family-owned, and the footer even carries the Texas master plumber license (RMPL36236) ([bearsplumbing.net](https://bearsplumbing.net/)).
- **Anti-pattern.** A three-paragraph company history above the services. "We are passionate about excellence" with no verifiable specific. A founding year on the homepage that contradicts the About page or the GBP.
- **PASS test.** Two to four sentences with at least one verifiable, schema-consistent entity fact (year, ownership, credential) and a link to the full About page.

### 4.10 FAQs (the objection + AI-citation surface)

- **Must-contain.** A short band answering the real service hesitations in one to two crisp sentences each, written as extractable question-and-answer blocks: pricing (a floor, a range, or "free estimates"), availability and response time, service area, what is included, licensing/insurance, warranty. Each question an `<h3>`, each answer the paragraph beneath.
- **Framework.** Mechanism: every unanswered objection is a silent exit. Build for humans and for AI-answer extraction. This is the `passage-block-protocol` in action.
- **Local-SEO requirement.** These clean Q&A passages are prime AI-citation surfaces; write them so a single block answers the question standalone. Address "how much" somewhere; it is the top silent objection, and vague evasion reads as untrustworthy in 2026.
- **Schema requirement.** `FAQPage` schema is optional and legitimate for AI ingestion, but do NOT sell it as a Google rich-result play: Google retired FAQ rich results for most sites. Keep numbers and claims in the schema identical to the visible text.
- **Precedent (illustrative).** Service FAQ bands targeting pricing, availability, and area ([hookagency.com](https://hookagency.com/blog/home-service-website-examples/)).
- **Anti-pattern.** Keyword-bait FAQ that ignores the buyer's real worries. Answers that require reading three other paragraphs to make sense (not extractable). Selling FAQ schema as a Google ranking win.
- **PASS test.** Each FAQ is a self-contained Q&A block answering a real objection, "how much" is addressed, and any FAQ schema matches the visible text exactly.

### 4.11 Footer NAP (the entity anchor, literal)

- **Must-contain.** The canonical NAP in crawlable text: exact legal/brand name, full `PostalAddress`, tappable phone, hours, and links to the GBP and the authoritative profiles. This is the literal entity anchor of the page.
- **Framework.** NAP-consistency doctrine (see `knowledge/foundations/nap-consistency`). Structural and load-bearing.
- **Local-SEO requirement.** The footer NAP must be byte-for-byte consistent with the GBP and the top directory citations: same suite formatting, same phone format, same business name. Inconsistency here fractures the entity ([lseo.com, 2026](https://lseo.com/blog/uncategorized/nap-consistency-why-it-matters-for-local-seo-rankings-in-2026/)).
- **Schema requirement.** The `LocalBusiness` `name`, `address`, `telephone`, `openingHoursSpecification`, and `url` must match the footer text exactly. A mismatch between visible NAP and schema NAP is a manipulation signal.
- **Precedent (real).** Bear's footer carries name (Bear's Plumbing Services, LLC), address (18903 Tomato St, Spring, TX 77379), phone, plus the Texas license number and EIN ([bearsplumbing.net](https://bearsplumbing.net/)). Genz-Ryan lists both office addresses and the phone ([genzryan.com](https://genzryan.com/)).
- **Anti-pattern.** NAP baked into an image (uncrawlable). A phone number in the footer that differs from the GBP (call-tracking numbers must be handled so the GBP primary matches, see section 7). A P.O. box or a formatting variant that does not match citations.
- **PASS test.** Footer NAP is crawlable text, byte-consistent with the GBP and the schema, phone is a `tel:` link.

## 5. Best-in-class teardowns (real, fetched live)

Two real local service homepages, fetched and quoted this session. Neither is a national brand; both are exactly the kind of business this system writes for.

### 5.1 Genz-Ryan (HVAC / plumbing / electrical, Twin Cities MN) - genzryan.com

Fetched live ([genzryan.com](https://genzryan.com/)). Why it wins:

- **Entity clarity is total.** Family-owned since 1950, named metro, two real office addresses (Burnsville and Blaine), one phone, 4.7 stars across 8,297 reviews. Every entity fact a Knowledge Panel needs is on the page and consistent.
- **The fold does all four jobs at compressed weight.** Headline names all four services and the metro ("Preferred Twin Cities Heating, Cooling, Plumbing, & Electrical Provider"); subhead carries the emotional benefit ("Putting Your Family's Comfort First"); "Book Appointment!" plus the phone are the ask; the rating and "Since 1950" are the trust glance.
- **The services grid is a real link hub.** Four categories, each expanding to subservices with individual quote links, routing authority into the service silo rather than trying to rank every service on the homepage.
- **Risk reversal is stacked and specific.** "Same Day Service & Installation Guaranteed," "LIFETIME Guarantee," "Financing Available." Concrete, not "quality you can trust."

The honest caveat: a 4.7 average on 8,297 reviews is a massive, hard-to-fake proof asset most small clients will not have. The transferable lesson is the structure (entity clarity + compressed fold + link-hub grid + specific guarantees), not the review volume.

### 5.2 Bear's Plumbing Services (plumbing, Spring TX) - bearsplumbing.net

Fetched live ([bearsplumbing.net](https://bearsplumbing.net/)). Why it wins for a smaller operator:

- **A memorable, tappable brand asset in the fold.** "(281) 350-BEAR" is a vanity number that doubles as branding and a one-tap call, exactly right for an emergency-driven trade on mobile.
- **Dual-platform proof, honestly displayed.** "4.8 Google / 4.7 Angi," PHCC Gulf Coast membership, family-owned. Two real platforms, real counts, vertical-appropriate.
- **A named 15-city service area** in crawlable text, each a genuine `areaServed` signal and a link target into the location silo, matching a defined geography rather than "the greater Houston area."
- **The strongest entity footer in either example.** Legal name, full address, phone, plus the Texas master plumber license (RMPL36236) and EIN. That license number is a real, verifiable E-E-A-T and trust asset almost no competitor bothers to display, and it is uncopyable because it is literally theirs.
- **A genuinely human tagline.** "Our Best Advertisement Is Your Satisfaction" is plain, slightly imperfect, and sounds like a real tradesperson, not an AI template.

The transferable lesson: a small operator wins on specificity a big competitor cannot copy (the license number, the exact service towns, the vanity number, the plain human voice), not on review volume.

**What both prove.** The durable moat is real, verifiable, local specifics: the license number, the founding year, the named towns, the real rating and count, the actual guarantee. None of it is fabricable, and that is precisely why it reads human and ranks the entity.

## 6. Worst teardowns (documented patterns)

A responsible note: naming and shaming a specific small business in a client-facing playbook is a liability, so this section documents the real, cited failure patterns (each observed and written up by practitioners) rather than pillorying a named local shop. Every pattern below is the exact opposite of what sections 4 and 5 require.

- **The GoDaddy special / 30-minute template.** A generic template, three thin pages, stock photos, barely any text, a copyright year frozen at 2018 in the footer. It signals the business is not real, active, or capable ([blog.kofordmedia.com, template sites killing credibility](https://blog.kofordmedia.com/posts/template-sites-killing-credibility/)). Failure: no entity clarity, no local specificity, no proof, an actively negative trust signal (the stale year).
- **The handshake-and-glass-office stock hero.** Suited models shaking hands, a smiling generic call-center team, none of them the actual crew. Stock imagery signals the business is not the one in the photo ([amyshamblen.com, why bad stock photos kill your brand](https://www.amyshamblen.com/blog/why-bad-stock-photos-are-killing-your-brand)). Failure: kills the single biggest local conversion lever, real photos of the real team and trucks.
- **The interchangeable-headline homepage.** "We are passionate and we care about your success," a headline that could belong to any business in any industry ([cheapwebsitedesigner.org, cheap template mistakes](https://www.cheapwebsitedesigner.org/cheap-website-template-mistakes/)). Failure: fails the grunt test, names no service and no place, ranks for nothing, and is the exact string an LLM produces when it has no real facts. It fails Harry Dry's "could nobody else write this" screen instantly.
- **The no-NAP, no-city homepage.** "Serving the greater metro area" with no named cities, no crawlable footer address, a phone as plain text. Failure: no `areaServed` entity signal, no map-pack relevance, no NAP anchor, no tap-to-call. The entity is invisible to Google and the AI engines.
- **The company-as-hero autobiography.** A three-paragraph founding story above the fold, services buried below. Failure: leads with the business as the hero instead of the customer, violating the narrative order and burying the ask.

**What every worst pattern shares:** it is generic, it is stock, it says nothing only this business could say, and it gives the machines no clean entity to recognize. The fix is never a prettier template; it is real facts, real photos, real NAP, and real local specifics ([leadsbylocation.com, finding businesses with bad websites](https://leadsbylocation.com/blog/find-businesses-with-bad-websites/)).

## 7. The entity + NAP consistency section (exactly how the homepage anchors the business)

This is the job no other page can do, so it gets its own protocol. The homepage is the canonical declaration of the business entity; everything else on the web should match it, and it should match the GBP.

**The single source of truth.** Pick one canonical form of each NAP element and use it identically everywhere: on the homepage (hero context, contact section, footer), in the schema, on the GBP, and in every directory citation.

- **Name.** The exact brand/legal name, one form. Not "Bear's Plumbing" here and "Bear's Plumbing Services, LLC" there and "Bears Plumbing" on Yelp. Pick one; make the others match.
- **Address.** One `PostalAddress`, one suite format, one abbreviation convention (St vs Street, Ste vs Suite). This is the physical anchor; if the business is service-area-only with no storefront, follow GBP service-area rules and do not display a fake address, but still declare `areaServed`.
- **Phone.** One primary number. If call-tracking numbers are used for attribution, the GBP primary number must remain the business's real, consistent number, with the tracking number added as a secondary, so the entity's primary phone stays consistent across the web. Display the same primary on the homepage and in schema, in E.164 format in the markup (`+1-...`).

**The `sameAs` corroboration web.** In the homepage `Organization` / `LocalBusiness` schema, link `sameAs` to every authoritative profile that confirms the same entity: GBP, Facebook, LinkedIn, Yelp, BBB, the licensing body, the vertical directories (Angi/HomeAdvisor for trades, Avvo for legal, Healthgrades for medical), and Wikidata if present. This is how scattered mentions collapse into one recognized entity node and how a small local business earns a Knowledge Panel ([stackmatix.com, 2026](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/organization-schema-knowledge-graph)).

**The consistency audit (run before ship).** The homepage NAP must be byte-for-byte identical to: (1) the GBP, (2) the schema block, (3) the top-20 citations. Businesses consistent across the top directories are ~3x more likely to appear in AI local recommendations; inconsistent NAP makes Google fracture the entity into competing variants and suppress the panel and map ([lseo.com, 2026](https://lseo.com/blog/uncategorized/nap-consistency-why-it-matters-for-local-seo-rankings-in-2026/); [cited.so, 2026](https://cited.so/blog/ai-seo-for-local-business)). See `knowledge/foundations/nap-consistency` for the full audit routine.

**PASS test for section 7:** name, address, and phone on the homepage match the GBP and the schema exactly, character for character; `sameAs` links to the GBP plus at least two authoritative profiles; the canonical form is documented in the client's `brand.yaml`.

## 8. Google-compliance notes

Hard gates. A page that violates these is not lower-converting, it is a legal or platform liability. This is the axis generic homepage advice ignores.

- **No self-serving review stars.** Google's docs restrict `aggregateRating` / `review` markup to sites reviewing other businesses, and Google has not rendered self-serving review stars since September 2019 ([Google LocalBusiness docs](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business)). Display ratings as static text linked to the real profile; never mark up your own aggregate for stars.
- **FTC Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (16 CFR Part 465, in force since 2024).** No fabricated, AI-generated, or purchased-undisclosed reviews. No filtering negatives to inflate an average (this is why a fake 5.0 is a legal violation, not just a trust miss). Every quantified claim, testimonial outcome, and rating must be substantiated on file with a data date. Live-activity counters ("312 booked this month") are prohibited unless literally true and provable; default is to drop them. Displaying a lapsed or unheld license/insurance/certification mark is its own exposure, verify before display.
- **Regulated verticals.** Legal (ABA Model Rule 7.1 and state analogues): an ad that guarantees or warrants a result is a presumptive violation; use process/access promises ("free case review," "response within one business day") plus required disclaimers, never outcome guarantees. Medical/dental: state board rules plus FTC substantiation, no outcome guarantees. Financial: SEC/FINRA constraints on performance and testimonial claims. Route any guarantee/outcome copy on a legal, medical, or financial homepage through a compliance read before shipping.
- **Named-client consent.** Naming or quoting a client requires written testimonial consent, and any attributed outcome becomes an implied warranty you must be able to honor.
- **Schema accuracy.** Numbers and facts in schema must match the visible page exactly; a mismatch is a manipulation signal. Validate with the Rich Results Test and the schema.org validator (the old Structured Data Testing Tool is deprecated; do not name it).
- **Accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA, and the EAA for EU-facing).** 4.5:1 contrast for normal text on the CTA label; the AA tap-target floor is 24x24 CSS px (SC 2.5.8), though sizing the primary CTA and `tel:` button at 44 to 48px is better usability. Do not mislabel 44px as "AA."
- **No doorway pages downstream.** The homepage's service-area link list is only compliant if the pages it links to carry unique, genuinely local value. Templated near-duplicate city pages are a spam-policy violation; the homepage that hubs into them shares the exposure (see the location and service-area playbooks).
- **Core Web Vitals framing.** Targets at p75: LCP under 2.5s, INP under 200ms, CLS under 0.1. Frame CWV as a conversion and bounce lever, not a meaningful ranking factor (it is a lightweight tiebreaker). The honest case for a fast hero and no live review widget is conversion, not rank.

## 9. Voice and humanization notes (the homepage sets the brand voice for the whole site)

Doctrine Law 8 is binding: this system never uses detector evasion, humanizer chains, or "passes AI detection" gates. A page reads human because it IS specific and true, not because it was laundered. The homepage is where the brand voice is set for every other page on the site, so the voice decision here propagates.

- **Humanize with specificity, not tricks.** The line that makes a homepage read human is the one only this business could write: "the Texas master plumber license number in the footer," "family-owned in Spring since 2005," "we answer in Burnsville and Blaine," "hard water in Montgomery County eats water heaters." Real local facts, pulled from the SME interview and the client profile, are the humanization method. A competitor's LLM can hallucinate "innovative solutions"; it cannot hallucinate the client's real license number, real founding year, or the real towns they drive to.
- **Set the register deliberately.** Decide who is speaking and how, on two axes: authority (warm-neighbor / seasoned-expert / plain-blunt-tradesperson) and entity scale (a solo operator sounds like one accountable human "I"; a 40-truck company sounds like an accountable team "we, and here is your named lead"). Write the three-line house-style guardrail into `brand.yaml` (allowed words, banned words, sentence rhythm). This register survives when every competitor mines reviews with the same tools; without it, extraction flattens everyone into the same "Tired of X? We get you Y" template.
- **Voice of customer, verbatim.** Mine the client's real reviews and recorded sales calls for the exact words buyers use for their problem and their anxiety, and route those words into the hero, the empathy band, and the FAQ. This is the one durable moat in an AI-flooded 2026 web (section 5's Bear's tagline is a real example of an imperfect, human line).
- **Kill the AI tells.** Apply `knowledge/voice/`: no "in today's fast-paced world," no "we pride ourselves on," no stacked adjectives standing in for proof, no throat-clearing intros. Vary sentence rhythm; let one blunt short sentence land. But this is craft, not evasion, and there is no detector gate.
- **The plain-human bar.** A slightly imperfect, plain line in the operator's real voice ("Our Best Advertisement Is Your Satisfaction") beats a polished, generic one every time. Do not sand the client's voice into corporate smoothness.

**PASS test for section 9:** at least three specifics on the page could be written by no other business; the register is documented in `brand.yaml`; the copy is free of the AI-tell blocklist; no detector-evasion technique was used or claimed.

## 10. Meta formulas and JSON-LD

### 10.1 Meta title and description formulas

- **Title tag** (about 50 to 60 characters). Primary formula: `[Primary Service] in [Primary City] | [Brand]`. Trust-modifier variant: `[Primary Service] [City] - [Rating/Trust Hook] | [Brand]`. Examples: `Plumber in Spring, TX | Bear's Plumbing Services`; `HVAC & Plumbing in the Twin Cities | Genz-Ryan`. Lead with the service and city (the ranking and click terms), close with the brand.
- **Meta description** (about 140 to 160 characters). Formula: `[Service] for [city/area] homeowners. [Trust proof: rating/years/licensed]. [Availability]. [CTA].` Example: `Licensed plumbers serving Spring and 14 nearby towns. 4.8 stars on Google, family-owned since 2005. Same-day service. Call (281) 350-BEAR.` Include the city, one proof, one availability, one CTA. Do not keyword-stuff; write for the click.
- **Rules.** One H1 only, containing the primary service and city as real text. Title and description must reflect the real page; no bait. Numbers cited (rating, years) must be true and substantiated (section 8).

### 10.2 JSON-LD (Organization + LocalBusiness + WebSite + BreadcrumbList)

Use the most specific `LocalBusiness` subtype that exists. Verified real schema.org home-service subtypes under `HomeAndConstructionBusiness`: `Plumber`, `Electrician`, `HVACBusiness`, `HousePainter`, `Locksmith`, `MovingCompany`, `RoofingContractor`, `GeneralContractor` ([schema.org/HomeAndConstructionBusiness](https://schema.org/HomeAndConstructionBusiness)); plus `Dentist`, `AutomotiveBusiness`, `LegalService` / `Attorney`, and `ProfessionalService` for others. Google requires only `name` and `address`; everything else is recommended and adds precision ([Google LocalBusiness docs](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business)). ## 11. Finished-page checklist

A homepage is not done until every line is checked with evidence. This is the gate `/qa` enforces.

**The four jobs**
- [ ] Entity job: NAP + schema + `sameAs` declare one unambiguous entity, matching the GBP.
- [ ] Conversion job: one dominant CTA in the fold, repeated, with live `tel:` on mobile.
- [ ] Trust job: real rating + count + platform (linked out), plus two-plus hard local trust signals.
- [ ] Hub job: services grid links to service pages; coverage list links to location pages, both with descriptive/city anchors.

**Hero and copy**
- [ ] Grunt test passes in five seconds on a 375px screen: service, area, next action.
- [ ] H1 is real `<h1>` text containing the primary service and city; targets one "[service] [city]" term.
- [ ] At least three specifics no competitor could write (license, year, towns, real numbers).
- [ ] Register documented in `brand.yaml`; AI-tell blocklist clean; no detector-evasion used or claimed (Law 8).

**Sections present and correct**
- [ ] Problem/empathy band in customer-verbatim, locally-specific language.
- [ ] Three or four customer-experience process steps.
- [ ] Named-city service-area coverage, matching schema `areaServed` and the GBP.
- [ ] Three-plus named, specific, locally-grounded testimonials; aggregate links to the real profile.
- [ ] About snippet with a schema-consistent entity fact, linking to the full About page.
- [ ] FAQ as extractable Q&A blocks; "how much" addressed.
- [ ] Footer NAP in crawlable text, byte-consistent with GBP and schema, phone as `tel:`.

**Compliance (hard gates)**
- [ ] No self-serving `aggregateRating` / `review` schema on the business.
- [ ] Every rating, testimonial, credential, and number substantiated on file with a data date; client-naming consent in hand.
- [ ] Regulated vertical (legal/medical/financial): no outcome guarantees; compliance read done.
- [ ] License/insurance marks verified current before display.
- [ ] Schema numbers/facts match the visible page exactly; validated with Rich Results Test + schema.org validator.
- [ ] Service-area links point to genuinely unique pages, not templated doorways.

**Mechanics**
- [ ] LCP under 2.5s, INP under 200ms, CLS under 0.1 at p75; hero text is real text, not baked into an image.
- [ ] No live third-party review widget in the LCP region.
- [ ] Sticky `tel:` button reachable at every scroll depth; CTA contrast 4.5:1; CTA/tel target 44 to 48px.
- [ ] Form (if present) at three or fewer required fields, contact field accepts phone or email.

**Output package (per the Output contract)**
- [ ] `page.md`, `schema.json`, `internal-links.md`, `compliance-report.md`, `sources.md` all exist; every gate marked pass with evidence.
