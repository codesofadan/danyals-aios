# Meta and Headings (Local Pages)

The title tag, the meta description, and the H1/H2/H3 outline are three of the highest-leverage short strings on a local page. They decide whether the result is clicked in the pack-adjacent organic block, and increasingly whether an AI answer engine surfaces the URL as a citation. They are also the exact place local content shops get caught: one templated title stamped across 40 city pages, a description that keyword-stuffs "plumber Tempe plumber Mesa plumber Chandler," H2s that repeat the money keyword six times. That pattern is a doorway signal, and doctrine Law 8 plus the location playbook both treat it as a spam violation, not a shortcut.

Governing rule: **every page carries a unique title, unique description, and a unique heading outline built from that page's real, page-specific facts.** If two city pages could swap their titles with a find-and-replace on the city name, both are thin. The differentiator string in each formula is where you force uniqueness, and it must be a genuine local specific (a neighborhood, a permit rule, a climate fact, a real price, a real proof point), never filler.

---

## 1. Title tags for local pages

### Constraints

- **Length: ~50-60 characters** (design to ~575 px rendered width). Google truncates past that in most SERPs. Local titles run long fast because they carry a city; keep the city and cut adjectives, not the city.
- **Primary keyword front-loaded.** The service and, on geo pages, the city belong in the first 30-40 characters. Left-side real estate is the most weighted and the least truncated.
- **Weave four elements where length allows:** service + city + differentiator + brand (or CTA). Rarely all four fit; the fixed pair is service + city, then add whichever of differentiator/brand earns the pixels.
- **Brand at the end, after a pipe**, only if it fits. If length is tight, drop the brand before you drop the city or the differentiator.
- **Primary keyword once.** "Plumber Tempe AZ | Emergency Plumber Tempe" is stuffing; regenerate.
- **Unique per page.** No formula stamped across a city set with only the city swapped. The differentiator must change too.
- **No em dash. No banned vocabulary** ("Best," "#1," "Ultimate," "Top-Rated") unless it is a defensible, on-page-proven claim.

### The formula library (one per page type)

Notation: `{{service}}`, `{{city}}`, `{{state}}` (2-letter), `{{brand}}`, `{{differentiator}}` = a true local specific from `brand.yaml`.

**Location / city page** (`/write-location-page`)
- Formula: `{{service}} in {{city}}, {{state}} | {{differentiator}}`
- Filled: `Roof Repair in Gilbert, AZ | Tile & Foam Specialists`
- Alt (base-city home service): `{{city}} {{service}}: {{proof or since-year}} | {{brand}}`
- Filled: `Gilbert Roofing: Local Since 2004 | Summit Roofing`

**Service page** (`/write-service-page`, brand-wide, no single city)
- Formula: `{{service}} | {{coverage or differentiator}} | {{brand}}`
- Filled: `Sewer Line Repair | Trenchless & Same-Day | Valley Plumbing`
- Note: no city here (this page ranks brand-wide across the coverage area); lead with the service and a real differentiator, not a stuffed city list.

**Service-in-city combo / money page** (`/write-service-city-page`)
- Formula: `{{service}} in {{city}}, {{state}} | {{differentiator or CTA}}`
- Filled: `AC Repair in Chandler, AZ | Same-Day, 24/7 Service`
- This is the page type most abused by templating. The `{{differentiator}}` must be city-true (a response-time you actually hit in that city, a neighborhood you specialize in, a permit/HOA fact), so no two money-page titles are interchangeable.

**Homepage** (`/write-homepage`)
- Formula: `{{brand}} | {{primary service}} in {{primary_city}}, {{state}}`
- Filled: `Valley Plumbing | Emergency Plumbers in Tempe, AZ`
- The homepage is the one page that may lead with the brand (it is the entity anchor). Still include the primary service and base city so the title earns non-brand clicks.

**About / team page** (`/write-about-page`)
- Formula: `About {{brand}} | {{service}} Team in {{city}}, {{state}}`
- Filled: `About Summit Roofing | Family Roofers in Gilbert, AZ`
- Or credibility-led: `Meet the {{city}} {{service}} Team Behind {{brand}}`

**Service-area page** (`/write-service-area-page`, the coverage hub)
- Formula: `{{service}} Service Areas Across {{metro/region}} | {{brand}}`
- Filled: `Plumbing Service Areas Across the East Valley | Valley Plumbing`
- The hub names the region, not a stuffed city list; the individual city pages carry the per-city titles.

### Title anti-patterns (regenerate if any appear)

- The same title across a city set with only the city swapped (doorway signal).
- City repeated twice, or the service keyword twice.
- A stuffed city list in one title ("Plumber Tempe Mesa Chandler Gilbert").
- Brand in the leftmost position on a non-homepage (wastes the weighted slot).
- Unproven superlative ("#1 Rated," "Best in Arizona") with no on-page proof.
- Over 60 characters so the city or differentiator truncates away.

## 2. Meta descriptions for local pages

The description does not rank, but it wins or loses the click and often becomes the snippet an AI engine paraphrases. Write it to earn the click with a local proof, a differentiator, and a real CTA, with the city and service mentioned naturally once each. Not to keyword-stuff.

### Constraints

- **Length: ~150-160 characters.** Design for 160; longer truncates mid-thought.
- **City + service mentioned once, naturally.** Not repeated, not listed.
- **Carry a local proof point:** response time, years in the city, a real guarantee, a review count (only if genuine and on-page), a neighborhood, a licensing fact.
- **A real CTA**, not "Learn more" / "Contact us today." Use "Call for a same-day quote," "Book a free roof inspection," "Text a photo for a fast estimate."
- **Active voice, first-person plural** ("we") matches how a local business talks.
- **No em dash. No stuffing. No generic AI opener** ("Looking for a plumber in Tempe?").

### Formula patterns

**Pattern L1 - Proof-led (best default for money and location pages)**
- Shape: `{{Local proof}}. {{Service}} in {{city}} with {{differentiator}}. {{CTA}}.`
- Filled: `Licensed AC repair in Chandler since 2008. Same-day service, up-front pricing, no after-hours fee. Call now for a fast diagnosis.`

**Pattern L2 - Response/urgency-led (emergency services)**
- Shape: `{{Response promise}} for {{service}} in {{city}}. {{Trust element}}. {{CTA}}.`
- Filled: `60-minute response for emergency plumbing across Tempe. Upfront quotes, 24/7 crews, warrantied work. Call or text a photo now.`

**Pattern L3 - Differentiator-led (competitive service pages)**
- Shape: `{{What makes us different}}. {{Service}} for {{city/area}} homes. {{CTA}}.`
- Filled: `Trenchless sewer repair means no torn-up yard. Serving East Valley homes since 2004. Book a free camera inspection.`

**Pattern L4 - Question-answer (matches PAA / AI-answer intent)**
- Shape: `{{Real question}}? {{Short honest answer}}. {{CTA}}.`
- Filled: `Need a roof repair before monsoon season in Gilbert? We inspect and quote same week. Book your free tile-roof assessment.`

### Description anti-patterns

- Any city or service listing ("serving Tempe, Mesa, Chandler, Gilbert, Scottsdale...").
- Generic AI openers ("Looking for...," "Are you searching for...").
- "Discover," "Unlock," "Trusted," "Reliable," "Top-notch" as filler.
- A review count or rating that is not genuine and visible on the page.
- Ending on "Learn more" or "Visit our website."
- Copy identical across city pages with the city swapped.

## 3. Heading hierarchy

Headings carry three loads on a local page: the reader's scan path, the on-page keyword signal, and AI-citation eligibility. Retrieval models use H2s to find candidate passages, so an H2 phrased as a real customer question gets its following 40-90 words preferentially considered as the cited answer. That is why the outline is built as passage blocks (see `passage-block-protocol.md`), not as decoration.

### Universal rules

1. **Exactly one H1 per page.** It states the page's single job and carries the primary keyword once, naturally. Never two H1s, never an H1 that is just the brand name.
2. **Linear nesting.** No H3 without a parent H2, no H4 without a parent H3. Skipping levels breaks accessibility and confuses retrieval.
3. **The H2s tell the story.** Read top to bottom, they should walk a prospect from problem to trust to action. If the H2 stack does not make sense read alone, rewrite it.
4. **Most H2s are extractable questions.** Phrase the core H2s as the real questions a customer in that city asks ("How fast can you get to me?", "What does it cost?", "Are you licensed in {{state}}?"). Each is followed by a direct-answer lead per the passage-block protocol.
5. **City/service placement without stuffing.** The H1 carries service + city once. Put the city in roughly one to three H2s where it is genuinely load-bearing (a city-specific cost, a local code, a neighborhood), never in every H2. A city in six of seven H2s is stuffing; regenerate.
6. **Vary length and structure.** Mix 3-word and 10-word headings, mix question / declarative / imperative families. A uniform stack of "{{Service}} in {{City}}" H2s reads as machine-stamped.
7. **No generic headers.** No "Introduction," "Overview," "Our Services" (as a lone label), "Conclusion," "Why Choose Us" (as filler). Replace with a concrete claim.

### The H1 formula per page type

- **Location page:** `{{Service}} in {{City}}, {{State}}` (e.g. `Roof Repair in Gilbert, AZ`).
- **Service page:** `{{Service}}` or `{{Service}} for {{Region}} Homes` (no single city; e.g. `Trenchless Sewer Line Repair`).
- **Money page (service-in-city):** `{{Service}} in {{City}}, {{State}}` with a converting subhead below (e.g. `AC Repair in Chandler, AZ`).
- **Homepage:** the brand's value line, not just the brand name (e.g. `Emergency Plumbers Serving Tempe and the East Valley`). The brand name is the logo/nav; the H1 states what you do and where.
- **About page:** `About {{Brand}}` or a credibility line (e.g. `The Family Behind {{Brand}}'s {{City}} Roofing Crews`).
- **Service-area page:** `Where We Work` / `{{Service}} Service Areas Across {{Region}}`.

### Per-page-type heading skeletons

**Money page (service-in-city) - the conversion-critical outline**
- H1: `{{Service}} in {{City}}, {{State}}`
- H2: What {{city}} homeowners call us for most (declarative, sets local relevance)
- H2: How fast can we reach you in {{city}}? (question - response time, real)
- H2: What does {{service}} cost in {{city}}? (question - real pricing band)
  - H3: What changes the price (genuine cost drivers)
- H2: Why {{city}} homes need this (local specific: climate, housing stock, code)
- H2: Are we licensed and insured in {{state}}? (question - trust/E-E-A-T)
- H2: What our {{city}} customers say (proof, only if genuine)
- H2: Book your {{service}} in {{city}} (CTA block)
- H2 (FAQ): 3-6 real questions feeding the FAQPage schema

**Location / city page**
- H1: `{{Service}} in {{City}}, {{State}}`
- H2: {{Service}} across {{city}} and nearby neighborhoods
- H2: The {{city}}-specific problems we fix (local specificity, anti-doorway core)
- H2: Our {{city}} team and response area (E-E-A-T + coverage honesty)
- H2: Pricing and what to expect
- H2: FAQ

**Service page (brand-wide)**
- H1: `{{Service}}`
- H2: What {{service}} includes
- H2: When you need {{service}} (and when you do not - trust signal)
- H2: How our process works
- H2: What it costs
- H2: Where we do {{service}} (links to city/money pages)
- H2: FAQ

**Homepage**
- H1: value line (service + region)
- H2: Services we provide (hub links)
- H2: Areas we serve
- H2: Why {{region}} homeowners choose us (proof, specifics)
- H2: How to reach us (NAP + CTA)

**About page**
- H1: `About {{Brand}}`
- H2: Who we are / the {{founded_year}} story (real)
- H2: Meet the team (feeds `Person` schema)
- H2: Licenses, insurance, and credentials (feeds E-E-A-T)
- H2: The area we serve

**Service-area page (hub)**
- H1: `{{Service}} Service Areas Across {{Region}}`
- H2: Cities we serve (linked list to real location/money pages, no fake coverage)
- H2: How our coverage works (response times, dispatch)
- H2: Do not see your town? (honest overflow handling)

### Heading anti-patterns

- Two H1s, or an H1 that is only the brand name.
- The city in every H2 (stuffing).
- A uniform stack of same-length, same-family headings ("{{Service}} in {{City}}" x6).
- Generic placeholders ("Introduction," "Why Choose Us," "Conclusion").
- A heading with no informational payload ("About Our Work").
- Question headers used as rhetorical filler with no direct-answer lead beneath them.

## 4. Worked example end to end: money page, "AC Repair in Chandler, AZ"

Target query: `ac repair chandler az`. Page type: service-in-city money page (`/write-service-city-page`). Placeholder business `{{brand}} = Valley Air`, an HVAC company based in Chandler. Illustration only; every specific would come from `brand.yaml` and the SME interview.

**Title tag** (57 chars)
`AC Repair in Chandler, AZ | Same-Day, 24/7 Service`

**Meta description** (154 chars)
`Licensed AC repair in Chandler since 2009. Same-day crews, upfront pricing, no after-hours fee. Call now and we will have cold air back on today.`

**URL slug**
`/ac-repair-chandler/`

**Heading outline**
- H1: `AC Repair in Chandler, AZ`
  - (converting subhead, not a heading: "Same-day diagnosis, upfront pricing, cold air back on before dark.")
- H2: How fast can you get to my Chandler home?
  - direct-answer lead: 40-90 words, real response time for Chandler zip codes.
- H2: What does AC repair cost in Chandler?
  - direct-answer lead: real diagnostic fee and repair band.
  - H3: What drives the price up (compressor vs capacitor vs refrigerant)
- H2: Why Chandler ACs fail in July (local specific: 115F load, hard water on coils, undersized 1990s units in Ocotillo-area builds)
- H2: Common Chandler AC repairs we handle same day
  - H3: No cold air / frozen coil
  - H3: AC runs but the house stays hot
  - H3: Unit trips the breaker
- H2: Are you licensed and insured in Arizona?
  - direct-answer lead: ROC license reference (real number from `brand.yaml`), insurance.
- H2: What Chandler homeowners say about us (proof block, only if genuine reviews exist)
- H2: Book same-day AC repair in Chandler (CTA + NAP)
- H2: AC repair in Chandler: common questions (FAQ block, 4 real Q&As feeding FAQPage schema)

Why this passes: the title and description are unique to Chandler and carry a true differentiator, not a swapped city token. The H1 states the one job once. The H2 stack reads as a real problem-to-action path, most H2s are extractable questions with direct-answer leads (feeding both the passage-block protocol and the FAQPage schema in `schema-library.md`), and the city appears in the H1, the "Why Chandler ACs fail" section, and the CTA, three genuinely load-bearing spots, not stuffed into every heading. Swap "Chandler" for "Gilbert" and the July-heat section, the Ocotillo housing-stock detail, and the response-time answer all break, which is exactly the test that separates a real local page from a doorway.

## Self-test before delivery

1. Title under 60 chars, service + city front-loaded, one differentiator, no city or keyword repeated?
2. Description under 160 chars, one local proof, one real CTA, city and service once each, no stuffing?
3. Could another city page in the set use this exact title or description with only the city swapped? If yes, both are thin. Rewrite the differentiator.
4. Exactly one H1? H2s tell the story read alone?
5. Are 3+ H2s phrased as real customer questions with a direct-answer lead beneath (cross-check `passage-block-protocol.md`)?
6. Is the city in only the H2s where it is load-bearing, not every H2?
7. Zero generic placeholders, zero unproven superlatives, zero em dashes?

If any check fails, rewrite the failing string. Do not pad or trim mechanically.
