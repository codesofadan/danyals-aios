# Med-Spa / Aesthetics: The Example Library (YMYL)

Real, live-verified good and bad examples for every one of the six page types, in the med-spa / aesthetics vertical. This is a YMYL health category: a stranger is deciding whether to let a needle, a laser, or a fat-freezing applicator touch their body, so **trust is the dominant E-E-A-T aspect and the named, licensed, responsible human is the whole game.** Every example below is graded against the compliance spine gate codes (`knowledge/doctrine/google-compliance-spine.md`), the quality gates (`knowledge/quality-gates/gates.md`, G0-G13), and the content laws (`knowledge/doctrine/local-content-laws.md`, esp. Law 16 Experience-proven and Law 20 no-fabricated-proof), governed by Law 8 (optimize the reward function, not detector-evasion).

**How to read this file.** For each page type: at least 2 real GOOD examples (live URL, why it wins, the gate/law it satisfies) and at least 2 real BAD or penalty-risk examples (live URL, or a clearly labeled real recurring pattern where a single URL cannot be pinned without asserting intent). Every URL was fetched live this session unless labeled "pattern, not a single live-verified URL." Where a live claim could not be verified, it is flagged, never dressed up. Provider names and credentials are quoted as they appear on the page.

Verification date: 2026-07-19/20 PKT. Re-verify perishable facts (staff rosters, prices, disclaimers) at build time; med-spa provider pages change often.

## 0. The med-spa YMYL compliance spine (read before the examples)

These are the vertical-specific hard rules the examples are graded against. All are verifiable this session.

- **Name the responsible licensed human.** A med spa that performs injectables, lasers, or body contouring operates under a medical director / supervising physician, and a "good faith exam" by a physician or advanced-practice provider is required before treatment in most states (americanmedspa.org; state boards vary: CA requires physician supervision, AZ grants NPs independent scope, TX requires a physician/PA/APRN on-site or immediately available). The page carrying a medical claim must name the medical director or the credentialed injector (MD, DO, RN, BSN, NP, FNP-C, NP-C, PA-C, DNP, CANS). An anonymous "team of experts" on a YMYL money page fails **C3, C5, C6**. This is the single most common med-spa failure and the highest-leverage fix.
- **Before/after and results claims must be honest (Law 20, C5).** The FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule took effect **2024-10-21**; it bans fake reviews, AI-generated reviews, and undisclosed insider/material connections, with civil penalties currently **$51,744 per violation** (first warning letters issued 2025-12-22, ftc.gov). Before/after photos must show typical results, use consistent lighting/angles, disclose any editing, state treatment count and interval, and carry a "results may vary / not typical" disclaimer for outliers (ftc.gov Health Products Compliance Guidance; 16 CFR Part 255; NY 8 NYCRR 29.1). Written, marketing-specific patient photo consent is required and retained (six years in NY).
- **No guaranteed or absolute outcomes.** "Permanent," "guaranteed," "smooth skin for life" on a medical treatment is FTC-actionable and a YMYL trust breach even when a small-print disclaimer exists elsewhere on the page. A contradictory disclaimer does not cure a guaranteed-outcome headline (C5, Law 20).
- **"FDA-approved" only for the specific approved use** (C5, G10). Stating a drug/device is FDA-approved for its cleared indication is fine; implying blanket safety or off-label efficacy is not.
- **Pricing transparency is rare and therefore a differentiator, not a requirement.** When present (per-unit Botox, per-syringe filler), it is a strong first-hand specificity signal (G1, Law 17). When absent, it is a weakness, not a violation.
- **Real photos of real staff and real patients only.** Stock "smiling clinician" imagery in the proof zone is an Experience failure (Law 16, C2).

## 1. HOMEPAGE (entity anchor + primary conversion)

### GOOD

- **Ever/Body - `https://everbody.com`** (NYC + Washington DC). Names providers with real credentials on the entity-anchor page itself: **Chris Blaisdell, MSN, CRNP-BC** and **Lauren Moody, MSN, FNP-BC, LE** (nurse practitioner + licensed esthetician). Carries an explicit medical disclaimer ("does not constitute medical advice ... not intended to be relied upon for medical diagnosis or treatment") and names services with trademark discipline (Botox, Hydrafacial, DiamondGlow, Morpheus8, VI Peel, Lumecca IPL, Kybella, Sculptra, Emsculpt NEO, CoolTone, laser hair removal). Financing disclosed (Cherry). **Why it wins:** the responsible clinicians are named and credentialed on the money page, not hidden two clicks away. Satisfies **C3, C5** and G2. (Weakness: no per-treatment pricing on the homepage.)
- **Milan Laser - `https://www.milanlaser.com`** (good bones, one risky claim, use as a teaching case). Names medical leadership: **Dr. Nate Haecker, MD, MBA, Chief Medical Officer**; providers described as Registered Nurses / Authorized Candela Practitioners. Carries FTC-aware language ("Individual results may vary," an asterisked reduction-claim footnote clarifying that "permanent results does not necessarily imply the elimination of all hairs"), plus 0% payment plans and a lifetime-cost comparison. **Why it wins:** named CMO + disclaimer discipline + pricing transparency. **The caveat that makes it a lesson:** the headline "Smooth skin for life, guaranteed" is a guaranteed-outcome claim that the footnote does not cure (see BAD below). Satisfies **C5** on the credential/disclaimer axis; trips it on the headline.

### BAD

- **SkinSpirit homepage - `https://www.skinspirit.com`** (live-verified). Names **no** providers and **no** credentials: only "Highly-trained experts" / "Medical Professionals" with a generic "meet our experts" link. Trust is carried by press logos (Vogue, ELLE, NYT) and "over 50 locations" scale, not by a named medical director. No pricing, no disclaimer visible. **Why it fails:** for a YMYL health entity page, anonymous clinicians is the core E-E-A-T miss; Google does not trust anonymous medical claims. Trips **C3, C5**. (Note: SkinSpirit redeems this decisively on its provider and location pages, see sections 3 and 5, so the failure is homepage-specific.)
- **The guaranteed-outcome headline** - pattern, exemplified live by Milan Laser's "Smooth skin for life, guaranteed" and "Unlimited treatments for life." **Why it fails:** absolute outcome guarantees ("guaranteed," "permanent," "for life") on a medical treatment are FTC-actionable and undercut YMYL trust; a small-print "results may vary" footnote elsewhere contradicts rather than cures the headline. Trips **C5** and **Law 20**. The single most common med-spa homepage compliance failure.

## 2. SERVICE PAGE (one service, described brand-wide)

### GOOD

- **Ever/Body - Botox / wrinkle relaxers - `https://everbody.com/treatment/botox/`** (live-verified). Best-in-class YMYL service page. Explains the mechanism honestly (neuromodulator "temporarily blocks" nerve impulses), states the specific approved use ("Botox is a safe, FDA-approved treatment"), and, instead of hype, gives **quantified, honest risk disclosure**: side effects listed (headache, minor bruising, swelling, "rare") with a specific complication rate, eyelid sagging "less than 0.1% of the time," and contraindications (not for pregnancy/breastfeeding). Results are hedged ("results should appear," last "3-4 months" depending on "metabolism and dosage"), never guaranteed. **Why it wins:** it treats the reader as a patient, not a mark. Satisfies **C5, G1, Law 17** (real statistics beat adjectives). (Weakness: no pricing and no named injector on this specific page.)
- **InjectCo (Plano, TX) - `https://injectco.com/areas-we-serve/med-spa-plano-tx/`** (live-verified). Names the credentialed injector: **Chloe, RN**, "Clinical Aesthetic Nurse Injector," BSN from Chamberlain University, positioned as "100% Nurse-Led. No Delegated Injections" with "physician oversight." Rare **pricing transparency**: Botox "starts from $12 per unit," dermal fillers "from $699 per syringe," lip filler "from $399 per syringe"; realistic timeline (results in 3-7 days). **Why it wins:** named RN + physician-oversight statement + real numbers = strong G1 and C3. **Caveats to flag as teaching points:** "Every photo is unedited" is an unverifiable claim inviting scrutiny, "50,000+ Patients Treated Nationwide" is an unsubstantiated scale claim (C6 risk), and there is no informed-consent or complication-rate language. Strong on credentials + pricing, weaker on disclaimers than Ever/Body.

### BAD

- **Thin service entry - SkinSpirit Botox/Dysport - `https://www.skinspirit.com/services/botox-dysport`** (live-verified). The service is described in effectively one sentence ("Diminish wrinkles & fine lines with injections that reduce repetitive muscle movements") with no pricing, no named/credentialed injector, no FDA/safety context, no risk disclosure, no "results may vary." **Why it fails:** for a YMYL injectable, that is thin content carrying a medical claim with zero on-page E-E-A-T. Trips **G3** (thin), **A3** (not a satisfying/complete answer), **C5**.
- **The guaranteed-outcome service page** - pattern, exemplified live by Milan Laser's laser-hair-removal offer ("Smooth skin for life, guaranteed," "Unlimited treatments for life"). **Why it fails:** promising a permanent/guaranteed medical result on the page that sells the service is the highest-risk med-spa pattern, FTC deception exposure plus loss of YMYL trust. The compliant version states "results may vary / not typical," never guarantees an outcome, and qualifies "permanent." Trips **C5, Law 20**.

## The sharpest med-spa-specific insight

In every other local vertical the anti-doorway moat is the ground: the soil, the freeway, the courthouse, the water chemistry. **In med-spa the moat is the person, not the place.** The verified proof of this is SkinSpirit: its Scottsdale and Austin pages pass the strip-the-city test not because of neighborhood copy but because the two clinics list five completely different named, credentialed injectors (Barrett/Kardjian/Van Eimeren vs Patek/Davis/Dunavant/Jacques/Clayton). You cannot find-and-replace a real RN roster the way you can swap a city token. The chains that fail (Milan's city hub, LaserAway's grid, Priva's town pages) all fail the same way, they abstract the human up to "highly trained medical professionals" and let only the address vary. So the med-spa build rule is inverted from the plumber build rule: **lead the uniqueness with the named licensed human who works that specific location, then layer local geography on top.** This also happens to be the exact thing YMYL demands (C5, named credentialed responsible human) and the exact thing Law 20 protects (the person and their results claims must be real), which is why on a med-spa page the anti-doorway control and the trust control are the same control. Get the named injector plus an honest "results may vary, images may contain models" disclaimer (the Skin Shop pattern) and the page clears the doorway gate and the YMYL gate at once.
