## 1. Decision

**We build the geo-grid ourselves on DataForSEO's Google Maps SERP standard queue at $0.0006 per coordinate (up to 100 Maps results per coordinate), behind a `GridDataProvider` interface, with Serper retained as a burst/secondary implementation of the same interface and the Google Places API deliberately excluded from the grid path.** Grid points are generated by geodesic offset from a **shared per-metro lattice anchor** (not from each client's own pin), at a **pinned constant `zoom`** stored on the grid definition and included in the cache key; the raw provider response for every point is persisted as Evidence. Organic rank tracking runs on the **same** DataForSEO account at **depth 20 (2 billing pages, $0.0012 per check)** — never depth 100, which is 5× the price — while Maps runs at **depth 100 because the Maps base price already includes 100 results**. The grid cache is keyed `(provider, keyword, lat_4dp, lng_4dp, zoom, date)` and is built now because it is nearly free to build and is the only cost lever that improves with client density — but its saving is **not** put in the business case at 100 geographically diffuse clients (modelled 14.6% hit rate, $5.15/month). We will **not** call the Google Places API for any client-facing ranking surface, because Google's Maps Platform ToS forbids copying and saving business names and forbids displaying Places content alongside a non-Google map — two restrictions a white-label geo-grid product cannot live inside. Places is used for exactly one thing: resolving and refreshing a client's `place_id`, which Google explicitly exempts from caching restrictions and permits us to store indefinitely. On the money side we commit to the gate contract **dial → cache → per-client cap → daily stop → call → log ACTUAL**, which requires **five** fixes to shipped code before any of this goes live — the daily stop is not wired; `cost_log.cost` is `numeric(10,2)` and silently rounds every sub-cent provider call to $0.00; **`client_budgets.spent` is also `numeric(10,2)`, so the per-client cap can never trip on grid or rank traffic even once the ledger is fixed**; `CostGate.run()` writes estimates into the ledger as if they were actuals; and blocks other than the global halt are not raised as typed refusals. **A sixth defect sits outside the gate and is the largest single money bug in the repo: the rank tracker ships `DEFAULT_DEPTH = 100` and prices organic in stale 100-result blocks, so it both overspends 5× and under-reports it 6× (§3.2).** Finally, **GSC history is archived on the day a client connects or it is lost forever**: Google keeps 16 months and the BigQuery bulk export does not backfill.

**Expected all-in provider spend at 100 clients: ~$279/month (~$2.79 per client per month), band $110 – $930.** The top three drivers are organic rank tracking (32%), paid audits (20%), and **content (11.4%)** — with the geo-grid fourth at 10.8% net of cache (12.6% gross). *(Corrected 2026-08-23: this line previously named the geo-grid third at "13%", which matches neither its net share (10.8%) nor its gross share (12.6%), and content at $31.80 outranks the grid's $30.13 net in the §5.3 table's own terms.)*

## 2. Context — the question this track had to settle

Four things blocked engineering:

1. **Build or buy the geo-grid.** A prior estimate put geo-grid data at ~$3.38 per location per month, which made building look pointless next to Local Falcon. That number was wrong. Until it was corrected with real vendor prices, the local module could not be scoped and the client-facing pricing could not be set.
2. **The rankings source.** `docs/recovery/DECISIONS_LOG.md` records **D-6 as still open**, with a recommendation of *"serper.dev for local-pack/geo-grid; DataForSEO for organic rank; never mixed, always labelled."* The specification itself flags the conflict at §30.3 and says it *"was never picked."* A mixed source is worse than either source alone — historical rank data is worthless if the provider changed mid-stream — so one had to be picked and the other demoted to a labelled fallback.
3. **The retention traps.** Some data is destroyed by the provider if we do not capture it on day one (GSC's 16-month window), and some data we are contractually forbidden to keep (Places content). Both are cheap to build now and impossible to retrofit — GSC history that has aged out cannot be recovered at any price.
4. **The money model.** `docs/recovery/DECISIONS_LOG.md:78` flags (D-3/Q-1) that provider quotas *"were sized for 15 clients and will not survive 100"* and names serper.dev's free tier explicitly at line 79: *"free tier is 2,500 searches/month in total."* That line is internally contradictory ("per month" vs "in total") and serper.dev states only *"Get 2,500 free queries"* with no renewal period — see §3.7, which treats it as a one-time grant. Nobody had costed the platform bottom-up at 100 clients.

**Explicit supersession.** This record supersedes the D-6 *recommendation* in `docs/recovery/DECISIONS_LOG.md` (Serper as the local-pack/geo-grid source). Grid and map-pack data now come from **DataForSEO Maps**, with Serper as a labelled secondary. The evidence for the change: Serper publishes no pricing page, no SLA and no uptime commitment anywhere on its public site (only a "Status page" link), its free tier is 2,500 queries in total, and at the volume we will actually buy ($50 Starter pack) it costs $1.00/1,000 versus DataForSEO Maps at $0.60/1,000 — while DataForSEO's Maps base price returns **100** results per unit against Serper's 10. The *"never mixed, always labelled"* half of D-6 is **kept and strengthened** (see §5.7). The build-not-buy half of D-6 is confirmed with corrected arithmetic (§5.2).

## 3. Findings

### 3.1 The grid arithmetic — building is 5.7× cheaper than the best vendor, and the old $3.38 figure was ~29× too high

**Conclusion: at 3 tracked keywords, a 7×7 grid and weekly cadence, our grid data costs $0.35 per location per month. Local Falcon's own published credit pricing puts the same volume at $1.86 per location per month.**

DataForSEO Google Maps SERP, standard queue (normal priority): **$0.0006 per SERP page**, and the Maps task documentation states *"Your account will be billed per each SERP containing up to 100 results."* One coordinate at `depth: 100` therefore costs $0.0006 and returns up to 100 ranked Maps businesses — enough to find a client sitting at position 87.

```
Per client per month (3 keywords, 7×7 = 49 points, weekly = 4 scans):
  3 × 49 × 4 = 588 coordinate queries
  588 × $0.0006 = $0.3528 per client per month

At 100 clients: 58,800 queries × $0.0006 = $35.28 / month
```

Local Falcon's published pricing defines **one credit = one grid point**: *"A credit in Local Falcon is defined as one data point, often referred to as a 'map pin,' used on the geo-grid during a scan."* Its cheapest effective rate is the Premium plan at $199.99/month for 63,150 credits = **$0.0031669 per credit**. (Verified 2026-08-23: the standard ladder is Starter $24.99/7,500 · Basic $49.99/15,150 · Pro $99.99/31,250 · Premium $199.99/63,150. Enterprise tiers of $499–$4,999 for 157,000–1,570,000 credits exist and are *fractionally worse* per credit — $0.003178 and $0.003184 — so Premium is genuinely the cheapest published rate.)

```
Same 58,800 grid points at Local Falcon Premium:
  58,800 × $0.0031669 = $186.21   (and you must buy the $199.99 plan to hold 63,150 credits)
  $199.99 / $35.28 = 5.67× more expensive to buy than to build
  Annual delta: ($199.99 − $35.28) × 12 = $1,976.52
```

**Reproducing the "~30× wrong" claim.** At 1 keyword, a 7×7 grid and weekly cadence: `49 × 4 × $0.0006 = $0.1176` per location per month. `$3.38 / $0.1176 = 28.7×`. The old estimate was ~29× high under those assumptions. The multiplier is entirely assumption-dependent — at 3 keywords it is ~9.6× — so the honest statement is: *the original figure was off by roughly an order of magnitude and its error grows as the grid shrinks.*

The brief's stated band of **$0.10–$0.55 per location per month** is confirmed: it corresponds to 1–4 tracked keywords at a 7×7 weekly grid.

### 3.2 The depth-billing trap is real and it is asymmetric between Organic and Maps

**Conclusion: Organic SERP is billed per 10 results; Maps SERP is billed per 100 results. Configure organic at depth 20 and Maps at depth 100.**

DataForSEO changed organic billing on **19 September 2025**: *"the `depth` (the number of results) covered by the base price of Organic SERP APIs changes to `10`."* The help-centre FAQ states the resulting prices verbatim:

| Organic depth | Standard / normal | Standard / high | Live |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 results (1 page) | $0.0006 | $0.0012 | $0.002 |
| 100 results (10 pages) | $0.006 | $0.012 | $0.02 |

So tracking to position 100 costs **$6.00 per 1,000 checks**, not $0.60 — a 10× multiplier, confirmed at the vendor's own FAQ. Top-20 (2 pages) costs **$0.0012 per check** — note this is a *linear interpolation*: the FAQ publishes only the 10-result and 100-result rows ($0.0006 and $0.006), which are exactly 1× and 10× a $0.0006 page, so 2 pages = $0.0012. Confirm the depth-20 line on the first real invoice. (The FAQ also notes the base depth is 10 results *"for most search engines"*, with Naver an exception at 15 — irrelevant to us, we query Google.)

Maps is billed differently: the pricing page gives *"Price per 1 SERP page: $0.0006"* (also stated as $600 per 1M SERPs, with Priority $0.0012 and Live $0.002) and the `task_post` documentation defines the Maps page as *"up to 100 results."* The depth FAQ confirms the asymmetry in its own words: the September 2025 revision applies *"exclusively to Organic search-type endpoints"* and **does not extend to the Google Maps SERP API**. There is no reason to run Maps at less than depth 100 — deeper data is free within the unit.

**Engineering consequence:** the depth parameter must be a per-endpoint constant in code, not a caller-supplied integer, or a single careless `depth=100` on organic multiplies the platform's largest line item by five (§6, requirement 12).

**This is not hypothetical — the trap is already the shipped default.** `backend/app/modules/rank_tracker/provider.py:68` sets `DEFAULT_DEPTH = 100`, and every `fetch_serp` / `estimated_cost` signature in that module defaults to it (lines 131, 134, 253, 265, 353, 365, 458, 474). Worse, the module's own price function is stale against this finding: `_dfs_cost` (`provider.py:214-217`) computes `blocks = ceil(depth / 100)`, i.e. it still bills DataForSEO organic in 100-result blocks — the pre-19-September-2025 model. At the shipped `DEFAULT_DEPTH = 100` it therefore prices one block where DataForSEO now charges ten, understating the platform's largest line item by **6×** against the configured `rank_tracker_cost_estimate = 0.001` (`config.py:517`). Requirement 11 must change the default, not merely guard it.

The IDs-Only SKU returns only `name`, `id`, `attributions`, `consumerAlert`, `movedPlace`, `movedPlaceId`, `nextPageToken` — an ordered list of opaque place IDs. `locationBias`/`locationRestriction` accept a circle with a centre lat/lng and a radius of *"between 0.0 and 50000.0"* metres, so a grid point is expressible. In principle you could determine your own client's rank at each grid point for free, because you already know your client's `place_id`.

**It is still the wrong source, for three reasons that price cannot fix:**

1. **You cannot store the competitor names, which is most of the product.** Google Maps Platform ToS §3.2.3(a) *No Scraping* prohibits pre-fetching, indexing, storing, resharing or rehosting Google Maps Content outside the Services, and prohibits copying and saving business names, addresses, or user reviews. A geo-grid whose value proposition is *"here is who outranks you at each pin, over time"* is a stored index of business names.
2. **You cannot render it on our own map.** ToS §3.2.3(e) *No Use With Non-Google Maps* prohibits displaying or using Places content on a non-Google map; the Places API Service Specific Terms §14.2 repeat it. A heatmap on Leaflet/Mapbox/OSM would be a breach; forcing it onto a Google Map adds the Dynamic Maps SKU and Google Maps branding into a white-label agency product.

You can therefore store place ID values indefinitely."* The 12-month refresh guidance is on the separate **Place IDs** page, not the policies page — also verified verbatim: *"Google recommends refreshing place IDs if they are more than 12 months old"* because *"Place IDs may change due to updates on the Google Maps database,"* and they can be refreshed *"at no charge"* by a Place Details request specifying only the place-ID field.

### 3.5 GSC gives 16 months and the bulk export does not backfill — this is the one irreversible deadline in the platform

**Conclusion: archive on connect or the history is gone. Google confirms both halves at primary sources.**

Retention: Google's own documentation states *"Search Console keeps data for the last 16 months"* and *"Search Console data is available in Search Console and in Analytics 48 hours after it is collected by Search Console."*

No backfill: the bulk data export documentation states *"If you want to see historical data that precedes your initial setup, use the Search Console API or the reports,"* and *"The first export will happen up to 48 hours after your successful configuration in Search Console. The first export includes data for the day of the export."*

Quota is not the constraint. The Search Console API limits page gives Search Analytics **1,200 QPM per site**, **1,200 QPM per user**, **40,000 QPM and 30,000,000 QPD per project**; URL Inspection is **2,000 QPD / 600 QPM per site**; everything else **20 QPS / 200 QPM per user**.

So a 16-month day-by-day archival pull for a new client is roughly 480 API calls and completes in minutes. **There is no technical reason not to do this on connect, and every day of delay permanently destroys one day of the oldest history.**

### 3.8 DataForSEO: a 99.95% marketing claim, a best-efforts contract, and generous rate limits

**Conclusion: DataForSEO's "99.95% uptime" is a blog claim, not a contractual SLA. Its Terms of Service contain only a best-efforts availability clause. Plan for outages; do not rely on a remedy.**

The ToS say: *"DataForSEO does its very best to make the Service available as detailed in the applicable Service plan, except for: (a) planned downtime (of which You will be notified in advance), or (b) any unavailability caused by circumstances beyond Our reasonable control."* That is a best-efforts clause with no uptime percentage, no credits and no remedy.

Their engineering blog separately claims *"we've been able to achieve a guaranteed 99.95% uptime"* and *"we never charge for requests that failed due to our system error."* Neither statement appears in the contract. Treat 99.95% as an observed figure, not a promise.

Rate limits (help centre): **2,000 API calls per minute** general; **up to 100 tasks per `task_post` request** (tasks beyond 100 return error `40006`); **30 simultaneous requests** for database-backed APIs (Labs, Backlinks, OnPage, Content Analysis, Trends, AI Optimization). At 100 tasks per POST this is 200,000 tasks/minute — orders of magnitude above our peak.

Storage rights: the ToS are **silent** on the customer's right to store, cache, resell or redistribute returned data. §7.1 restricts using SERP data *"to compete with or adversely affect"* search engine providers. There is no clause prohibiting white-labelling or client resale. Minimum deposit: **$50**.

### 3.9 The current cost ledger cannot see most of its own spend

**Conclusion: three shipped defects make the platform's cost reporting materially wrong today. All three must be fixed before any grid or rank-tracker volume ships.**

1. **`cost_log.cost` is `numeric(10, 2)`** (`db/migrations/0006_cost.sql:57`). A DataForSEO Maps grid point costs $0.0006 and logs as **$0.00**. `app/services/pricing.py:75` computes to six decimal places; the precision is discarded at insert (`app/services/cost_store.py:60-75`). At 58,800 grid queries per month the entire geo-grid line item currently records as zero dollars. Migration `0044_cost_budget_numeric.sql` addressed the *adjacent* bug on `client_budgets.cap`/`.spent` — and did not touch `cost_log.cost`.

   **`0044` only half-fixed its own column, and this record originally overstated it.** `0044` widened `client_budgets.cap`/`.spent` from `integer` to `numeric(10,2)`, which rescues a $0.15 content charge but *still rounds every sub-cent charge to zero* — a $0.0006 grid point and a $0.0012 rank check both accumulate as $0.00 in `spent`. So the per-client cap check at `cost_gate.py:145-147` can never trip on the two highest-volume line items in the entire model, even after `cost_log.cost` is widened. **Requirement 15 must migrate `client_budgets.cap` and `.spent` alongside `cost_log.cost`, or the cap enforcement stays broken for exactly the traffic this track exists to meter.** Note also that `0044`'s own header comment claims *"Only the org-wide daily spend-stop … actually bites today"* — that comment is stale; defect 2 below shows the daily stop does not bite at all.
2. **The daily stop is not enforced.** `cost_settings.daily_stop numeric(10,2) not null default 75` exists (`db/migrations/0006_cost.sql:39`) and is surfaced in the UI (`backend/app/schemas/command_center.py:109`), but `CostGate.evaluate` (`backend/app/services/cost_gate.py:121-149`) checks halt → dial → cache → per-client cap and **never reads `daily_stop`**. The module docstring records the removal deliberately: *"The old per-day dollar spend-stop THRESHOLD was removed - the halt replaces it."* The R5 contract requires it back.
3. **`CostGate.run()` writes estimates into the ledger as actuals.** `cost_gate.py:157-167` calls `commit(ctx, ctx.estimated_cost)` for an allowed call. ### 5.4 The grid cache model

**The cache key.** `(provider, keyword_normalised, lat_4dp, lng_4dp, zoom, date)`.

**Rounding precision: 4 decimal places, and here is what that costs in accuracy.** DataForSEO accepts *"the maximum number of decimal digits for latitude and longitude: 7."* We round to 4.

```
R = 6,371,008.7714 m           (IUGG mean Earth radius, R1)
1e-4 degree of latitude  = R × (1e-4 × π/180) = 11.12 m
1e-4 degree of longitude = 11.12 × cos(φ) m   (8.52 m at φ = 40°, 6.92 m at φ = 51.5°)
```

Maximum positional error from rounding is therefore **±5.6 m**. Against a 7×7 grid at a 1.5 km step, that is **0.37% of the grid spacing** — invisible in the result. Three decimal places (111 m) would be too coarse: in a dense urban core the pack can change within one block. Five places (1.1 m) buys nothing, because Google's own local ranking does not resolve at metre scale, and it collapses the cache hit rate to near zero by making every coordinate unique. **4dp is the point where the cache still works and the accuracy loss is undetectable.**

**The lattice requirement — without this the hit rate is zero.** If each client's grid is centred on that client's own business pin, two clients in the same city generate two entirely disjoint sets of coordinates and share nothing, at any density. The cache only works if grids are **snapped to a shared per-metro lattice**: each metro has a stored anchor coordinate, and every grid point is `anchor + (i, j) × step`, with the client's own grid selected as the 7×7 window of lattice cells nearest that client's pin. **This is the single design decision that makes the grid cache possible and it must be built in from the first migration, because retrofitting it invalidates every stored coordinate and every historical comparison.**

**Hit-rate model.** Two clients share a cache entry only when they share (keyword, lattice cell, zoom, date). Let `p` be the probability that a given co-metro client pair shares any given query, decomposed as:

```
p = P(same keyword) × P(overlapping lattice window)
```

For a mixed-vertical agency: co-metro clients are usually in different verticals, so keyword overlap ≈ **0.25**; co-metro clients are typically 5–20 km apart while a 7×7 grid at a 1.5 km step spans ~9 km, so window overlap ≈ **0.30**. Therefore `p ≈ 0.075`.

Approximating set-union deduplication with a mean-field expression (**stated as an approximation, not a derivation**) — for a metro with `c` clients, the expected distinct fraction is `1 / (1 + (c−1)·p)`:

| Metro size `c` | Metros | Clients | Distinct fraction | Hit rate |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 2 | 16 | 0.656 | **34.4%** |
| 5 | 4 | 20 | 0.769 | **23.1%** |
| 3 | 8 | 24 | 0.870 | **13.0%** |
| 2 | 10 | 20 | 0.930 | **7.0%** |
| 1 | 20 | 20 | 1.000 | **0.0%** |
| | **44** | **100** | | **14.6% blended** |

```
Saving at 100 clients:  $35.28 × 0.146 = $5.15 / month = $61.80 / year
```

**Be honest about this: $5.15/month is not a business case.** The cache's real value at 100 diffuse clients is elsewhere and is still worth building:

- **Idempotency.** A grid scan that partially fails and is retried costs $0 for the points already fetched. At an 8% partial-failure rate: `58,800 × 0.08 × $0.0006 = $2.82/month` — comparable to the sharing saving, and it also removes the pressure to make retries "safe" by making them rare.
- **Re-read is free.** Viewing, re-rendering, exporting to PDF and regenerating a client report never re-queries.
- **Rate-limit headroom.** DataForSEO's 30-simultaneous ceiling on DB-backed endpoints and the 2,000/min general ceiling are consumed by distinct queries only.
- **It compounds with density.** This is the sensitivity that matters:

| Scenario | `p` | Metro shape | Blended hit rate | Monthly saving |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| Modelled (mixed vertical, diffuse) | 0.075 | 44 metros | 14.6% | $5.15 |
| Concentrated (5 metros × 20 clients) | 0.075 | 5 metros | 58.8% | $20.75 |
| Single-vertical agency (all plumbers), concentrated | 0.300 | 5 metros × 20 | 85.1% | $30.02 |

**Verdict: build the cache — it costs almost nothing to build, it is the only lever that gets better as Daniel grows, and it makes retries free. Do not put its saving in the pricing model at 100 clients.**

**How the cache interacts with the Places 30-day limit: it does not, by design.** The grid cache never holds Places API content, because the grid never calls the Places API. ### 5.5 Engineering care on the grid

**Geodesic offsets, not naive degree spacing.** One degree of longitude is `cos(φ)` times one degree of latitude. Using the same degree step for both axes produces a grid that is compressed east–west by that factor: at London (φ = 51.5°, cos φ = 0.6225) a nominally 5 km × 5 km grid is actually **5.0 km × 3.1 km** — a 38% error, and it silently distorts every "visibility radius" claim made from it. At Miami (φ = 25°) the same bug is only a 9% error, which is why it survives testing in warm markets and breaks in northern ones.

The correct construction, for grids up to ~50 km (local tangent-plane approximation, accurate to well under 0.1% at that scale):

```python
import math

R_EARTH_M = 6_371_008.7714          # IUGG mean Earth radius R1, metres

def grid_points(lat0: float, lng0: float, n: int, step_m: float):
    """(2n+1) x (2n+1) grid, step_m metres between adjacent points."""
    dlat_per_m = (1.0 / R_EARTH_M) * (180.0 / math.pi)
    dlng_per_m = (1.0 / (R_EARTH_M * math.cos(math.radians(lat0)))) * (180.0 / math.pi)
    for i in range(-n, n + 1):
        for j in range(-n, n + 1):
            lat = lat0 + (i * step_m) * dlat_per_m
            lng = lng0 + (j * step_m) * dlng_per_m     # NOTE: cos(lat0), not cos(lat)
            yield round(lat, 4), round(lng, 4)         # 4dp = 11.12 m N-S, ~8.5 m E-W
```

**Use `cos(lat0)` — the grid's anchor latitude — not `cos(lat_ij)`.** Using the per-row latitude gives a marginally more equal-area grid but destroys column alignment, and column alignment is what makes lattice snapping and cache sharing possible. The cost of the constant-`lat0` choice: for a 7×7 grid at a 1.5 km step at φ = 51.5°, the grid spans φ = 51.4595° to 51.5405°, and the east–west spacing error between its northern and southern edges is `cos(51.4595°)/cos(51.5405°) = 1.0018` — **0.18%**, or 2.7 m on a 1,500 m step. Negligible. Do not add a `pyproj` dependency for it. *(Corrected 2026-08-23: previously stated as 1.0023 / 0.23% / 3.4 m. The bounding latitudes were right; the ratio was overstated by ~30%. The conclusion is unchanged and the error ran in the conservative direction.)*

**Zoom must be pinned constant across a grid.** DataForSEO's `location_coordinate` takes `"latitude,longitude,zoom"`; *"if zoom is not specified, 17z will be applied as a default value,"* with a *"minimum value for zoom: 3z"* and a *"maximum value for zoom: 21z."* Zoom sets the viewport Google searches from, so it changes the candidate set. **Two points captured at different zooms are not comparable and must never appear in the same heatmap or the same trend line.** Requirements: store `zoom` on the grid *definition* (not per point), include it in the cache key, write it into every Evidence row, and make the renderer **refuse** to draw a grid whose points carry mixed zoom values rather than drawing a misleading one. Default to `17z` (the vendor default) pending calibration — see §8, item 5.

**Raw provider responses stored as Evidence.** Every coordinate query persists its full, unmodified JSON response, addressed by content hash, with the exact request parameters that produced it. When Daniel or a client disputes a ranking, the answer must be *"here is the exact provider response for that coordinate at that zoom on that date"* — not a recomputation. This is also what makes a provider swap survivable: the old series remains reproducible from its own evidence.

### 5.7 One source per metric type, never mixed, always labelled

Every metric row records its provenance inline:

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `source` | provider enum: `dataforseo` · `serper` · `places` · `gsc` · `ga4` · `psi` · `audit_engine` |
| `source_endpoint` | e.g. `serp/google/maps/task_get` |
| `source_params_hash` | sha256 of the normalised request params — **must include `zoom`, `depth`, `language_code`, `device`, `location_coordinate`** |
| `collected_at` | timestamptz |
| `evidence_id` | FK to the raw response blob |

**Provider-swap protocol.**

1. A swap **opens a new series**. `metric_series(id, client_id, metric_type, source, started_at, ended_at)`; rows point at a series. History is never rewritten and never re-attributed.
2. **Charts render the boundary.** A visible discontinuity marker at the swap date, both segments labelled with their source.
3. **No trend line crosses a source boundary.** Deltas, "positions gained/lost", and share-of-voice are computed within a series only. This is enforced in the query layer, not left to the chart component.
4. **Optional 14-day dual-run overlap.** Run both providers, measure and publish the offset as a caveat on the boundary. **Never** use the measured offset to "correct" one series into the other — that manufactures data.
5. The dial's `provider` field (`backend/app/schemas/cost.py:15-17`) is already a closed enum. Extend it rather than passing free strings, so an unregistered provider is unswitchable-on — the same protection the dial keys already have.

### 5.8 The cost gate contract, and exactly what must change

**Contract: dial → cache → per-client cap → daily stop → call → log ACTUAL.** Blocks are loud. Expensive actions are pre-flighted. Modelled spend is reconciled against actual monthly.

Current implementation (`backend/app/services/cost_gate.py:121-149`): `halt → dial → cache → per-client cap → call`, logging the estimate on the `run()` path. The changes required are enumerated in §6 requirements 15–24.
