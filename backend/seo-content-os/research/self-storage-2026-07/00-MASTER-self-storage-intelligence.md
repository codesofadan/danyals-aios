## 1. The single defining fact of the vertical

Storage is a near-commodity: a 10x10 at Facility A holds the same boxes as a 10x10 at Facility B. When the product is undifferentiated, the only durable ranking-and-trust edge is **proof of the specific physical place and the specific operating practice** - exactly the first E of E-E-A-T, and exactly what a SERP-remix tool cannot produce. The governing test (Law 16, the paste test): if a sentence stays true when pasted onto the facility across town, it is asserted and it fails. "Clean, safe, secure storage" survives the paste and is dead. "Over 40 HD cameras recording continuously across entry points, hallways, and parking lots" (Big Tex, real) breaks on the paste and is proven.

The winning storage page = **national-brand page structure + directory-grade live inventory + the one thing neither competitor has: dated first-party local proof.**

**Single vs multi-facility (the collapse rule).** One location: the homepage IS the facility page; unit sizes shown inline as a section, no state/city/type/size pages (verified: packitup.us, one location, Kings Mountain NC). Do not spin up separate axis pages for a one-location client - they become thin. Multi-facility: real state > city > facility URL tree, multiplied by unit-size and storage-type axes at the city level. This is where index-bloat and doorway risk live; the defense is a unique local block per node.

**Silo discipline (penalty-proof).** Facility page is the hub every axis links back into. Every facility page carries a genuinely unique "About This Facility" block naming local roads/landmarks (the doorway-defeating block). Every city hub carries unique city prose (population, neighborhoods, local use-cases). Type/size pages differentiate by real local inventory and price, never a restated national definition.

**The two hardest non-thin contracts:**

*Unit-size page must contain:* exact dimensions + derived area/volume ("10x20x8 = 1600 cu ft"; "100 sq ft / 800 cu ft"); a physical-world equivalent ("one-car garage", "large shed", "half a one-stall garage"); recommended truck size ("22-foot moving truck"); room-grouped what-fits inventory (bedroom/living/kitchen/business/garage), real item lists not adjectives; a box-count estimate with an honesty hedge ("144 boxes max, 100 is more realistic"); price context + "find one near me"; up/down cross-links to adjacent sizes; a photo/video of the size.

*Storage-type page must contain:* precise definition with a number (climate control = 55-80 F); a "do I need this" decision block; the sub-types (climate-controlled vs air-cooled/evaporative vs heated); a specific sensitive-item list (cameras, vinyl, instruments, wood furniture, electronics); common sizes it comes in; prep/pack/move tips; a cost-vs-standard note; a type FAQ; links to city instances + sibling types.

## 4. Schema / structured data patterns (dossier 03)

**`schema.org/SelfStorage` is real.** Hierarchy: `Thing > Organization/Place > LocalBusiness > SelfStorage`. Description: "A self-storage facility." It defines **no properties of its own** - all usable properties are inherited from LocalBusiness. There is no storage-specific `unitSize`/`climateControlled`/`gateHours` property; those facts use `amenityFeature`, `openingHoursSpecification`, and free-text `description`. Google says use the most specific LocalBusiness subtype, so use `SelfStorage`, not bare `LocalBusiness`.

**Facility entity (safe, high-value) - emit on facility/location/single-location homepage:**
- `name`, `url`, `image` (real dated facility photos), `telephone`, `priceRange` (free text, must match visible band), `currenciesAccepted`, `paymentAccepted`
- `address` (`PostalAddress`: `streetAddress`, `addressLocality`, `addressRegion`, `postalCode`, `addressCountry`)
- `geo` (`GeoCoordinates`: `latitude`, `longitude`), `hasMap`
- `areaServed` (real `City`/`Place`/ZIPs only, matching service-area copy - inflated lists = doorway/mismatch risk)
- `openingHoursSpecification` for **OFFICE hours only** (`dayOfWeek`, `opens`, `closes`)
- `amenityFeature`: array of `LocationFeatureSpecification` (`name` + boolean `value`), one per real attribute (climate-controlled units, drive-up access, 24-hour gate access, video surveillance, individually alarmed units). Mark `value: false` honestly for features the facility lacks.
- `sameAs` (GBP, Yelp, Facebook), optional `@id` so other page schemas reference the same facility node
- **Access-hours nuance:** storage has two clocks. Map office/leasing hours to `openingHoursSpecification`; express 24hr gate access via `amenityFeature` ("24-hour gate access", `value: true`), NEVER as `opens: 00:00 / closes: 23:59` on the office spec.

**Unit-type page (only when a real live price is on the page):** `Product` + `Offer` with `businessFunction: http://purl.org/goodrelations/v1#LeaseOut` (rental, not sale) + `UnitPriceSpecification` (`price`, `priceCurrency: USD`, `unitCode: MON`, `billingDuration: 1`, `billingIncrement: 1`). `availability` enum (`InStock`/`SoldOut`/`LimitedAvailability`) must reflect true inventory. Range page: `AggregateOffer` (`lowPrice`/`highPrice`/`offerCount`/`priceCurrency`), values equal to the on-page table. **There is NO self-storage pricing rich result in Google Search** (merchant-listing requires a purchasable retail product; a rental unit is not one). Use as entity/AEO signal only, never expect a SERP price chip.

**`BreadcrumbList` on every deep page** (`itemListElement` > `ListItem` with `position`/`name`/`item`) - the one reliably-rendered rich result for storage.

**`FAQPage`** (`mainEntity` > `Question` > `acceptedAnswer` > `Answer` > `text`) - keep for AI/answer-engine extraction, but **FAQ rich results were deprecated 2026-05-07** (after Aug-2023 gov/health-only restriction). Produces NO SERP dropdown. Never sell it as a rich result.

**HARD DO-NOT-EMIT LIST:**
1. **Self-serving `aggregateRating`/`review` on the facility or brand.** Since Sept 2019, Google does not render stars when the entity controls reviews about itself. Exact rule (review-snippet doc): "If the entity that's being reviewed controls the reviews about itself, their pages that use LocalBusiness or any other type of Organization structured data are ineligible for the star review feature" - directly in structured data OR via an embedded third-party widget. `SelfStorage` is a LocalBusiness/Organization subtype = squarely inside the wall. Drive reviews through GBP instead.
2. Fabricated/stale `availability` or prices that differ from the page.
3. Bare `Offer` without `LeaseOut` (mislabels a rental as a sale).
4. Inflated `areaServed`; amenity/`priceRange` claims contradicting visible copy.
5. `FAQPage` promised as a rich result.

Enforcement stakes: markup must be visible and up-to-date; misleading/fake-review/invisible markup risks losing rich-result eligibility or a structured-data manual action (Google SD policies).

## 5. Conversion / CRO element set and truthful-urgency rules (dossier 04)

**Three distinct conversion actions (not interchangeable), with hard numbers:**
- **Reserve** (free, no card, no obligation - a lead only): reservation -> rental converts 40-80% (best operators 50-60%). A reservation is worth at best ~0.5-0.6 of a rental.
- **Rent online** (pay + e-sign, unit is theirs now): online-rental completion averages ~31% (~70% abandon).
- **Call / in-person**: call -> sale ~35% (the highest-converting single action).

**Primary CTA splits by intent (the 5-6 Ds of demand: Death, Divorce, Dislocation, Downsizing, Decluttering, Distribution):** URGENT move -> lead with **Rent Online Now** + click-to-call co-primary. PLANNED declutter -> lead with **Reserve free/no-card/no-obligation** (locks the daily-fluctuating web rate + promo). One primary CTA matched to page intent; keep the other present but subordinate; click-to-call always one tap away on mobile.

**The non-negotiable conversion elements the category actually tests:**
1. Live per-unit price + availability on the page (the #1 lever: "facilities with live inventory outperform those without"). "Call for pricing" is structurally lower-converting.
2. A visible, real move-in special.
3. Transparent fee disclosure (admin fee, no deposit, month-to-month).
4. The tenant protection plan / insurance line item (the margin engine and a disclosure trap).
5. Trust signals near the decision (reviews, security specs, real photos).
6. Mobile-first e-commerce checkout (1-3 steps, autofill, instant SMS/email confirm).

**Real move-in specials + fine print:** Public Storage runs "$1 First Month", "50% off first month", "2nd month free", tagged "LIMITED TIME OFFER" with a disclosed **$29 administrative fee** ($29 is Public Storage's number, not a default). CubeSmart runs first-month-free / half-off / two-months-free, and its terms explicitly state rent promos "do not apply to taxes, insurance/protection plan fees, administrative fees, or other fees". "Free" never means $0 at move-in.

**Web rate is a teaser, not a locked price:** in-store/in-place rates can surpass web rates by 30-50% via existing-customer rate increases (ECRI). Never present a web rate as permanent; a "no increase for a year" claim ships only if contractually true.

**Truthful vs fabricated urgency (the line the gate polices - FTC "Bringing Dark Patterns to Light", 2022, Section 5):**
- TRUTHFUL: "Only X units left" ONLY when wired to real-time PMS inventory; a "LIMITED TIME OFFER" with a real enforced end date; "reserve to lock today's web rate" (rates genuinely change daily).
- FABRICATED (forbidden): a resetting/per-session countdown timer; a hard-coded "only 1 left" not wired to inventory (the FTC's own illegal-scarcity example is "Only two left in stock" shown "when stock is plentiful"); presenting a web rate as locked/permanent. The FTC test is the reasonable-consumer standard, and "false urgency is a material misrepresentation."

**G13/CONVERT rules (storage overlay):** no scarcity claim without a live verifiable PMS basis (ban hard-coded counts); no countdown timer unless it targets a real fixed server-enforced end-datetime that does not reset; any "free"/"$1"/"50% off" must disclose still-payable fees in-copy (admin fee + required protection plan are not waived; never imply "$0 due at move-in"); admin fee/deposit/month-to-month stated truthfully from `brand.yaml`; web rate never presented as locked; reservation copy stays honest (a hold, not a confirmed rental); page price/promo must equal checkout price.

## 6. Voice profile and the storage cliche blocklist (dossier 05)

**Two registers, never crossed.** BACK-of-house trade lexicon (ECRI, in-place vs move-in rate, overlock, delinquency, lien, auction) - read it to sound competent, never write it to renters. FRONT-of-house register (the counter manager / owner review reply) - plain, declarative, locally anchored, owner-present, unsentimental about money. This is what public copy emulates.

**Renters solve a life event, not a storage problem.** They say "space"/"stuff", not "unit". They phrase everything around five worries in priority order: (1) what fits / what size, (2) how much / hidden fees, (3) is my stuff safe, (4) when can I get in (gate vs access vs office hours), (5) will my stuff survive (climate/humidity). Their fears are concrete and visceral (theft-then-denied-claim, "$15,000 of amplifiers... ruined" by water, "roach eggsacks... rat feces everywhere", fee-then-auction). Consequence - the **mechanism-not-reassurance rule**: every security/safety/climate claim must carry a specific defense (camera count, gate-hour window, disc-lock requirement, verified temp range, on-site manager hours) or it pattern-matches to the exact empty promise that burned them.

**Renter-to-operator translation table:** space -> unit; "temperature controlled"/"won't get moldy" -> climate-controlled; "drive right up" -> drive-up; "no long contract" -> month-to-month/no deposit; "the deal" -> move-in special; "extra fee they didn't tell me" -> admin fee / tenant protection plan; "they raised my rent" -> ECRI (never say this to renters). Meet their words, then anchor to the trade term without condescension.

**Trade-glossary precision pairs the copy must never fumble:** climate-controlled (temp + humidity) vs temperature-controlled (temp only); the three occupancies (unit/physical, square-footage, economic); gate hours vs access hours vs office hours (the #1 renter grievance vector); disc lock (shrouded shackle, bolt-cutter-resistant) vs cylinder lock (flush-mounted, drill-proof); tenant protection plan (operator-billed) vs tenant insurance (tenant-bought, state-licensed); reservation (no payment) vs rental (money moves); move-in/street rate vs in-place/achieved rate; overlock; lien / lien letter; prorate; PMS; unit mix.

### The self-storage cliche blocklist (Tier-1 hard ban, appends to `knowledge/voice/vocabulary-blocklist.md`)

Five banned families, each grounded in real published slogans (verbatim from soocial.com):
- **"Peace of mind" family:** peace of mind, "Peace of Mind, Piece by Piece", rest easy / sleep easy knowing your stuff is safe, worry-free / stress-free storage, "All Space, No Worry".
- **"Safe and secure" family:** "clean, safe and secure" (and any 2-of-3 permutation), "your belongings are safe with us", safe and sound, safe haven / safe harbor, "where security meets serenity", bare "keeping your belongings safe" with no camera count/gate spec/lock type.
- **"Space / storage solutions" family:** space solutions / storage solutions / your storage solution, "Space Solution for Every Situation", "your space, your way", room to breathe (as tagline), "more than just storage".
- **"Made easy / we've got you covered" family:** storage made easy/simple, we've got you covered, "storage you can trust", "where your belongings belong".
- **"Your belongings" possessive filler:** "your treasured belongings", "your precious memories", "your prized possessions" as decoration. Name what actually gets stored (the mountain bike, the seasonal inventory, the estate furniture).

**Tier-2 (context-banned, allowed only with a real number/spec):** secure/security, climate-controlled, affordable/competitive rates, convenient/convenient location, state-of-the-art, clean, spacious/roomy, flexible.

**Tier-3 structural anti-patterns:** the generic size-guide non-answer ("the size you need depends on how much you have"); the vague "your security is our top priority" paragraph; the "whether you're moving, downsizing, or decluttering" opener; fabricated urgency on the move-in special without a real live PMS count.

**The reframe rule:** reaching for a storage cliche means the sentence lacks a fact. "Clean, safe and secure" becomes "Gated, 24-camera lot; the manager lives on-site and cuts the lights at 10pm; every unit takes a disc lock we sell at the counter for $12." Only this business can truthfully write the second version - the entire point (Law 16).

## 7. Experience-marker taxonomy and the "license number" stack (dossier 06)

**Eight storage Experience markers** (bare claim -> proven form -> proof artifact -> brand.yaml field):

1. **Original facility photos** (the cleanest un-fakeable artifact). Shot-list: entry gate + keypad; hallway with real unit numbers; drive-up row; loading bay + carts; camera domes; well-lit night aisle; climate corridor; on-site office; manager on site. Every image resolves to a real first-party asset in `sources.md`, captioned to this facility. -> `eeat.media[]`.
2. **Security stack as concrete specs, not adjectives.** Real published example (Big Tex): "over 40 HD cameras... continuous live recording... stored for review"; every unit "its own personal door alarm", individually monitored; "unique key code both when entering and exiting". Plus perimeter fencing, motion-activated LED lighting, resident vs office-hours manager, disc locks, and a break-in-free counter (Capitola: "5,420 days without security breach"). -> body + `eeat.proof`.
3. **Climate as real numbers + the honest caveat.** "climate controlled" is NOT an industry-regulated term. Working spec: 50-80 F, humidity 45% or below, true climate control = HVAC AND dehumidifiers (StoragePug). Capitola publishes "50 to 80 degrees fahrenheit... constant humidity year-round". A 55-80F/30-50% range is a pattern, not this facility's number. -> product specs / `eeat.proof`.
4. **Live availability + real prices from the PMS** (proof the facility is operating). Where no live feed, the honest current published web rate with a dated stamp, never invented. -> live feed or dated rate table.
5. **Access hours vs gate hours as distinct published facts** (StorageMart: "Office hours are when the manager is onsite... Access hours are when you can access your storage unit"; 6am-10pm common, some 24-hour with approval). Never claim 24-hour access the gate does not grant. -> `access_hours`.
6. **Named manager + CSSM.** The Certified Self Storage Manager (SSA): 15-session course, 70% pass, printable trademarked certificate, renew every 2 years with 4 CE credits, $599 member/$799 non-member. Industry average is only 1.5 CSSMs per facility - genuinely distinctive. -> `eeat.team[]`, `schema.same_as`.
7. **Years in operation + local ties + community proof** (Capitola: "15 Years in Business", on-site local-history exhibit). -> `founded_year`, `eeat.differentiators[]`.
8. **Reviews with count/rating/platform/attribution.** SOCi study (~5M reviews, via StoragePug): +4.4% conversion per tenth-star, +2.8% per 10 reviews, +16.4% for responding to all vs none, +126% traffic top-3 pack, avg facility gets 5.8 new reviews/month. Cite real count/rating on a named platform (GBP). -> `eeat.reviews[]`.

**The storage "license number" stack (there is NO single universal storage license - "self storage businesses typically do not require specialized certifications", Wexford). Ranked most-verifiable first:**
1. **State Self-Service Storage insurance/agent license** (the closest true analog, required to sell tenant protection, which is effectively mandatory). California: Ins. Code 1758.75, issues a verifiable number, $323 fee, 2-year term. Parallel programs in OR, WA, MA, AL. This is the print-it number where held.
2. **City facility license** where it exists (NYC DCWP under Local Law 171 of 2025, separate license per building, fees ~$590/$443, $295 annual).
3. **The named manager's CSSM certificate** (third-party issued, dated, distinctive).
4. **Operational un-fakeables** (no external issuer but impossible to fake without lying): the live PMS feed, the per-tenant gate-access log, dated original facility photos/footage, the incrementing break-in-free counter.
5. **Baseline registrations** (business license, sales tax, zoning; ADA 5%/2% accessible-unit rule).

Never invent a number to fill the slot - empty = a missing-evidence flag, not a fabrication.

## 8. Compliance auto-fail rules with authority (dossier 07)

Two legal layers: federal FTC Act Section 5 + FTC guides (national), and state Self-Service Storage Facility Acts (per-state). Statute text verified this run for CA (Bus. & Prof. Code ss. 21700-21716), TX (Property Code ch. 59), FL (Statutes ch. 83 **Part III**, ss. 83.801-83.809). `STATE-DEP` = the rule's existence/threshold depends on the client's state; the gate reads `brand.yaml.state` before firing.

| # | Banned / required pattern | Disposition | Authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | "insurance"/"coverage"/"insured"/"policy"/"premium" used for an in-house **protection plan** | AUTO-FAIL (STATE-DEP) | Heckart v. A-1 Self Storage (Cal. 2018, protection plans are not insurance); NM levied a fine for the opposite |
| C2 | Say "insurance" without the operator licensed to sell insurance in that state | AUTO-FAIL (STATE-DEP) | Tenant insurance is a state-regulated licensed product |
| C3 | "safe and secure", "your belongings are safe", "protected", "theft-proof", "100% secure", "guaranteed secure" | AUTO-FAIL | Dilbeck v. Yates (GA 1992): absolute security claim can void the lease liability cap as fraud |
| C4 | Any break-in/theft/fire history claim ("we've never had a break-in") | AUTO-FAIL | The exact Dilbeck fraud fact pattern |
| C5 | Bare "climate controlled" paired with "dry"/"moisture-free"/"prevents mold/mildew" when facility controls temperature only | AUTO-FAIL unless `brand.yaml` confirms humidity control | DiSanto v. Safeco (OH 2006); no standard definition |
| C6 | "First month free"/"$1 move-in"/"free" without in-line disclosure of admin fee, required protection/insurance, autopay, or minimum term (conditions in footnote = fail) | AUTO-FAIL | 16 CFR 251.1 (FTC "Free" guide: conditions "at the outset... in close conjunction with the offer") |
| C7 | "Free"/discount measured against an inflated non-bona-fide "regular price" | AUTO-FAIL | 16 CFR 251.1 (regular price = openly sold prior 30 days) |
| C8 | "Rate locked", "price never increases", "guaranteed rate for life", "no rate increases" on month-to-month | AUTO-FAIL unless contractually guaranteed in `brand.yaml` | FTC Act s.5 / state UDAP; ECRI reality |
| C9 | Promo/intro-rate copy implying permanence | REQUIRE promo duration + "subject to change"; hard first-page requirement in CA for agreements on/after 2026-01-01 | CA SB 709 (adds Bus.&Prof. s.21715.2; disclosure-only, does NOT cap rates) |
