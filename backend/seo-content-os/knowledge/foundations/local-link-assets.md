# Local Link Assets: What Earns Links, and How We Measure It

Link signals are roughly **15% of local relevance** (Whitespark Local Search Ranking Factors 2026, weight from secondary coverage of the gated report, directional not Google-published). It is the one significant ranking category this writing system currently does nothing for: every conversion page competes for links it gives no one a reason to grant. This file owns the **strategy layer** for that gap - what actually earns a local link in 2026, which asset types are outreach-worthy, and how the result is measured. The **writing spec** for the assets themselves lives in `knowledge/playbooks/local-asset.md`; read this file for the why and the what, that file for the how-to-write.

Scope note: this is a **white-hat link-earning** file. Genuinely useful assets plus honest outreach, plus reclamation of the client's own unlinked brand mentions and lost citations. **No PBNs, no paid link schemes, no link exchanges at scale, no automated tiered links, no scholarship-bait pages, no directory-spam.** That is a doctrine hard line (Law 8), not a preference. Manufactured links are discounted at best and a manual-action risk at worst, and the tactic that still works is the boring one: make something worth pointing at, then tell the people who would point at it. If a brief asks for bought or automated links, refuse and cite the doctrine.

The honest constraint stated up front: **content alone cannot move the 15% link category.** An asset earns links only when it is genuinely link-worthy *and* someone does the outreach. This system writes the asset and identifies the audience; the client or the operator does the human outreach. Selling "we will write a page and links will come" is dishonest. The page is necessary, not sufficient.

## 1. What actually earns a local link in 2026

Local links come from a small, real set of sources. Ordered by durability:

1. **Original local data.** A number no one else has: seasonal service-call patterns, local failure-rate analysis, real local price data, a survey of local customers. A publisher can only cite the number by linking the source, so data is the most reliably link-earning asset that still works. This is the `local-asset.md` data study and cost guide.
2. **Genuine local usefulness.** A resource a real local person needs and would share or link: a neighborhood homeowner's guide, a cost guide with real local numbers, a curated list of verified local resources. Usefulness-independent-of-the-sale is the test; a thinly veiled sales page earns nothing.
3. **Community involvement (real).** Local sponsorships, event participation, charity work, chamber and trade-association membership, scholarships that are real programs (not bait pages). These earn links from the organizations involved and are a legitimate, evidenced unique-content element (Sterling Sky lists "local charity/sponsorships with external links" and "Chamber membership" among real unique-page signals; sterlingsky.ca, read 2026-07-20; verify). The link follows a real relationship, not a transaction.
4. **"Best of / top local" list inclusion.** Being named in a third-party "best [service] in [city]" listicle is a called-out 2026 signal with impact on both traditional and AI search (Whitespark 2026; verify). This is a **digital-PR outcome**, not something the client publishes about itself: you earn inclusion by being genuinely good, being reviewable, and being reachable to the journalists and bloggers who compile these lists. The client's own genuinely-useful resource pages build the goodwill and reachability that support the ask.
5. **Relationship and reclamation links.** Suppliers, partners, trade bodies, and local businesses the client already works with; plus **reclaiming the client's own unlinked brand mentions** (someone named the business without linking) and **fixing lost citations** (a citation pointing at a dead or redirected URL). Reclamation is pure white-hat and often the fastest real-link win available. It is in scope; manufacturing links on sites you control is not.

What no longer earns links (evidence-based negatives, do not build):
- **Thin scholarship-bait / "resource" pages** built only to solicit .edu links. The tactic is dead (lseo.com local link-building, read 2026-07-20; verify).
- **Generic "ultimate guide" rehashes** with no first-party substance (fails Law 15; earns nothing; scaled-content risk).
- **Directory-spam and paid-link networks** (Law 8 hard line).

## 2. The outreach-worthy asset types (mapped to the playbook)

Each asset type below maps to `local-asset.md` and to a real link audience. An asset with no plausible audience is a blog post, not a link asset; identify the audience before building.

| Asset type | What earns the link | Link audience to outreach | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Local data study** | Original data a publisher must link to cite | Local news, industry blogs, journalists covering the topic/region | Whitespark 15% links; original-data link-building consensus |
| **Local cost guide** | Real local price data no national page has | Homeowner/comparison resource pages, community forums; AI cost-query citations | Whitespark; Sterling Sky (first-party price data as unique content) |
| **"Best of / resource" page** | Genuinely useful curation of verified local resources | The listed parties, neighborhood/community sites | Sterling Sky (related-local-business recommendations) |
| **Neighborhood / local guide** | Deep, real local knowledge useful to residents | Neighborhood associations, community hubs, homeowner resources | Sterling Sky (local connection signals) |
| **Community-involvement page** | A real sponsorship/event/charity relationship | The organizations involved (chamber, charity, event) | Sterling Sky (sponsorships, chamber, charity with external links) |

The two elements the 2026-07-20 research flagged as underweighted in the system's existing playbooks, now first-class here: **first-party data** (price ranges, project counts, settlements where the vertical permits) and **verified related-local-business recommendations with external links**. Both are named by Sterling Sky (the strongest tactical source) as legitimate unique-content and link-earning signals.

## 3. The outreach loop (the non-content half, stated honestly)

The asset is half the job. The loop that turns it into links:

1. **Identify the audience before building** (this file's job, recorded in the asset's `sources.md`): who would cite or link this - which publishers, community sites, listed parties, or journalists. No audience, no asset.
2. **Build the asset** to the `local-asset.md` spec (real first-party substance, genuine utility, passes the information-gain gate).
3. **Reach out** (the operator/client, not this system): tell the identified audience the asset exists, why it is useful to them, with no manipulation and no link-buying. For data studies, this is a journalist pitch; for best-of and community pages, it is a heads-up to the listed/involved parties; for resource pages, it is outreach to relevant community hubs.
4. **Reclaim in parallel:** find the client's unlinked brand mentions and lost/broken citations and request the link or fix the target. This is often the fastest real win and needs no new asset.

This system owns steps 1 and 2 and flags step 4 targets. Steps 3 is human relationship work; the system does not automate outreach (no spam, no scale-mailing). Positioning the asset as "publish and links appear" is the dishonest version and is banned.

## 4. Measurement (what to track, and what not to claim)

An asset's KPI is **not** its own booked-lead rate. It is the links and citations it earns and routes. Track:

1. **Referring domains earned** to the asset (and to the domain overall), attributable to the asset and its outreach. The primary metric.
2. **Answer-engine citations:** the asset being named in AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Claude answers for its target informational/hybrid queries. Assets live in the informational/hybrid surface where AI answers dominate (92% of informational, 97% of hybrid local queries carry AI Overviews; Whitespark, read 2026-07-20; verify).
3. **"Best of" inclusions** won: the client named in third-party best-of lists over time (the digital-PR outcome).
4. **Reclamation wins:** unlinked mentions converted to links, broken citations fixed.
5. **Equity routed to money pages:** that the asset actually links out to conversion pages (a build check, not a lift metric), so earned authority reaches the pages that convert.

What not to claim:
- **Do not attribute ranking movement to a single asset.** Link effects are diffuse, delayed, and confounded; correlation-not-causation binds every ranking-factor study cited here. Report links and citations earned, not "this asset moved you to position 3."
- **Do not promise link volume.** Outreach is uncertain; a great asset can still earn few links if the audience is small or the outreach is thin. Report the pipeline (audience identified, outreach done, links landed), not a guaranteed number.
- **Watch the scaled-content smoke signal:** if assets are being published faster than they can be truthfully sourced, GSC index coverage ("Crawled / Discovered - currently not indexed" across the asset cohort) is the early warning that Google is treating them as low-value. One strong asset beats ten thin ones.

---

## 5. The rules

1. **White-hat only.** Genuinely useful assets, honest outreach, and reclamation of the client's own mentions/citations. No PBNs, paid schemes, exchanges at scale, automated tiers, scholarship-bait, or directory-spam (Law 8 hard line).
2. **First-party substance is the link magnet.** Original data, real local prices, real local knowledge, real relationships. No first-party input, no asset (the `local-asset.md` build-vs-skip gate).
3. **Content is necessary, not sufficient.** The asset earns links only with outreach; the system writes the asset and identifies the audience, the operator does the human outreach. Never sell "publish and links come".
4. **Identify the audience before building.** An asset with no plausible link audience is flagged, not built.
5. **Measure links and citations, not asset-level rankings.** Report referring domains, AI citations, best-of inclusions, reclamation wins; never claim a single asset moved a ranking.
6. **Route earned equity to money pages.** Every asset links out to the relevant conversion pages with descriptive anchors (`internal-linking.md`).
7. **Reclamation is always in scope and often the fastest win.** Find unlinked brand mentions and broken citations first; they need no new asset.
