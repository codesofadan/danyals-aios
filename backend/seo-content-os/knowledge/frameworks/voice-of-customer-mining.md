# Voice-of-Customer (VoC) Mining

Canonical spec. Defined once here. VoC is the **input** that fills every framework in this library, not a framework itself. PAS, AIDA, StoryBrand, and the FAQ are containers; the buyer's own words are what fill them.

## What it is
The method for sourcing the raw language a page is written in: the buyer's exact words for their problem, their anxiety, and the outcome they want. Copyhackers' core insight is that conversion copy moves the reader using **voice-of-customer data**, not a swipe file. In an AI-flooded web, the buyer's real words are the durable moat, because a model can synthesize a category but not the specific phrasing of a specific market's customers.

## When to use for local (emergency vs considered)
- **Before every page, both intents,** at the `/brief` and RESEARCH steps. VoC precedes drafting; you cannot mine it after.
- **Emergency:** mine the panic and cost language ("water coming through the ceiling," "no AC and a newborn in the house").
- **Considered:** mine the deliberation and fear-of-being-burned language ("worried I'll pick the wrong contractor," "last guy left a mess").

## The method (where to pull, how to extract, where to route)
**Where to pull:**
- The client's own reviews (Google, Facebook, Angi, BBB) - the highest-value source.
- The SME interview: what customers say on the phone, the most common thing they get wrong, the exact objection they raise.
- Recorded or recalled sales calls.
- Competitor reviews in the same local market (especially 3-star reviews, which name the real friction).
- "People Also Ask" and autocomplete for the target query.

**How to extract:**
- Capture the buyer's **exact phrasing verbatim**, not a paraphrase. "Little rocks in the gutter" is the asset; "granule loss" is the company's translation of it.
- Tag each snippet by type: problem language, anxiety/objection language, desired-outcome language, and trigger event.
- Prefer the specific and concrete over the general.

**Where to route it:**
- **Problem language -> PAS problem block and the H1** (`pas-and-pastor.md`, `copyhackers-hero-and-belief.md`).
- **Anxiety/objection language -> the FAQ and inline reassurances** (`objection-handling.md`) and StoryBrand's internal problem (`storybrand-sb7.md`).
- **Desired-outcome language -> AIDA desire/picture and the hero subline** (`aida-and-4ps.md`).

## PASS test
- The problem statement and at least the top objections are in **customer-verbatim language traceable to a real source** (a review, the SME, a call), not invented.
- Every mined snippet is **sourced** (which review, which interview) so it can be checked and is never a fabrication (Law 16).

## Anti-pattern
- Writing the problem block in the company's or the agency's words instead of the buyer's.
- Inventing plausible-sounding customer language with no source (a fabrication risk under Law 16).
- Treating a swipe file of generic copy as a substitute for the client's actual reviews.

## Evidence grade
**Craft consensus** (Copyhackers, CXL), foundational to conversion copywriting. No single controlled coefficient, but it is the input the strongest evidence depends on: real, specific, cited language is what powers information gain (Law 15), proven experience (Law 16), and the GEO citation lift (Law 17). Method, not a lift claim.
