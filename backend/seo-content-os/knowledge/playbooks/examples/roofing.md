## Roofing reality (why this vertical is its own animal)

Roofing is not plumbing with a different noun swapped in. Five facts reorder every page:

1. **The dominant query is storm-triggered and time-compressed.** A single hailstorm can spike "roof repair near me" and "hail damage [city]" overnight. The buyer who lands after a storm is in an insurance-claim mindset, not a price-shopping one. The money page is the intersection of a **dated local hail/wind event + the insurance-claim adjuster workflow**.

2. **High ticket, considered, trust-gated.** A roof replacement is $10k-30k+. Even the storm buyer is letting a stranger onto the house and into an insurance claim. Trust signals (license, manufacturer certification, real named crew, real warranty) are load-bearing, not decorative.

3. **Manufacturer certification is an externally verifiable moat.** GAF Master Elite (roughly the top 2-3% of roofers), CertainTeed SELECT ShingleMaster, Owens Corning Platinum Preferred: these are look-up-able in each manufacturer's contractor locator, and they gate the enhanced system warranties (GAF Golden Pledge, etc.) that a homeowner cannot get from an uncertified installer. This is the roofing equivalent of a license number: a hard, third-party-verifiable E-E-A-T signal. Use it. Never claim a certification the client does not hold.

4. **Materials are a specificity lever.** "Top-quality shingles" is empty. "GAF Timberline HDZ architectural asphalt," "24-gauge standing seam with a Kynar 500 finish," "Class 4 impact-rated shingle," "Galvalume substrate" are the words that prove the page was written by someone who does the work. Asphalt, metal, and tile each carry their own vocabulary.

5. **The local-proof moat is dated storm history + verifiable claim outcomes + real before/after.** The "gumbo clay soil" equivalent for roofing is a **named, dated local storm** ("the March 2020 tornado through East Nashville," "over 300 hail events a year on the Front Range," "hail well over two inches this spring") plus a real permit-and-supplement claim process and dated before/after photos of local roofs. That triad collapses the instant you swap the city. That collapse is the target (strip-the-city test, service-city playbook Section 5.3).

The single sharpest roofing insight: **the un-fakeable page is the one that fuses a dated local storm event, the insurance-claim adjuster process, and the manufacturer-certification-gated warranty into one flow.** A doorway clone can fake named neighborhoods now (an LLM will invent plausible ones), but it cannot produce a real claim outcome on a real roof under a real Golden Pledge warranty after a real dated hail event. Build every roofing money page on that triad.

### BAD

**B1 - HHH Roofing, Texas City** - https://hhhroofing.com/texas-city/
Fails: keyword-stuffed doorway. The title is a pipe-delimited keyword string and the body reuses Houston-area boilerplate with the city name swapped in.
Gate/law: fails strip-the-city + local-proof; matches the scaled-content-abuse "content contains search keywords" example.
Quotes:
- Title/intro: "Texas City Roofing | Roofer Texas City | Roof Replacement | Roof Repair | HHH Roofing"
- "Serving Houston and Surrounding Areas" (not even Texas City); formulaic headers "Average Cost of A New Roof in Texas City," "Why choose hhh roofing as your Texas City roofer?"
- No Texas City neighborhoods (no Bayou Vista/Westside), no local hail/permit content, no city-specific reviews or FAQ.
Roofing takeaway: a pipe-delimited keyword title is a spam tell a rater catches instantly. The service-in-city title formula is "[Service] in [City], [ST] | [Differentiator] | [Brand]," written for one human, not stuffed for a crawler.

**B2 - The AI-spun combo network (labeled pattern, not a single victim URL).**
The 2026 failure mode this playbook exists to prevent: a roofer generates 80-160 "[service] in [city]" pages in one pass (8 services x 15-20 towns), each convincingly specific (an LLM invents plausible neighborhoods and a plausible storm), but none tied to a real booked job, a real permit, a real dated event, or a real review that resolves to a Google profile. It passes a naive strip-the-city read yet fails the external-verifiability test, and it is the exact "using generative AI tools to generate many pages without adding value" example in Google's live scaled-content-abuse policy (developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies). Documented outcome for the pre-AI version of this pattern: a suburb-swap contractor network lost 80%+ of its doorway pages and a reported 60%+ of organic traffic after the March 2024 core-plus-spam update (secondary case studies, verify). The smoke detector: "Crawled/Discovered - currently not indexed" spreading across the combo-page cohort in Search Console.
Roofing takeaway: in roofing specifically, the verifiability that defeats this is the claim outcome. If you cannot attach a real permit number, a real adjuster-settled claim, or a dated before/after from that town, do not publish the page for that town yet (service-city playbook Section 5.6).

## The roofing scorecard (apply to every roofing page before it ships)

1. **Storm/claim triad present where relevant?** A dated local storm event + the insurance-claim adjuster process + the manufacturer-cert-gated warranty. This is the roofing money flow.
2. **Materials named, not adjectived?** "GAF Timberline HDZ," "24-gauge standing seam, Kynar 500," "Class 4 impact-rated," not "top-quality shingles."
3. **Certification named and verifiable?** GAF Master Elite / CertainTeed SELECT ShingleMaster / Owens Corning Platinum Preferred, at the exact tier the client actually holds. Never claim one they do not.
4. **Warranty stated with terms?** Manufacturer system warranty (Golden Pledge, etc.) + workmanship years. Not "we stand behind our work."
5. **License / bond as a literal look-up-able string.** State contractor license number in the trust bar.
6. **Real project proof.** Dated before/after from real local roofs, real named reviews, no stock crews.
7. **Strip-the-city test.** Remove the city and named anchors; if the roofing body still reads for any town, it fails.
8. **External verifiability.** At least one claim resolves outside the business: a permit, a settled claim, a manufacturer-locator listing, a review that resolves to a real profile.

A roofing page that misses 1-4 is generic; a location or service-city page that misses 7-8 is a doorway. Both are unfinished drafts.
