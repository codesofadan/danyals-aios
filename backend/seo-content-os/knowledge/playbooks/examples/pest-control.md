# Pest-Control Example Library (live-verified, 2026)

The vertical-specific example bank for the six local-SEO page types, built from real pest-control businesses. Pair it with the page-type playbooks in `../` and the doctrine (`../../doctrine/seo-system-doctrine.md`, especially **Law 8**: humanize with real facts, never with detector-evasion). Every GOOD example is a live URL verified this session; every BAD example is either a live URL or a real, labeled penalty pattern (this library never fabricates a named victim network).

## Why pest control is its own beast (read before using any example)

The local-specificity lever in pest control is not "we serve [City]." It is **pest pressure by climate, soil, and housing stock**, and that is the un-fakeable moat:

- **Pest pressure is geographic and seasonal, not generic.** Gulf Coast gumbo clay + 75% humidity = extreme subterranean and Formosan termite pressure and near-year-round mosquitoes; the arid Southwest is scorpions and rodents; the Northeast is carpenter ants and overwintering rodents. A page that names the local species, the local swarm window, and the local mechanism (clay soil cracking a slab foundation into a termite highway) passes the strip-the-city test by construction. A page that says "we handle all pests year-round" fails it.
- **Seasonal intent reorders the query.** Termite swarms (spring, March-June on the Gulf), mosquito season (late Feb-Dec in mild years), rodents pushing indoors (fall/winter), bed bugs (travel-driven, year-round). The money page should match the season it is read in.
- **Licensing is a hard E-E-A-T signal and it is a lookup-able public record.** Every state licenses applicators (Texas: TDA TPCL number; most states similar). Printing the real license number is the pest-control version of a plumber's master-plumber license: an externally verifiable trust anchor. Missing it, or "fully licensed" with no number, is a weak page.
- **Safety / pet-safe claims are conversion-critical AND a compliance trap.** "Pet-safe," "eco-friendly," "kid-safe," "non-toxic" are the top objection-killers on a pest page. They are also FTC-substantiation and (for pesticide claims) EPA/FIFRA-adjacent. Only publish safety claims the operator can back; "reduced-risk products, kept away from children and pets until dry" is defensible, "100% non-toxic" usually is not.
- **The business model is recurring.** The real LTV is the quarterly/annual plan, not the one-time treatment. The page converts to a plan (initial + quarterly), so the offer, guarantee ("free re-service between visits"), and no-contract framing are load-bearing.
- **Service-area reality.** Most pest operators are SABs (drive-to, no walk-in office) or hub-and-spoke branch networks. Honest coverage statements matter; a rented address to look local is a GBP-suspension and doorway trigger.

## 1. City / Location page (`/write-location-page`)

Target: "[pest control] [city]" broad, whole-business-in-one-town.

### GOOD

**G1 - Barefoot Mosquito & Pest Control, Houston** (`https://www.barefootmosquito.com/pest-control-houston-tx/`, verified live 2026).
Why it wins: names real Houston neighborhoods and suburbs it serves (The Woodlands, Sugar Land, Pearland, League City, Cypress, Spring, Katy, Missouri City, The Heights, Montrose, Meyerland, Bellaire); carries genuinely local pest mechanisms that collapse if you swap the city ("gumbo clay soils," "average humidity above 75% year-round," Eastern and Formosan subterranean termites "documented across Harris, Fort Bend, and Montgomery counties," swarm season "March through June, with Formosans often swarming later near outdoor lighting," roof rats in "tree-canopy neighborhoods"); prints the applicator license (#557713); states 30 years operating (since 1996); pet-safe positioning done defensibly ("natural essential oils and plant-based ingredients to reduce the reliance on synthetic chemicals"); no-long-term-contracts + a real between-service booster guarantee. Passes strip-the-city and Minimum Unique Local Substance. **Gate: doorway/uniqueness gate + local-proof block (playbook 4.4/5.3).**

**G2 - Barefoot Mosquito, Katy TX** (`https://www.barefootmosquito.com/pest-control-katy-tx/`, verified live 2026).
Why it wins: proof the network is un-spun, not a clone of the Houston page. Names Katy-specific subdivisions (Cinco Ranch, Cross Creek Ranch, Firethorne, Cane Island, Elyson, WoodCreek Reserve) and zip codes (77449, 77450, 77493, 77494); ties rodent pressure to a real local cause ("open fields and rice farms west of Katy support large outdoor rodent populations" + "new construction displacement" from west-Houston development); ties termites to "slab-on-grade construction" cracks and "decorative mulch placed against foundation walls, common in Cinco Ranch, Cane Island, and Elyson"; Katy swarm window ("March and peaks through May"). The rodent and termite deep-dives are town-true; only the value-prop scaffolding is shared across markets, which is the correct 50/50 split. **Gate: strip-the-city test + per-page floor (5.4).**

### BAD

**B1 - the pest "areas we service" page that names no area** (live pattern, `https://availablepestcontrol.net/areas-we-service/`, verified 2026 as thin). The H1 promises "Areas We Service" and delivers boilerplate: a generic pest list ("roaches, ants, spiders, rats, mice and many more"), one Jacksonville address, and standard one-time/monthly/quarterly copy, with **no neighborhoods, no local pest pressure, no zip coverage**. It reads identically for any town in the country. Fails the strip-the-city test on sight. **Gate: doorway/thin-content (spam policy, playbook 5.3).**

**B2 - the "[City] and surrounding areas" template clone** (labeled real pattern, the dominant pest-SEO doorway shape). One template, city token swapped across 30-80 suburb pages, body reads "Looking for reliable pest control in [City]? Our licensed technicians handle ants, roaches, termites, and rodents in [City] and surrounding areas." No named neighborhood, no local species, no swarm window, no license number, no booked-job proof. This is the exact "substantially similar pages" doorway example in Google's live spam policy; post-March-2024 these cohorts show up in GSC as "Crawled - currently not indexed" spreading across the set. **Gate: scaled-content-abuse + doorway (developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies).**

## 2. Service page (`/write-service-page`, single service, brand-wide)

Target: "[service]" (termite control, mosquito control, bed bug treatment) across the whole business.

### GOOD

**G1 - Trio Pest Control, bed bug heat treatment** (`https://triopestcontrol.com/bed-bug-heat-treatment/`, verified live 2026).
Why it wins: the service is described with real operational specifics only a practitioner has, not adjectives. Concrete thermal science ("temperatures 120F or warmer kills bed bugs and their eggs in less than a minute," treatment held "a minimum of 2 hours and, ideally, 3-4 hours"); a **named** detection asset ("a K-9 inspection performed by Geiger and his human Jim is about 95% accurate"); a real risk-reversal ("free K9 follow-up clearance inspection 10-14 days after the heat service") and a 90-day guarantee; a defensible safety claim ("eco-friendly and non-toxic to humans") paired with an honest DIY-danger warning. Experience is shown, not claimed. **Gate: E-E-A-T Experience + extractable-answer passages (playbook 4.3/4.4).**

**G2 - EnviroCon, termite treatment (Houston)** (`https://www.enviroconpest.com/termite-treatment/`, live; content corroborated by multiple live Houston termite pages this session). The termite service page done to pest reality: frames the service around the local pressure mechanism ("Gulf Coast's year-round humidity, heavy seasonal rainfall, and the region's notorious gumbo clay soils" creating high subterranean and Formosan pressure), explains why the method is location-driven ("you cannot simply pour liquid on the ground... high-pressure injection rods push chemicals 2-4 feet deep into the clay"), and names the species and product class (Eastern and Formosan subterranean termites, Termidor). This is the model: a brand-wide service page can still be locally grounded when the whole business sits in one pressure zone. **Gate: specificity-over-boilerplate (5.3), safety/method claims substantiated.** (Note: page temporarily unreachable on one fetch this session; content verified via live search snippet and matching live competitors. Re-verify at build.)

### BAD

**B1 - the "comprehensive pest solutions" service page** (labeled real pattern, extremely common). "We offer comprehensive termite solutions using state-of-the-art technology and industry-leading products to protect your home and family. Our trained professionals deliver quality results you can trust." Names no species, no product, no method, no local pressure, no guarantee terms, no license. Zero extractable answer for an AI engine, zero differentiation. Not penalized on its own, but it ranks for nothing and converts nothing. **Gate: thin-value / content-depth (helpful-content, playbook 4.3).**

**B2 - the unsubstantiated safety-claim service page** (labeled real pattern, and a compliance hazard specific to this vertical). "100% safe, completely non-toxic, all-natural pest control, guaranteed harmless to children and pets." Pesticide-efficacy and safety claims like these are FTC-substantiation exposure and can conflict with EPA/FIFRA label language (no registered pesticide is "100% non-toxic"). It is both a trust risk (a rater reads absolute claims as manufactured) and a legal risk. Replace with the defensible version: "reduced-risk products applied by licensed technicians, kept away from kids and pets until dry." **Gate: FTC substantiation + YMYL trust (compliance spine); Law 8 (win on real facts, not claims).**

## 3. Service-in-city combo page (`/write-service-city-page`, the money page)

Target: "[service] in [city]" - one service x one town. Highest intent, highest doorway risk.

### GOOD

**G1 - No Contract Pest Solutions, Termite Control in Houston** (`https://nocopest.com/services/houston/termite-control/`, verified live 2026).
Why it wins: the textbook compliant combo page. Clean city-first URL (`/services/houston/termite-control/`); names Houston neighborhoods (Heights, Montrose, Bellaire, West University, Memorial, River Oaks); a Formosan mechanism that fails-to-generalize ("colonies of millions vs. hundreds of thousands for native species," "swarm at dusk in May-June"); local construction reality ("pier-and-beam, common in older Houston, vs. slab, post-1970 construction") and "neighborhoods near bayous, wooded areas"; the applicator license as a lookup-able string (**TDA TPCL #0912977**); rating with count (4.9, 200+ reviews); named methods (Sentricon bait + Termidor/Taurus SC); and a recurring-revenue path ("annual inspection and bait replenishment, $395/year renewal") plus a "1-year warranty with free retreatment." Meets all five items of the Minimum Unique Local Substance bar. **Gate: full doorway/uniqueness gate + 5.4 floor.**

**G2 - Barefoot Mosquito, Katy TX (as service-in-city for rodents/termites)** (`https://www.barefootmosquito.com/pest-control-katy-tx/`, verified live 2026). Doubles as the combo model: the "rice farms west of Katy," slab-crack, and named-subdivision content is exactly the un-templated local-proof core (playbook 4.4) that separates a converting combo page from a doorway clone. Its per-service local mechanisms (rodent displacement from new construction, mulch-against-foundation termite risk) are the kind of causally-tied local condition item 2 of the floor requires. **Gate: strip-the-city test (5.3); external-verifiability via named zips and county.**

### BAD

**B1 - the AI-spun combo network** (labeled real pattern, the 2026 version this system exists to stop). Generative AI produces "[Service] in [City]" pages for every service x every suburb (a 6-service, 25-town operator = 150 pages) in one pass. Each reads convincingly specific ("Rockwall homeowners face unique termite challenges due to our local climate") but names no real neighborhood, no lookup-able license, no booked job, and ties to no real service area. It can pass a naive read yet fails the **external-verifiability** gate: nothing resolves to a record outside the business's control. It is the literal "using generative AI tools to generate many pages without adding value" example in the live scaled-content-abuse policy, which now also governs AI Overviews (May 15, 2026 update). **Gate: external-verifiability (5.3) + scaled-content-abuse.**

**B2 - the keyword-padded combo FAQ** (labeled real pattern; RicketyRoo names this exact tell). "What is the best termite control in [City]? We are the best termite control company in [City]. Where can I find termite control near me in [City]? Our [City] termite control is the top-rated termite control in [City]." Says nothing to a human, exists only to repeat "[service] [city]," and is the spam signal a rater is trained to catch. **Gate: doorway keyword-stuffing (playbook 4.10 anti-pattern; ricketyroo.com/blog/location-page-spam/).**

## 4. Homepage (`/write-homepage`, entity anchor + primary conversion)

### GOOD

**G1 - Barefoot Mosquito & Pest Control** (`https://www.barefootmosquito.com/`, verified live 2026).
Why it wins as an entity anchor: clear brand promise H1 ("Breathe Easy. Walk Barefoot. Enjoy Your Home Again."); the full service entity graph (residential pest, mosquito, termite, rodents, scorpions, lawn, commercial, HOA); explicit two-state footprint (Texas and Oklahoma; Austin, Dallas, Houston, San Antonio, OKC, Marble Falls/Kingsland) that feeds the service-area hub; a differentiated method as the positioning wedge ("the Barefoot Method... significantly fewer chemicals than traditional pest control"); trust stack (licensed and insured, license #557713, nearly 30 years, physical offices); a recurring-plan offer with real numbers ("$99 initial mosquito service, 50% off," "$200 off termite prevention," free termite inspection); and a "guaranteed for 12 months in most cases" risk-reversal. The homepage converts AND establishes the entity for the pack/AI surfaces. **Gate: entity clarity + conversion (playbook homepage; NAP/GBP integrity).**

**G2 - Massey Services** (`https://www.masseyservices.com/`, verified live 2026). The scaled-operator model done right: homepage carries the full guaranteed-service entity (Pest Prevention, Termite Protection, Mosquito, Landscape/Irrigation), a real scale-and-tenure signal ("founded in 1985," "nearly 1,000,000 customers" across 10 states), a guarantee-forward promise, and industry-authority trust (EPA environmental-stewardship awards, leadership on NPMA/FPMA/Entomological Society boards). Authority caveat: Massey ranks partly on national brand and entity strength; a single-location operator inherits none of that and must earn it through GBP, reviews, and links. Structure is necessary, not sufficient. **Gate: E-E-A-T authority + entity graph.**

### BAD

**B1 - the "welcome to our website" homepage** (labeled real pattern, endemic among small operators). H1 = the company name or "Welcome"; a stock exterminator-in-a-hazmat-suit hero; "We provide quality pest control services at affordable prices. Contact us today!"; no services list crawlable above the fold, no service area named, no license, no reviews, no guarantee. Establishes no entity for the pack and gives an AI engine nothing to cite. **Gate: entity clarity + content-depth (helpful-content).**

**B2 - the homepage stuffed with every city + service** (labeled real pattern, and a doorway-adjacent hazard). To rank everywhere at once, the homepage foot-stuffs "Termite control in Dallas, Plano, Frisco, McKinney, Allen, Fort Worth, Arlington... mosquito control in Dallas, Plano, Frisco..." for 40 towns x 8 services. It reads as machine text, dilutes the entity, and is the "content makes little sense to a reader but contains search keywords" scaled-content signal. The correct move is a real service-area hub linking to substantive individual pages. **Gate: scaled-content-abuse / keyword-stuffing.**

## 5. About / team page (`/write-about-page`, the E-E-A-T surface)

### GOOD

**G1 - McDuffie Pest Control, Our Story** (`https://mcduffiepest.com/about/our-story`, verified live 2026).
Why it wins: real, checkable, first-hand Experience, not "family-owned since [year]" filler. Names the founder and his actual credential ("Walter McDuffie" holds "a degree in entomology from North Carolina State University," "founded McDuffie Pest Control in 1964 in Elizabethtown, North Carolina"); names the second-generation certified expert ("Walter's daughter Dana... an Associate Certified Entomologist"); an un-fakeable tenure detail ("that first part-time employee? Still on our team today, more than 50 years later"); technicians framed as locals ("who know the pest pressures of this region because they live here too"); named service region and named certifications (NC/SC licenses, Certified Sentricon Specialist, NPMA/NCPMA). This is E-E-A-T shown with specifics only this business has. **Gate: E-E-A-T (Experience + named credentialed human), YMYL-adjacent trust.**

**G2 - ABC Home & Commercial Services** (about pages, e.g. `https://www.abchomeandcommercial.com/austin/pest-control`, verified via live listing 2026). The named-expert model: "locally owned and operated since 1949," third-generation owner named (Bobby Jenkins), and a **Board Certified Entomologist** named with tenure (Alan Brown, 27 years). A Board Certified Entomologist (the highest ACE/BCE credential) on the about page is the pest-control equivalent of a named master plumber or attorney of record: the machine-readable "who is responsible" anchor an AI engine and a rater both reward. **Gate: E-E-A-T authoritativeness + author/entity accountability.**

### BAD

**B1 - the "trusted since [year], quality you can trust" about page** (labeled real pattern). "Founded years ago, our family-owned company has proudly served the community with quality pest control. Our friendly, professional technicians are dedicated to customer satisfaction." No founder name, no credential, no entomologist, no license, no specific story, no named region. It claims trust without a single verifiable specific, which is exactly the empty "family-owned since 1998" the playbooks warn about. **Gate: E-E-A-T Experience-not-claimed (playbook hard rule).**

**B2 - the fabricated-credential about page** (labeled real pattern, and a hard line). Inventing an "in-house entomologist," a fake "50 years combined experience," badge logos for certifications the business does not hold, or a stock-photo "team." Fabricated credentials on a YMYL-adjacent trust page (people let this business spray chemicals in their home) are the fastest path to a trust penalty and a real liability. If the operator does not have the credential, do not print it; surface what is true (years, real license, real training). **Gate: no-fabricated-facts (CLAUDE.md hard rule) + FTC.**

### GOOD

**G1 - Barefoot Mosquito, Service Areas hub** (`https://www.barefootmosquito.com/service-areas/`, verified live 2026).
Why it wins: a real coverage hub, not a thin link farm. Lists the primary metros (Austin, Dallas, Houston, San Antonio, Oklahoma City, Marble Falls) and honestly frames the edges ("our service area includes, but is not limited to," then "Round Rock, Cedar Park, and Pflugerville," "Edmond to Norman"); carries regional pest intelligence per market ("humidity-loving insects of Houston," Formosan termites, "Striped Bark Scorpions"); shows real physical office locations with maps; and links down to substantive individual city pages (e.g. `/pest-control-austin-tx/`). Transparent coverage + real local content + crawlable links to deeper pages = the correct hub-and-spoke shape. **Gate: coverage transparency + no-doorway (playbook service-area; 5.2 useful-not-count).**

**G2 - Massey Services state/metro coverage** (`https://www.masseyservices.com/fl/pest-control/` and metro pages like `/orlando/`, verified via live listing 2026). The branch-network model: a state hub that resolves to real staffed service centers, each metro page tied to a genuine local operation rather than a rented address. Because each node is a real branch that books jobs in that market, the network is destination pages (usefulness test), not intermediate doorways. **Gate: SAB/storefront honesty + doorway intermediate-vs-destination.**

### BAD

**B1 - the "areas we service" page that lists no areas** (live, `https://availablepestcontrol.net/areas-we-service/`, verified thin 2026). Already dissected in 1.B1. As a service-area page specifically it is the canonical failure: the page's entire job is to communicate coverage, and it names zero cities, zero neighborhoods, zero local pressure. Pure boilerplate under a geo headline. **Gate: thin-content / doorway.**

**B2 - the subdomain-or-massive-list city grid funneling to one form** (labeled real pattern, matches Google's named doorway example almost verbatim). Either `dallas.pestco.com`, `plano.pestco.com`... as thin clones, or one service-area page listing 200 city links each opening a near-identical "pest control in [City]" clone that funnels every visitor to the same central quote form. Google's doorway policy names this exactly: "multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page." Fix: flat subfolders on one domain, and only build a city page for a town where the local-proof block can be written truthfully (5.2). **Gate: doorway network structure (spam policy).**

## Cross-cutting build rules for the pest vertical

1. **Lead every local page with the pressure mechanism, not the city name.** "Gulf Coast humidity and gumbo clay drive extreme Formosan termite pressure in [named counties]" beats "Looking for termite control in [City]?" every time, and it passes the strip-the-city test automatically.
2. **Print the real applicator license number** (TDA TPCL in Texas; the state equivalent elsewhere). It is public, lookup-able, and the single strongest externally-verifiable trust item on a pest page.
3. **Name the species and the season.** Eastern vs Formosan subterranean, the local swarm window, roof rats vs Norway rats vs house mice, the local mosquito active-months range. Species + season is local proof a competitor's template does not have.
4. **Convert to the plan, not the visit.** Initial + quarterly, "free re-service between visits," no-long-term-contract framing. The guarantee ("we come back at no cost") is the highest-leverage conversion mechanic here.
5. **Keep safety claims defensible.** "Reduced-risk products, licensed technicians, kept away from kids and pets until dry" ships; "100% non-toxic / all-natural / completely safe" is FTC/EPA exposure. Win on real method detail, not absolute claims (Law 8).
6. **Build pages for real service areas the crew books jobs in, never for a keyword list.** If the local-proof block cannot be written truthfully for a town (call the techs, pull the county pest reality), the page waits. That single rule is what separates the Barefoot/nocopest network from every penalized suburb-swap network.
